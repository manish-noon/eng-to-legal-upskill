# Episode 12: Financing structures

**Leo:** The contract calls itself a finance lease. All rentals have been paid. I tell my treasury team to stop the outgoing wire and I tell fleet planning we own the aircraft tomorrow.

**Mira:** And you might just breach your lease and fail to acquire the asset. Welcome to episode twelve, Financing structures. I'm Mira.

**Leo:** I'm Leo. Mira, the document literally says 'Finance Lease' at the top. The final regular rental left our account yesterday. Why doesn't title pass automatically?

**Mira:** Because the commercial label alone establishes nothing about the title step. Accounting, tax, insolvency, and property law can each classify the exact same documents under their own tests. A presentation describing an aircraft as owned, financed, or leased might just be using one of those specific lenses.

**Leo:** So if the final payment doesn't by itself identify whether an option must be exercised or a security release obtained, what is the controlling clause?

**Mira:** You need the transfer or option provision, and you need to clear all outstanding release conditions. The revealing question is what happens after that last regular rental. Does a party have a right to buy? Is a final large payment mandatory? Or must the aircraft be returned in a specified condition?

**Leo:** Right, a purchase option can require timely notice, payment of a fixed price, and the discharge of every other outstanding obligation. If treasury misses the notice window, the right to buy could vanish.

**Mira:** Exactly. Title may sit with an owner trustee, who holds it under a trust arrangement and needs instructions. You must identify who executes the bill of sale and who releases the lender's security. In a filed finance lease, the parties certify that the lessor took legal title by bill of sale.

**Leo:** And they also certify that failure to exercise the purchase option obliges the lessee to pay a residual value or termination sum at least as large as the option price. I've seen that in a Finance Lease Certification.

**Mira:** Yes. Read section twenty-one C of an aircraft lease before describing non-exercise as a way to save the exercise price. Then ask what property or use the airline receives on each path.

**Leo:** Let's contrast that with an operating lease. A finance lease can give an airline possession while arranging for its payments to repay the capital advanced to acquire the aircraft, plus the cost of funding. The economic bargain puts much of the value risk on the airline through rent, a terminal purchase obligation, or a stipulated termination payment.

**Mira:** Whereas an operating lease expects the aircraft back for another placement. Its owner is exposed to what the next operator will pay. Therefore, that owner cares deeply about the condition, records, and remaining life delivered at return.

**Leo:** If the airline's accounts show an aircraft-related asset, does that give us a shortcut to knowing who signs the bill of sale?

**Mira:** No. Accounting presentation alone cannot establish title or signing authority. You must find the legal owner and authorised signatory in the transaction papers.

**Leo:** Okay, let's rebuild one financing from the ground up so we can trace who finances the aircraft and who absorbs a shortfall. Let's draw an illustrative owner company with a single aircraft.

**Mira:** Alright. We need to split the funding into equity and senior debt. Equity means the owner's invested capital at risk after senior creditors are paid. Senior debt is borrowing with the agreed first claim on the specified repayment sources, ahead of equity.

**Leo:** Let's use some numbers. On delivery, the bank advances eighteen million dollars. The investors contribute six million dollars to the owner. The owner then pays the manufacturer twenty-four million dollars and leases the aircraft to the operator.

**Mira:** During the term, the airline pays rent to a designated account. A payment waterfall specifies the order in which money in that account pays expenses, debt service, and amounts available to the investors.

**Leo:** So we identify the source of each delivery dollar, and the account that receives rent after delivery. The security package might include a mortgage over the aircraft, an assignment of rentals, and rights under insurance.

**Mira:** Focus on that assignment. An assignment redirects rights to another person. You must read the notice delivered to the airline and any acknowledgement it signs. If a lender takes an assignment of rent, what should the airline seek in the written directions?

**Leo:** The commercial instruction is to get confirmation that payment to the designated account discharges its rent obligation. The direction changes the collection path, so discharge language protects the airline against paying the same instalment twice.

**Mira:** Exactly. Identify whether payment to the specified account discharges the airline, and importantly, who has the authority to change those account details later. Also, a bank taking security over lease rights may reserve approval over lease amendments or early termination.

**Leo:** That's a bottleneck. A bargain made with the owner's commercial team can still stall if the bank's consent is a condition. Let's say the owner proposes a lease amendment, but its lender must approve changes to payment rights. What belongs in the instruction to sign?

**Mira:** You condition effectiveness on the required written consent. An amendment can be commercially agreed, while the consent needed for its effectiveness remains missing. Never rely on the commercial team's approval alone, and do not treat a rate quote as consent.

**Leo:** Let's look at a non-recourse loan. A non-recourse loan to an owner vehicle can limit a lender's rights against the owner's shareholders to agreed assets, cash flows, and exceptions. Does it follow that the airline has no payment obligations?

**Mira:** Absolutely not. The airline's obligations still have to be read from the lease and any separate support it gives. A limitation on recourse under the owner's borrowing does not define the airline's independent promises.

**Leo:** Right, the lender may rely heavily on the lease precisely because the owner's other resources are limited. If a counterparty claims another entity will stand behind it, you ask for the relevant guarantee or indemnity. The existence of powerful investors in the ownership chain gives the airline no automatic claim on their balance sheets.

**Mira:** Let's do another quick calculation to lock down the equity concept. An aircraft costs thirty-six million dollars. The owner receives twenty-seven million dollars of debt at delivery. Assume no fees, other sources, or unpaid price. How many dollars of equity are required?

**Leo:** Price minus debt. That leaves nine million dollars of equity required.

**Mira:** Correct. Now, let's track a deposit after a sale. In a hypothetical financing, the airline lodged a cash deposit with the original owner. A buyer sends new rent instructions. The lease permits a sale if the buyer takes on the owner's duties and meets a financial test, either itself or through a guarantor.

**Leo:** So we follow the cash in both directions. The payment notice identifies who collects next month's rent. The signed transfer agreement should identify who must refund the deposit at expiry and from what date.

**Mira:** And suppose the buyer shows you its parent company's accounts to prove it meets the financial test. What do you do?

**Leo:** I reject it as incomplete. Those accounts only tell you about the parent. If the buyer relies on a guarantee to meet the test, I need to ask for the signed guarantee, check that it covers the refund, and establish when it takes effect.

**Mira:** Spot on. Before acknowledging release of the seller, you must reconcile the deposit balance with both parties. An unpaid refund can leave the airline exposed even after every rental has reached the correct account.

**Leo:** Let's move to pricing the end and the early exit. We need to define three things clearly. A balloon is a substantial final payment left after regular instalments. An option strike is the price payable if a purchase option is properly exercised. And a stipulated loss value schedule sets the amount due following specified events, which may decline over time.

**Mira:** Keep those three columns separate. A balloon can be obligatory, while an option strike depends on election. And crucially, a value in the casualty schedule need not be the airline's voluntary prepayment price.

**Leo:** This happens all the time. The loss-value schedule is published, but the airline wants to return a perfectly serviceable aircraft early. They look at the schedule and think that's their buyout price. What should they do first?

**Mira:** They need to find a voluntary termination right and its pricing method. A casualty formula assigns consequences to a different trigger. It grants no right to surrender early by itself. A request for early exit is also a request to identify whether the airline has any contractual exit right at all.

**Leo:** If they do have the right, early settlement can include outstanding principal, accrued return, break costs, and fees. Break costs are defined losses from ending a funding or hedging arrangement early. My instruction to the team is always: require a calculation statement and evidence for each input.

**Mira:** Yes, ask whether gains from unwinding the same position are credited, which discount rate applies, whether notice is irrevocable before the price is fixed, and whether the airline can verify quotations.

**Leo:** Because a fixed rental can still sit alongside a volatile early exit price. A route network that may contract before lease expiry puts extra value on a predictable exit.

**Mira:** Let's look at how a fleet planner compares structures. In a teaching comparison, a fleet planner discounts all future payments to the same date and currency. Under a purchase path, the present value of future rent is twelve million dollars, the option strike is five million dollars on the same present-value basis, and the aircraft retained at exit is worth four million dollars in today's money.

**Leo:** So the simplified net acquisition-and-use cost is thirteen million dollars. Twelve plus five is seventeen, minus the four million retained value.

**Mira:** Correct. Now compare that to a return path. You use its own rent and return expenses, with no residual credit to the airline. And if ownership is mandatory, describe that as an obligation even where the arithmetic resembles an option exercise.

**Leo:** Let's do another one to lock down the valuation date concept. All numbers share one valuation date. Future rentals are worth eight point one million dollars, mandatory final payment is three point two million dollars, and the aircraft retained at exit is two point four million dollars. What net cost in dollars follows, ignoring other items?

**Mira:** Eight point one million plus three point two million, less two point four million, gives eight point nine million dollars.

**Leo:** Right. But if a price comparison deducts the future value of the aircraft from both the buy path and the return path, what would you change?

**Mira:** I would credit value only to the party entitled to keep or sell the asset at the end of each path. In the return path, the airline does not keep the aircraft; it may have a condition settlement instead.

**Leo:** Let's pivot to export credit support. An export credit agency, or ECA, supports eligible exports under its programme. The US Export-Import Bank describes direct loans, guarantees, and insurance among its aircraft export tools.

**Mira:** For a guaranteed loan, you must distinguish the bank advancing cash, the party borrowing, the agency assuming specified credit exposure to the lender, and the operator undertaking obligations under its lease. The agency's support can improve the availability of funding.

**Leo:** But it does not insure the airline against weak passenger demand or excuse rent when a route closes. If a lender has an ECA guarantee, and the airline's route becomes uneconomic, which obligation does that guarantee ordinarily address in this structure?

**Mira:** It addresses the covered credit risk owed to the lender under the instrument. The protected party is the lender. An airline needs a separate contractual basis for relief.

**Leo:** EXIM identifies 'a first priority mortgage on the financed aircraft' as one possible element of the aircraft-backed security supporting its credit decision. When I'm looking at a term sheet, I list the actual requirements on eligibility, documentary evidence, aircraft security, reporting, and disbursement.

**Mira:** Because a commitment subject to unmet conditions is weaker than money ready for delivery. If the manufacturer can tender before financing conditions clear, draw the gap between the delivery obligation and earliest drawdown.

**Leo:** That gap is where deals die. You have to agree who provides bridge funds, what happens to deposits, and whether delivery can be deferred under the purchase agreement. I always ask treasury to demonstrate one failed drawdown using the proposed timetable.

**Mira:** Assume the owner has approval subject to evidence of a clean security position, but a release document is still in transit. Who has the ability to postpone acceptance? Which party can extend the financing commitment? And does interest accrue on emergency funding?

**Leo:** The consequences turn on the connections between instruments. A lender's commercial assurance alone cannot move the manufacturer's delivery deadline.

**Mira:** You also need to ask for the exact support instrument, its beneficiary, and its cost. A premium charged upfront should enter the all-in cost at its actual payment date. If the premium is financed, include the resulting financing charges.

**Leo:** And keep different countries' programmes separate. Avoid treating a published description as an approval for this specific buyer. Put the expiry of a conditional offer, the delivery window, and any change in export content side by side.

**Mira:** Let's read both branches of a JOLCO. That's a Japanese operating lease with call option. JP Lease Products and Services describes JOL and JOLCO structures. It combines Japanese investor equity with debt and gives the lessee a purchase option under specified conditions.

**Leo:** The structure can include several companies, a lease or sublease, and a secured lender. I need to find the party holding legal title, the party holding each option, and the parties bearing residual value if the option expires unused.

**Mira:** The option has value because the lessee can compare the exercise economics with the alternative. Read the alternative before telling the airline it has a free exit. You need to build a calendar with the notice deadline, exercise date, strike, transfer costs, and any conditions.

**Leo:** One SEC-filed financing description sets the JOLCO purchase right on a fixed date before lease expiry. That detail matters immensely. If the lessee misses the date, the remaining lease obligations and return exposure may persist.

**Mira:** So you must work through both branches from the same decision date. If a JOLCO notice date is earlier than final expiry, what makes the comparison economically complete?

**Leo:** It's the exercise cash flows and aircraft value versus all remaining rent, return costs, and alternatives on non-exercise. Option value depends on the alternative path from the same decision date. The original manufacturer price alone does not quantify that alternative.

**Mira:** And remember to obtain legal and tax advice on the particular investors, transactions, and jurisdictions before discounting the lease for an assumed tax benefit. A tax benefit for an investor does not automatically belong to the airline.

**Leo:** Here is a board-level scenario. A new route makes the aircraft unattractive to return, but financing the strike would exhaust a treasury limit. How should the board see the choice?

**Mira:** You show both the economic comparison and the dated peak cash requirement. Positive value cannot fund the exercise price. If treasury doesn't have the cash on the day, the option is useless.

**Leo:** Let's summarize the JOLCO branches. For the exercise notice, the airline wants late flexibility, while funding parties want time to release security. The illustrative landing is an agreed notice period and documents in escrow.

**Mira:** For break costs, the airline wants a fixed ceiling, but funding parties need to preserve actual funding costs because early exit can unwind funding. The landing is documented costs with gain credits.

**Leo:** And for non-exercise, the airline wants a clear return path, while the owner wants to protect condition and residual value because they may place it elsewhere. The landing is a priced return specification and inspection dates.

**Mira:** At expiry, which party must find the cash, which can choose, and who has the aircraft if nobody exercises? That's the ultimate test of any financing structure.

**Leo:** Let's bring this in for a landing. Three takeaways. One: The commercial label 'finance lease' does not transfer title. You must read the transfer provision, the option strike, and clear all release conditions to actually own the asset.

**Mira:** Two: A non-recourse loan to an owner vehicle limits the lender's rights against the owner's shareholders, but it does not erase the airline's independent payment obligations under the lease. Read the lease and any separate guarantees.

**Leo:** Three: When modelling an early exit or a JOLCO option, compare the exercise cash flows against all remaining rent and return costs from the exact same decision date, and ensure treasury can actually fund the dated peak cash requirement.
