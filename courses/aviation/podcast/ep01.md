# Episode 1: Who owns the aircraft?

**Leo:** Your buyer’s loan requires a maintenance report before they fund. But your purchase agreement requires unconditional payment tomorrow morning. The report is delayed. Where does the immediate problem sit?

**Mira:** The buyer may owe the purchase price before its lender is obliged to advance the funds. Separate contracts create separate clocks.

**Leo:** Exactly. A financing condition, alternative cash, or an agreed extension would address that mismatch, but right now, you are on the hook. Welcome to episode one of Clearing the Aircraft Deal. I am Leo, and we are asking: Who owns the aircraft?

**Mira:** And I’m Mira. The bank supplies the cash, the airline takes delivery, and another company holds title. Whose signature does the buyer need?

**Leo:** An airline paints its name on a new aircraft. A bank supplied most of the money. Another company bought the aircraft. We need to trace ownership, use, payment, and security to the right people and the right documents.

**Mira:** Let us start with one aircraft and four questions. Open the filed aircraft-specific lease alongside this conversation. In the recitals, you will find the earlier agreement this one incorporates.

**Leo:** So I start with the signed lease, but the answer might be in a separate agreement. How do I know I have the right version?

**Mira:** Precisely. Under section one point one, read the definition of Common Terms Agreement all the way to the closing parenthesis. It identifies a dated version and a letter amendment, and it qualifies when later changes count.

**Leo:** Write those on your file list. If you see section seven, it incorporates schedules as well. What happens if those common terms are missing from the data room?

**Mira:** If the common terms are missing, you mark each question that depends on them as open. And no, an identically titled form from a different airline will not fill the gap. The specific dated version matters.

**Leo:** Let us take one question through that file. When does the first rent instalment fall due?

**Mira:** You start by reading section three point two. That sends you to the definition of Rent Commencement Date. Return to that definition, then note the tender procedure it imports.

**Leo:** Meanwhile, section two point two separately starts the term on delivery, and it calls for two acceptance documents.

**Mira:** Which means you draw one line for payment, and another separate line for delivery. Even when both events happen the same morning, separate lines tell your team which evidence to obtain if tender is contested.

**Leo:** That read, follow, return sequence is crucial whenever a capitalised term carries the answer into another document. Do not guess. Follow the pointer.

**Mira:** Let us look at the fundamental exchange. A seller promises to deliver an aircraft. A buyer promises to pay for it. It sounds like a single exchange until the airline asks its bank to fund the delivery.

**Leo:** Because the bank wants rights over the aircraft if its borrower fails to repay. The airline just wants to put the aircraft into service. And a financing company may want to buy it and rent it to the airline.

**Mira:** Before you read the long agreement, write down four things. Who should own the aircraft? Who should control its use? Who should receive the cash? And who can claim against what if a payment fails?

**Leo:** Each of those answers needs a document. Think of the four seats in a financed lease. The original equipment manufacturer, or OEM, builds the aircraft. They pass title to an owner.

**Mira:** The owner, who acts as the lessor, holds title. They grant possession to an airline, the lessee, and they give security to their lender.

**Leo:** So the manufacturer sells. The lessor holds title. The airline operates. The lender advances cash and holds security. The airline pays rent to the owner or its designated collection account.

**Mira:** That is one possible arrangement. But you have to follow title, possession, and security separately. On a cash purchase, the owner and the operator could be the exact same company.

**Leo:** Let us test that with a sale and leaseback. An airline can buy the aircraft, immediately sell it to a financing owner, and lease it back. They might continue to operate exactly the same routes.

**Mira:** But two transfers of ownership and a lease still need to close in the right order. You have to ask which transfer the words Delivery Date refer to in the document in your hand. Identical defined words in two contracts can refer to different instants.

**Leo:** Here is a scenario for you. A carrier sells an aircraft and immediately leases it back. Which fact alone tells you the least about who now owns it?

**Mira:** Give me the options.

**Leo:** Option one: the seller signed and released a bill of sale naming the purchaser. Option two: the purchaser paid the sale price and closing conditions were satisfied. Option three: the carrier continues using its own crews on the same routes.

**Mira:** The third option. The carrier continuing to use its own crews on the same routes tells you the least about ownership. Flight operations can continue seamlessly through a change of title. The other facts actually concern the transfer under the sale.

**Leo:** Exactly. Never look at the physical operation to prove legal title. What happens if that onward lease starts a day before the sale actually closes?

**Mira:** You have a gap risk. You must identify who can give possession, when the rent starts, and whose policy covers that intervening day. You either align the triggers, or you agree an express arrangement for the gap.

**Leo:** Let us break down the terminology around the asset and the promise. We throw words around, but they have strict boundaries.

**Mira:** Title means ownership. Possession means physical control. Risk of loss identifies the party that bears specified damage or destruction at a particular time.

**Leo:** And a security interest? That gives a creditor rights in an asset to support payment of a debt. The debt is the promise to repay money. The security is the agreed support for that promise.

**Mira:** You must compare the security document, the evidence of registration where relevant, and the loan terms. Recording a debt amount next to the aircraft's price tells you very little about the order in which competing claims will be paid.

**Leo:** When you list the asset itself, where do you start?

**Mira:** Start your asset list with the manufacturer's serial number for the airframe. For this exercise, that is the aircraft structure excluding its engines. Then list each engine by its own serial number, the installed equipment, spare parts if included, and the technical records.

**Leo:** Because the aircraft's registration mark can change. And an engine may have been taken off for maintenance and replaced with a borrowed unit.

**Mira:** Which is why photographing a registration mark will never settle whether an engine on temporary loan belongs in the sale. You have to read the definition of Aircraft, the schedules identifying the property, and each exclusion.

**Leo:** Try this scenario. The seller owns the airframe. One installed engine is a temporary loan, but the draft agreement includes the phrase, all installed engines. What evidence resolves that conflict?

**Mira:** A whole-aircraft photograph attached to the signature page will not do it. The latest route schedule will not do it. You need serial-number schedules, proof of ownership or loan, and an agreed substitution or exclusion.

**Leo:** Right. The draft promises more than the seller may actually own. Appearance and operating history cannot transfer the lender's engine. You need the paperwork to match the metal.

**Mira:** Let us talk about competing claims on title. You will often read the phrase, free and clear of all liens, charges or encumbrances.

**Leo:** A lien is a claim against property that can secure an obligation. Other interests can constrain use or sale as well. But even with that clean title promise, a buyer still needs a condition test for a damaged engine.

**Mira:** Exactly. Ask counsel to check the relevant registers and potential claims that may arise outside those registers. A contractual promise to deliver clean title is one source of recourse. But a document releasing an existing lender's interest does a completely different job.

**Leo:** If that lender is owed money, they may deliver the release only when they get paid. You have to tie the payoff and the release to the closing instructions. Each part of the chain must identify the identical asset.

**Mira:** The seller has signed its promise of clean title. What still has to happen before treasury releases the money?

**Leo:** It is the effective release from the lender against its agreed payoff. The seller's promise might support a later claim if things go wrong, but only the lender controls the release of its own security.

**Mira:** Let us move to reading the contract itself. Read from verbs to consequences. For each important sentence, find the person who must act, what they must do, when they must do it, and what counts as successful performance.

**Leo:** And then find the exception, and the consequence of failure. We need to distinguish between representations, covenants, conditions, and remedies.

**Mira:** A representation is a statement of fact. For example, that the seller owns the listed engines. A covenant is a promise to do or refrain from doing something. Like a promise to maintain the aircraft until closing.

**Leo:** A condition to closing allows a party to withhold performance until that condition is met or waived. And a remedy tells you what a party can obtain or do if something goes wrong.

**Mira:** Those categories coexist. A seller can promise to remove its mortgage, which is a covenant. That release can also be a condition to the buyer paying. But if closing proceeds anyway, can the buyer still claim for breach of the promise?

**Leo:** That depends. You have to look at the waiver, the certificate signed at closing, and any terms saying which promises survive closing.

**Mira:** In your issues list, record the exact phrase that changes the outcome, and who has the power to change or waive it. A lawyer should confirm the effect under the governing law, which is the legal system the parties chose for their contract.

**Leo:** Here is a practical test on that. The buyer can waive a delivery condition that records are complete. But a separate promise to supply them survives closing. What should the closing team check first?

**Mira:** They must check whether the proposed waiver also releases claims under the surviving promise. Permission to close despite a gap, and the release of the underlying duty, have entirely different consequences. You have to read the waiver itself.

**Leo:** Not just whether the word records is printed in capital letters, or if the sale price exceeds the cost of the paper. It is about the specific release of the duty. Let us talk about dependencies.

**Mira:** List each document the clause sends you to. A short definition may send you to a schedule, which is an exhibit that forms part of the contract. That exhibit might incorporate a dated technical manual or a side letter.

**Leo:** A side letter being a separate agreement varying or supplementing the main terms. And an amendment changes the original agreement.

**Mira:** Start with the signed version, check every later amendment, and record the order of precedence for conflicting documents. A missing attachment is a request for evidence. If a filed copy conceals a price or a time limit, mark it unknown.

**Leo:** Because guessing the latest version can change the identity of the party bearing the loss. What if a term sheet shows a guarantee from the airline's parent, but the signature page is blank?

**Mira:** Put it on the issues list immediately. Identify who must execute the guarantee and whether receipt is a condition to funding. Track it as missing until the signed form and authority are verified.

**Leo:** Let us follow the money through these documents. When you are mapping this, draw a separate arrow for every single payment.

**Mira:** Its label should name the payer and beneficiary, the currency, the amount or formula, the date, the conditions, and the evidence of discharge.

**Leo:** In a financed sale, the bank might pay the seller directly on behalf of the buyer. That wire can satisfy part of the purchase price under the sale, and simultaneously create a debt owed by the buyer under the loan.

**Mira:** Do not double count. Adding the loan principal to the price would count the same funding twice. Interest and financing fees are additional costs. Keep funding sources separate from what the aircraft costs to acquire or use.

**Leo:** Let us run some numbers. Suppose the price is sixteen million US dollars. An airline has already paid two million US dollars under an agreement that credits the entire amount at delivery.

**Mira:** Okay, so fourteen million is left.

**Leo:** Right. A bank agrees to advance ten million US dollars to the seller at closing. The closing statement shows fourteen million US dollars still due. It is funded by ten million US dollars from the bank, and four million US dollars from the airline.

**Mira:** The price still totals sixteen million US dollars. But remember our cold open? If the bank requires an inspection report that has yet to arrive, the written commitment may still leave the airline without the money on delivery day.

**Leo:** Always read the conditions to the bank advance alongside the purchase payment deadline. Let us try another one. Price is nineteen million US dollars. A credited three million US dollar advance has cleared. A lender wires eleven million US dollars to the seller at closing.

**Mira:** Ignoring fees, how many millions must the buyer add at closing?

**Leo:** Nineteen less three less eleven leaves five. Five million US dollars. The buyer will separately owe its lender under the loan, but five million is what they need to close the sale.

**Mira:** One more. A special-purpose owner owes nine million US dollars to its lender. The sale price is fourteen million US dollars. Escrow pays that debt out of the price and no other amounts move. How many millions go directly to the owner?

**Leo:** Fourteen less the nine payoff leaves five. Five million US dollars. Paying the lender discharges that portion of the price under the assumed instructions.

**Mira:** You mentioned escrow. An escrow agent holds money or documents and releases them in accordance with agreed instructions. It can reduce the gap between paying and obtaining the documents that evidence title.

**Leo:** Review who may authorise release, how disputes freeze release, and who bears fees and interest. But remember, the agent's task is administrative. You still have to verify that the title chain is sound.

**Mira:** Exactly. An escrow account alone does nothing to cure a mismatch between a serial number in the release and the number on the engine.

**Leo:** Let us talk about decision makers. The logo on a slide deck is a poor guide to legal responsibility. An aircraft owner may be a special-purpose company, a separate entity set up for this transaction.

**Mira:** Its manager may handle emails and receive notices. The wider group's balance sheet helps only to the extent that it has given enforceable support, such as a guarantee under which another company promises payment on defined terms.

**Leo:** Ask for the legal name on the signature page, its capacity, evidence of authority, and any conditions attached to its promise. A decision maker may be commercially influential and still lack authority to release money.

**Mira:** Who can give effective consent when the lender, the aircraft manager, and the owner all attend a call?

**Leo:** You have to read the agreement naming the person entitled to consent, its delegation rules, and the required written form. Treat support expressed in the meeting as an input to obtaining the formal consent. The nod on a Zoom call is not legally binding.

**Mira:** Consider this. An owner has few assets and offers a refund promise. Its parent operates a large fleet. Which evidence changes expected recovery most directly?

**Leo:** Is it a brochure showing the fleet? A signed acknowledgement that owner and parent use the same brand? Or an executed, effective parent guarantee covering that specific refund?

**Mira:** It is the executed, effective parent guarantee. A valid guarantee with appropriate coverage can add a source of recovery. Group branding creates no payment undertaking by itself.

**Leo:** Let us look at how parties change. Assignment means transferring contractual rights. But passing obligations and replacing a party can require a different mechanism and consent.

**Mira:** Sometimes that is documented as a novation, which is an agreed substitution of one party for another on the terms of the substitution.

**Leo:** Suppose an airline orders a production slot and later wants a lessor to fund the purchase. You have to check whether the OEM permits a transfer of the slot, whether the original buyer remains liable, and whether training credits or warranty rights travel with the aircraft.

**Mira:** A consent for giving security to a lender may be narrower than consent for an outright sale of the purchase position. If a manufacturer allows a security assignment to a named bank, and the buyer now wants a new lessor to take over the entire order, what should counsel investigate?

**Leo:** They need to investigate whether substitution requires separate consent, and whether the original buyer will be released. A narrow permission to grant security need not permit replacement of the party responsible for performance.

**Mira:** Exactly. When dealing with a change of buyer, the buyer wants consent in advance for a financing company to ensure funding flexibility. The seller wants to control who receives a scarce slot.

**Leo:** A possible landing there is permitting a named funding vehicle after evidence of funds and agreed continuing support. The person preparing the first draft has the pen and controls its initial wording and exceptions.

**Mira:** A seller with another creditworthy buyer ready for the same slot can resist changes. A buyer able to fund immediately and take a difficult delivery date can ask for concessions at low cost to the seller.

**Leo:** To predict an outcome, record evidence of the alternative, its timing, and what it would cost to switch. Calling an airline large tells you little about whether it can meet this particular production deadline.

**Mira:** So how do we pull this all together into the first issue record? Take a single defect in the paper and write a decision that a commercial lead can approve.

**Leo:** For example, the buyer needs to nominate a funding owner before the delivery date. What is the instruction?

**Mira:** Copy the consent trigger into an internal evidence note, name the beneficiary, and attach the latest schedule. State what happens operationally if consent is refused.

**Leo:** Then propose a small amendment or signed consent, assign an owner, and set a decision date tied to the financing deadline.

**Mira:** An instruction to simply improve assignment gives the negotiating team no way to decide whether a counteroffer solves the problem. Be specific.

**Leo:** End your first reading with three working documents. First, on one page draw the parties and arrows.

**Mira:** Second, on another page, place each cash release, delivery, registration step, and transfer of risk on a timeline.

**Leo:** Third, in the issues list, put the open evidence requests next to the decision they could change. A missing lien release can be a hard stop even when its probability of causing a loss seems small.

**Mira:** While a delayed training credit might simply reduce the price you are willing to pay. That is how you clear the first hurdle.
