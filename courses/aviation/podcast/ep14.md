# Episode 14: Tax, governing law and the place of recovery

**Mira:** A Dubai-based airline pays a foreign owner for an aircraft delivered at a third-country airport. Some payments go through a lender's account in a fourth country. The contract selects a fifth country's law.

**Leo:** And somewhere in the draft, there is a single line saying taxes are for the lessee's account. That is not a commercial instruction. It is a bug hiding a massive cash leak. Welcome to episode fourteen: Tax, governing law and the place of recovery.

**Mira:** As deal counsel, I cannot route a cross-border closing to the right tax advisers until we break that one line into a detailed event map. Who invoices whom? Where are the parties resident for the relevant tax? Where is the aircraft when control passes?

**Leo:** For our listener building legal-tech, think of this as mapping your data architecture before you write the routing logic. You have to give each event a date, a location, an object, an amount, a payer, and a recipient.

**Mira:** Exactly. You include the deposit, acceptance, transfer of title, lease rentals, maintenance services, parts imports, the return flight, and the sale to the next operator. Then you ask local counsel to identify the relevant law for each specific event.

**Leo:** And you have to separate the financing events. You separate the owner's acquisition, its lease to the airline, and the payments under the loan. A conclusion about one supply can leave another completely unresolved.

**Mira:** Let us test that with a closing scenario. The delivery airport changes the night before closing. Which work products need to go back to their owners?

**Leo:** Everything tied to physical location. You have to send the change to your tax and customs advisers for the affected places. You revisit the import documents, the acceptance and title-transfer instructions, insurance, permits, and the closing evidence.

**Mira:** And critically, you confirm the real time and location after delivery. The place where the pilot signs a delivery receipt may be relevant evidence, but it does not settle every tax question.

**Leo:** Right, which brings us to the governing law fallacy. Suppose a contract is governed by English law. Delivery occurs abroad and the aircraft immediately enters another state. What should the tax memo analyze first?

**Mira:** It must analyze the events, locations, parties, and intended use that connect the transaction to the taxing states. A written choice of English law governs contractual questions within its scope, but taxing states apply their own connecting facts.

**Leo:** If a team just looks for which state has the highest headline tax rate before establishing the taxable event, they will price the wrong transaction. You have to separate the tax bill from the contract bill.

**Mira:** Let us break down those separate rows. Value-added tax, or VAT, is a transaction tax charged under local rules. A taxable business may be able to recover qualifying input tax on purchases, subject to local rules and supporting evidence.

**Leo:** Which is entirely different from withholding. A payer might have to deduct tax from a cross-border payment and remit it to an authority. And a customs charge might arise on import under its own valuation and relief rules.

**Mira:** You must keep those separate rows in your financial model even where all three appear in the exact same indemnity clause. You have to identify which amount is a permanent cost and which is temporary funding.

**Leo:** Let us work the math on temporary funding from the course text. Suppose a finance team expects to recover a tax amount in full after several months. The teaching case assumes four hundred thousand dollars of tax is paid now, and fully refunded in three months.

**Mira:** And simple annual financing costs six percent. If we are calculating the financing cost in dollars, we use three twelfths of a year.

**Leo:** Exactly. You multiply four hundred thousand dollars by zero point zero six, and then by three twelfths. That gives you a financing cost of six thousand dollars.

**Mira:** But treasury must still fund the full four hundred thousand dollars on the payment date. If the team budgets only the eventual net cost of six thousand, they will run out of cash at import.

**Leo:** Conversely, charging the full four hundred thousand dollar temporary payment as a permanent economic loss can kill a workable transaction. You have to calculate the peak cash requirement on the payment date and the financing cost over the assumed wait.

**Mira:** And if the expected refund is disputed, model both full recovery and denial. Then you take the evidence question straight to the adviser.

**Leo:** Let us talk about who actually gets that refund. Suppose a refund is legally available only to the named importer of record, while the airline has promised to reimburse the owner. What belongs in the draft?

**Mira:** You need a cooperation, evidence, and credit-back mechanism for amounts recovered by the eligible importer. Private reimbursement cannot give a party statutory refund rights.

**Leo:** Right. You cannot just draft an assumption that reimbursement itself creates refund eligibility. The contract allocates the economic benefit and requires the eligible party to support or pursue recovery on defined terms.

**Mira:** Let us look at a specific local rule. Under United Arab Emirates VAT Executive Regulations Article thirty-four, paragraph one, the zero rate for supply and import of the specified aircraft depends on its design or adaptation for commercial carriage of passengers or goods.

**Leo:** And the UAE Cabinet Resolution fifty-two of twenty seventeen specifically excludes aircraft with a recreational, pleasure, or sports design or adaptation.

**Mira:** So if a memo treats every aircraft supplied to a Dubai company as automatically zero-rated under Article thirty-four, paragraph one, what information tests that conclusion?

**Leo:** You need the type of supply, and you need concrete evidence of the aircraft's design, adaptation, and purpose. The customer's address in Dubai cannot substitute for the eligibility facts.

**Mira:** Exactly. A generic label like 'business jet' settles neither limb of that test. You have to verify the actual aircraft and the actual supply.

**Leo:** Let us shift to how we protect the ongoing cash flow. A gross-up increases a payment so the recipient keeps an agreed net amount after a required deduction.

**Mira:** And we have to solve the gross-up in the right direction. Let us work the second numeric example from the course. In an invented jurisdiction, assume a ten percent deduction from the entire gross rent, and an enforceable full gross-up.

**Leo:** And the recipient needs one hundred and eighty thousand dollars net. How many gross dollars must the payer fund?

**Mira:** You begin with the net amount due to the lessor. If the assumed withholding rate is r, and applies to the entire gross amount G, the cash received is G times one minus r.

**Leo:** So one minus zero point one is zero point nine. We divide our target net of one hundred and eighty thousand dollars by zero point nine.

**Mira:** Which gives us two hundred thousand dollars. The payer remits twenty thousand dollars to the authority and one hundred and eighty thousand dollars to the lessor.

**Leo:** If treasury just tried adding ten percent to the net amount, they would fund one hundred and ninety-eight thousand dollars, and the lessor falls short. But counsel must establish the actual tax base, rate, and relief before anyone uses this model on a live deal.

**Mira:** Now read the illustrative rider. It says if a deduction is required, the lessee shall increase rent so that the lessor receives the amount it would have received without that deduction, subject to the exclusions in Schedule T.

**Leo:** Schedule T is where the fight happens. An airline might accept responsibility for taxes arising from its location and use of the aircraft. But it will resist an increase caused solely by the lessor transferring the lease to a new recipient in a different tax position.

**Mira:** Meanwhile, the lessor wants freedom to sell or refinance, and certainty that scheduled receipts will service its debt. A workable compromise might protect the original net return, require the owner to supply relief forms, and cap the airline's additional burden on a voluntary transfer.

**Leo:** Let us test that compromise. A lease protects net rent but excludes increased withholding solely caused by an owner's voluntary assignment. The original payee would suffer no withholding. The new payee triggers a deduction solely because of the assignment.

**Mira:** What extra amount does the airline owe under this exclusion?

**Leo:** No extra gross-up for this increase. The exclusion allocates this incremental burden to the owner. But the airline must still comply with the statutory deduction to the authority.

**Mira:** Correct. The exclusion does not cancel the rent obligation or a statutory duty to remit. Now, suppose an owner proposes a transfer. The tax adviser finds that, under the law in force on the proposed transfer date, the change of recipient would materially increase the airline's gross-up.

**Leo:** And a second change in tax law is still under discussion by the government. Which amount belongs in the transfer test?

**Mira:** You ask the adviser to compare the same payment to each recipient under the law in force on the effective date. You give the deal lead that documented difference. On these assumptions, the proposed transfer fails the payment condition as drafted.

**Leo:** You price the possible future law change separately, and trace the tax clause that would allocate it if enacted. For a live transfer, have local tax counsel verify the payees' eligibility for relief and the documents required before treasury applies a rate.

**Mira:** And watch out for conflicting clauses. Suppose a lease indemnifies all taxes and elsewhere contains a narrower gross-up exclusion. Would the airline's edit to the exclusion work?

**Leo:** Not if the indemnity swallows it. You have to read both operative provisions, their defined taxes, exclusions, and priority clauses. An indemnity broad enough to recover the same amount can defeat the negotiated exclusion.

**Mira:** Exactly. You must make the interaction explicit and prevent duplicate claims. Now, let us move to choosing a place to resolve the right dispute.

**Leo:** Governing law supplies the rules for interpreting contractual promises. Forum describes the court or arbitral process chosen to hear the dispute.

**Mira:** Arbitration is private adjudication by a tribunal under an agreement. Its seat is its legal home and determines the supervisory framework. But a hearing can take place elsewhere.

**Leo:** The UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration, Article twenty, specifically distinguishes the legal place from a meeting elsewhere to hear witnesses. Local enactments determine the rules for a particular seat.

**Mira:** And enforcement means the steps that turn a judgment or award into recovery against a party or an asset. Choosing a respected forum can help with the first decision, but it leaves practical recovery problems in another state.

**Leo:** Let us test that geography. A clause chooses arbitration seated in City A. The witnesses testify in City B. And the engines we need to repossess sit in City C. What follows from those facts?

**Mira:** City A remains the stated seat. The hearing venue in City B was just chosen for convenience. You must investigate recognition and asset-specific relief in City C, because recovery against an object requires a route through the relevant law and authorities where relief is sought.

**Leo:** You need a route for a disputed invoice, and a completely second route for an aircraft that must be preserved today. A damages dispute might suit the main agreed forum, but urgent local orders are needed where the aircraft sits.

**Mira:** Have counsel check availability, time, notice, translation, and recognition of interim relief in that local jurisdiction. Compare an express right to seek urgent measures elsewhere with any other clause that purports to exclude local proceedings.

**Leo:** Get advice before promising what a court will do. Which brings up a massive modeling error. A lender's model treats a favorable final damages award as immediate payment. What change makes that model usable?

**Mira:** You must identify reachable assets and estimate enforcement time, expense, and collectibility. A right to collect and a funded recovery are separate stages. The nominal award can easily exceed recoverable value.

**Leo:** Let us bring all this back to the day of closing. We need to write instructions the closing team can actually execute. You create a short issues entry for each uncertainty.

**Mira:** Ask the tax adviser to confirm which party is the importer of record, the party legally responsible for the import declaration, and what evidence supports the treatment assumed for delivery.

**Leo:** Ask litigation counsel in the state where the aircraft is expected to sit to assess a preservation route on the assumed facts. Set a response deadline tied to funding.

**Mira:** And ask the treasury lead whether they can bridge a delayed refund. Name the exact person who can approve a change in maximum cash at closing.

**Leo:** A closing condition can require an opinion from local counsel. You have to agree on its addressee, assumptions, qualifications, and scope before the day of closing.

**Mira:** Read the assumptions as carefully as the conclusion. An opinion that assumes a valid import declaration is weak evidence when the identity of the importer is precisely what the parties dispute.

**Leo:** You convert the missing fact into an action, name its owner, and specify what becomes of the deal if no evidence arrives. Keep counsel's verified advice distinct from the commercial team's decision to retain a priced risk.

**Mira:** Here is a late-stage crisis. The tax adviser's memo assumes the aircraft is configured for commercial carriage. But the engineer tells you the cabin was extensively converted. What leaves the meeting?

**Leo:** You send the configuration and intended use to the adviser. You request revised advice, and you quantify the cash difference in both cases. Condition release on confirmation, or an authorized change to price and funding.

**Mira:** And you record exactly who owns that choice. Now, suppose your client needs certainty that an unexpected payment will never exceed available cash. The tax opinion is conditional on an unresolved fact.

**Leo:** A cash ceiling is a hard constraint. Counsel can evaluate the law on established facts, and treasury can test a funded fallback. A midpoint assumption does neither.

**Mira:** So which instruction addresses that need?

**Leo:** You resolve the fact before funding, or you agree on a capped exposure and a funded cover within the client's limit. You cannot just assume the governing-law clause magically resolves a cash shortfall.

**Mira:** Let us finish by comparing two versions of a tax indemnity. Read a tax indemnity from the point of view of the first claim. The lessor receives an assessment that could arise partly from operation in a new state and partly from the lessor's own business there.

**Leo:** Who must send notice, what information must it include, and how soon? Does failure to notify defeat recovery entirely, or only to the extent it prejudiced the airline?

**Mira:** Determine whether the lessor must pay before reimbursement, and whether an amount under dispute becomes payable immediately. The wording distributes control as well as money.

**Leo:** A generous right to challenge may be of little value if the airline must pay the full amount up front and lacks the documents to actually run the challenge.

**Mira:** Consider a proposed promise to cover any tax suffered by any owner. The airline would have to investigate the tax position of future owners, yet the assignment clause may let the lessor change that population without consent.

**Leo:** In a second version, the airline bears additional taxes arising from a change in the aircraft's use that it directs. Meanwhile, the owner bears agreed categories of tax attributable to its own business or its voluntary change of residence.

**Mira:** This gives each side an incentive to control its own decisions. You must ask how the wording treats a mixed cause. Agree what evidence establishes the increase and what comparison determines the incremental amount.

**Leo:** Because a hypothetical difference in tax liability can require both factual and legal assumptions. You need to test this revised package at three points.

**Mira:** At closing, check that the right parties deliver certificates, invoices, and residence evidence. During the lease, require notices of changes that affect the assumed tax treatment.

**Leo:** And at termination or transfer, track outstanding claims, audit periods, and refunds, including who remains bound to cooperate.

**Mira:** A clause that ends every cooperation obligation at redelivery can strand the party funding a later assessment. Explain to the client why a narrow promise that survives for a defined purpose and period can be safer than a broad, indefinite promise that is difficult to enforce.

**Leo:** Once counsel confirms the relevant deadlines, turn them into dated follow-up tasks with document owners. That is how you clear the deal. Time for takeaways.

**Mira:** One: Map every event by location, party, and intended use before comparing headline tax rates, because taxing states apply their own connecting facts regardless of your governing law.

**Leo:** Two: Separate permanent economic costs from temporary funding requirements in your cash models, and verify that the party promising reimbursement is actually legally eligible to claim the refund.

**Mira:** Three: Differentiate your chosen legal seat of arbitration from the jurisdictions where your assets sit, and build a separate legal route for urgent interim relief.
