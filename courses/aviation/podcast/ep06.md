# Episode 6: Reading the closing set

**Leo:** The shop says the aircraft is ready. The seller wants its money today. My instinct might be to just tell the closing team to wire the funds.

**Mira:** Which is how you end up buying an aircraft with an open repair invoice and no legal recourse. You have to read the documents behind those two statements, tell the closing team which instruction it can actually give, and explain what would change your answer. This is Reading the Closing Set.

**Leo:** I used to ask the team a simple question: 'Can we buy this aircraft?' But that just sends everyone back through the whole file, guessing what I actually care about.

**Mira:** Start with a question precise enough to settle on the call. Ask, 'Can our authorized signatory release the price at noon while the repair invoice is still open?' That tells you exactly which certificate, payment condition, and instruction to find.

**Leo:** So you put the executed purchase agreement, the incorporated schedules, signed amendments, and agreed escrow instructions beside one another. The executed set is the signed documents and attachments that record this transaction.

**Mira:** And you keep the draft that prompted the negotiation available. You need it to explain what moved.

**Leo:** That was always a pain point for me. Why look at the draft if we have the executed set?

**Mira:** Because when a dispute arises on the closing call about what a clause means, the draft shows you what the parties intentionally removed or changed. It provides the context. Then you read each document for its specific job.

**Leo:** Right. A specification describes the asset. A shop report records what someone observed on the hangar floor.

**Mira:** A signed election records which contractual option someone actually exercised. A release instruction directs a custodian to act. A page bearing a signature can serve any of those purposes, but you cannot mix them up.

**Leo:** You have to check the parties, the version, the date, the signature authority, and the referenced attachments. If two operative documents disagree, you read any express priority rule and its scope.

**Mira:** Exactly. A priority rule is a provision specifying which text controls a conflict within its stated scope. Get the conflict resolved before issuing instructions that assume an answer.

**Leo:** Let’s look at a hypothetical. Consider an invented sale packet. Draft four made a buyer's technical acceptance unconditional. But a margin comment asks for acceptance subject to repair.

**Mira:** Executed Section six permits a conditional election only for items listed in the signed defect schedule.

**Leo:** But here is the operational reality. The attached form still contains an unconditional box. The buyer plans to check that box and just staple on the shop report. They think the shop report acts as the defect schedule.

**Mira:** You have found a problem. The contract specifies a signed list, and the proposed form says something completely different. A shop report is not a signed defect schedule. Prepare a consistent election and get the signatures the executed contract requires before the election window expires. The election window is the period for exercising a contractual choice in the prescribed way.

**Leo:** Here is another scenario. A buyer requested a seller guarantee in a comment. But the executed schedule requires only an introduction to the service provider. Which obligation can you put on the closing checklist from this material?

**Mira:** Only the introduction. Record the guarantee as a requested protection that you could not trace into the signed set.

**Leo:** Why not both, provided the buyer circulated the comment before signature?

**Mira:** Because the executed obligation supports asking for the introduction. The comment just documents the request. Its timing supplies no promise from the seller.

**Leo:** What if the closing folder has two different copies marked 'final'? What settles which attachment was signed?

**Mira:** Retrieve the completed signing package or counterpart set, match the exhibit reference, and compare the actual attached pages. A file modification timestamp tells you when someone saved a copy.

**Leo:** Let’s follow one election through a public filing. We will open the filed Global five thousand purchase agreement used in module five. Start at the definition of Discrepancies.

**Mira:** That definition sends you to the delivery condition in Exhibit B and identifies the inspection facility as the decision maker on noncompliance. Next, find Section three point four.

**Leo:** Completion of the inspection and delivery of the final written report to both parties start the two-business-day election window.

**Mira:** The purchaser sends an executed Exhibit C to the seller, copying escrow. It can accept, accept subject to correction, or reject. Section three point five supplies the result of silence.

**Leo:** These steps tell you both what qualifies and how the purchaser exercises its choice. It involves the filed agreement, the definitions, and Sections three point four and three point five.

**Mira:** Now follow conditional acceptance forward. Under Section three point six, the seller pays to correct qualifying findings.

**Leo:** But the facility can require a verification flight, which is a flight used to check whether specified corrections worked.

**Mira:** The purchaser bears specified flight costs, so a seller-funded repair can still draw on the buyer's budget.

**Leo:** At Section four point three point seven, the applicable airframe and engine maintenance contracts listed in Schedule one to Exhibit B must be current at closing. Transfer depends on the provider permitting it.

**Mira:** A shop's repair sign-off cannot establish whether a provider permits transfer. Ask for the provider evidence separately. It involves the filed agreement, and Sections three point six and four point three point seven.

**Leo:** Now follow the money. The deposit definition ties its move to non-refundable status to acceptance evidenced by Exhibit C.

**Mira:** And Exhibit D serves as the delivery receipt. Read Sections four point one and four point four before equating an election with permission to pay the seller.

**Leo:** Because executed transfer papers can already sit undated with escrow.

**Mira:** On the closing call, the parties give linked instructions concerning releases, filings, registration, and payment. Section four point four makes the events contingent on one another.

**Leo:** The signed inspection election tells you why the buyer is still in the deal. The call instructions tell you when escrow can complete it. This involves the filed agreement, the deposit definition, Sections four point one and four point four, and Exhibits C and D.

**Mira:** Let's test this. In the filed agreement, the seller has deposited signed transfer papers. The purchaser sends Exhibit C accepting subject to repairs. What should the closing coordinator do next?

**Leo:** Should they send Exhibit D to the shop as authority to start repairs? Or release the papers immediately because escrow already has them?

**Mira:** Neither. Track Section three point six corrections, then verify closing conditions and obtain the Section four point four instructions. The election leads to correction. Custody of signed papers lets escrow act later. Exhibit D serves a different event entirely.

**Leo:** Let’s run the competing clocks. We will use a separate, invented closing set. All times are Dubai time. Business days for this exercise are Monday through Friday, with no holidays.

**Mira:** Executed Section six requires a signed election by sixteen hundred hours on the second business day after both parties receive the final report. Silence means rejection.

**Leo:** Section nine allows termination by written notice after Friday at eighteen hundred hours if closing has not occurred.

**Mira:** Section twelve requires notices to reach two specified mailboxes. And the escrow instruction separately sets Friday fourteen hundred hours as the bank cutoff for same-day payment. The bank cutoff is the latest time a bank accepts an instruction for a particular processing date.

**Leo:** The buyer gets the final report on Tuesday. The seller gets it Wednesday. You count Thursday and Friday.

**Mira:** Friday at sixteen hundred hours is the election deadline, but treasury loses the same-day payment route two hours earlier at fourteen hundred hours.

**Leo:** Friday at eighteen hundred hours opens a termination right. But remember, it does not send the notice automatically.

**Mira:** Put an owner and a receipt beside each deadline. Ask treasury whether it can position funds early without authorizing release. Ask counsel for a signed extension while the parties still have time to deliver it.

**Leo:** Both sides received the report as described. It is Friday fourteen zero five. The bank cutoff has passed. No election has been delivered. Did the sale terminate five minutes ago?

**Mira:** No. The buyer still has until sixteen hundred hours to deliver its election. Same-day payment needs a separate solution.

**Leo:** What about the bank cutoff? Does the election deadline move to the next banking day?

**Mira:** No, the bank cutoff controls payment logistics. The signed election and termination clauses have separate triggers entirely.

**Leo:** What if the broker replies 'extension fine' to a group chat? Which clocks can you erase?

**Mira:** Keep all three visible. Check authority and amendment formalities, secure the required signatures, and confirm delivery. Even a valid extension between principals cannot extend a bank's processing hours.

**Leo:** Let's convert the report into a decision. In this exercise, the specification requires a working cabin display and delivery of its installation record. The report says the display works but its installation record is missing.

**Mira:** Another entry reports a scuffed seat, and the specification expressly accepts ordinary cosmetic wear. The buyer would like both fixed.

**Leo:** You have to distinguish the document the seller promised from the cosmetic improvement you can ask the seller to fund. Mark the first item against the exact schedule line. Ask the shop whether the scuff exceeds the agreed wear standard before calling it a seller discrepancy.

**Mira:** Make the signed exception schedule usable by someone who never attended the inspection. An exception schedule is a list of identified matters reserved from an otherwise broader acceptance.

**Leo:** Give each item an identifier, its contract reference, the observed fact, the required evidence, who supplies it, who verifies it, and the latest time for action.

**Mira:** For the record gap, a photo of the working screen answers the wrong question. Ask for the installation record tied to the fitted unit, or an alternative expressly accepted by everyone whose consent matters.

**Leo:** If a lender requires the original record before funding, the buyer's willingness to accept a copy will not fund this closing.

**Mira:** Consider an invented election. Buyer accepts subject to item R on the attached signed schedule. All other closing conditions continue to apply.

**Leo:** Read this against the executed Section six in the example. Check the schedule is attached, signed, and complete when delivered. If item R never appears there, a later forwarded shop email may arrive outside the election window.

**Mira:** Have a named recipient acknowledge delivery of the exact package and save that acknowledgment with the copy sent.

**Leo:** What if the executed contract requires a signed exception schedule, and an authorized buyer signs the election but attaches yesterday's list, which omits the record gap? Thirty minutes remain. What should you prioritize?

**Mira:** Check the replacement-election procedure and deliver a correctly signed, complete package within the window.

**Leo:** Why not assume the seller knows about the gap because it attended the inspection?

**Mira:** Knowledge of the defect does not supply the required election. You still have time to use the applicable delivery process or secure a written agreement.

**Leo:** The buyer accepts a replacement record. Its lender insists on an original before releasing funds. What must happen before this financed closing can proceed? Can escrow declare the replacement authentic enough?

**Mira:** No. Supply the required original or obtain the lender's authorized change to its funding condition. The funding condition is a requirement the financier sets for advancing money.

**Leo:** The lender controls its advance. Buyer consent changes only what the buyer is entitled to accept.

**Mira:** Let's trade time, cash, and proof. Suppose a different, fully documented cabin repair can legally wait until after delivery. The lender agrees. The seller wants sale proceeds this week and fears an open-ended repair claim.

**Leo:** The buyer wants enough cash to finish the work and a remedy if the shop discovers more damage. A price credit reduces what the buyer pays and can leave it bearing the overrun. A holdback keeps part of the seller's proceeds in escrow.

**Mira:** Write down who ultimately gets that cash, what proves entitlement, how disputes are handled, and who pays when the reserve runs out.

**Leo:** For repair proof, the buyer wants shop sign-off and an invoice. The seller wants a finite obligation. This is because payment and verification can happen on different dates. A possible agreement here is that a named shop verifies completion against the listed defect.

**Mira:** For reserve release, the buyer wants time to notify a claim. The seller wants prompt return of unused cash. A late invoice could strand proceeds or leave the buyer short.

**Leo:** Release undisputed surplus at expiry and retain the documented disputed sum. A disputed sum is an amount whose entitlement remains unresolved under the agreed procedure.

**Mira:** Price the concession before recommending it. In this exercise, the fixed shop quote is eighty-four thousand A E D. Necessary positioning costs twelve thousand A E D, and three days of substitute transport cost six thousand A E D each. These three costs do not overlap.

**Leo:** The seller offers a credit of ninety-five thousand A E D as a full settlement. Compare that with the cash the buyer expects to spend. Then ask whether the quote covers a failed verification test and who carries any increase in downtime. Give your principal both the quantified base case and the open issue.

**Mira:** Let's walk through the math step by step. How many A E D of the estimated base cost remain with the buyer after the offered credit?

**Leo:** Eighty-four thousand, plus twelve thousand, plus three times six thousand is one hundred fourteen thousand. Subtract the ninety-five thousand credit to leave nineteen thousand A E D.

**Mira:** Let's change the structure to a holdback. Instead, the parties reserve one hundred sixty thousand A E D from seller proceeds. Accepted invoices of ninety-four thousand A E D are paid from it. At expiry, the instructions retain twenty-eight thousand A E D for a disputed invoice.

**Leo:** How many A E D can escrow return to the seller now? One hundred sixty thousand, less ninety-four thousand paid, and twenty-eight thousand still disputed, leaves thirty-eight thousand A E D for release. The disputed amount stays in escrow pending the agreed decision.

**Mira:** Would doubling the reserve solve the missing-original problem from the previous section?

**Leo:** Only if the funding condition is changed or satisfied. A larger reserve makes more money available for a covered claim after closing. The lender's condition determines whether there will be a funded closing at all.

**Mira:** Give an instruction someone can execute. Give the call leader three separate answers. What has the shop verified? Which conditions can the buyer confirm, and who else must consent? What can escrow release right now?

**Leo:** The diagram in the module follows a closing where the shop has finished its work but the lender has yet to confirm funding. Keep each answer next to its evidence and the name of the person entitled to act.

**Mira:** Three decisions on the closing call. The shop verifies the repair. The buyer checks its conditions. Escrow acts on agreed release instructions. Each decision requires different evidence.

**Leo:** Reading the closing call. A shop signature answers the first question. Identify who can answer each remaining question before funds move.

**Mira:** For a hold, name the missing item, owner, recipient, deadline, and consequence if it never arrives. For a close, identify the instruction version, amounts, payees, and linked document releases.

**Leo:** For termination, check the right has arisen, who may exercise it, the prescribed route, and the deposit accounting. A missed closing can give one party an option it prefers to keep open. Calling the deal dead too early can deprive your team of the chance to negotiate a short extension.

**Mira:** Write the hold instruction in language the person holding the money can use. 'Retain the full balance pending joint release instructions. The buyer’s finance lead will circulate written lender clearance to both principals and escrow by thirteen thirty Dubai time. If clearance is missing, keep the funds in place and reconvene before the fourteen hundred cutoff.'

**Leo:** Put a separate line beside the trustee or filing agent if it holds signed papers. Confirm receipt with each custodian. When the evidence arrives, compare the clearance against the condition it was supposed to satisfy. A lender acknowledging an email may still be reviewing the attachment.

**Mira:** On the Dubai call, show the contractual time zone beside the local time. Have the filing agent and bank confirm their own cutoffs for the actual date. Do not transplant the exercise calendar into your live file.

**Leo:** Friday at eighteen zero five, the sale remains open under the invented Section nine. The buyer wants to wait for the lender on Monday. What can you tell it? Does waiting guarantee the seller must close on Monday?

**Mira:** No. Check who may terminate now, whether notice has arrived, and secure a signed extension if the buyer needs certainty. The termination right creates an option. If the seller can exercise it, the buyer cannot assure a Monday closing by waiting.

**Leo:** Final scenario. All repair evidence is in. Escrow's signed instruction pays the full balance to the seller, but tonight's signed buyer-seller amendment requires a holdback. Escrow must assent to changed instructions.

**Mira:** Release authority is the permission the holder of funds or documents needs to deliver them. What belongs on the call sheet?

**Leo:** Hold disbursement until escrow and the required parties adopt matching instructions, then verify the revised split.

**Mira:** Exactly. The principals agreed the economics. Escrow still needs the authority required by its mandate to implement the payment split.

**Leo:** That brings us to three takeaways for reading the closing set. One, read each document for its specific job. A shop report records observations; it does not exercise a contractual option.

**Mira:** Two, map the exact deadlines and cutoffs. A bank cutoff for same-day payment does not change your contractual election window.

**Leo:** Three, verify the evidence before giving instructions. If a lender requires an original record, a photo of a working screen will not fund the closing.
