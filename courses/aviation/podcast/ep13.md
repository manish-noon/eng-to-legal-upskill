# Episode 13: Priority, de-registration and export

**Leo:** The maintenance team swaps an engine right after you circulated the signing pack. Which line of the closing checklist changes? Welcome to Priority, de-registration and export.

**Mira:** You have to reconcile the serial-number schedule, the ownership evidence, the lease or loan authority, and the priority searches for the newly affected engine. Update insurance and delivery records as needed. An unchanged tail mark on the airframe cannot establish which engine the buyer actually acquired.

**Leo:** That is the exact friction point where fleet operations meet legal documentation. I see tech teams treat an aircraft like a collection of interchangeable parts, but the law treats it as distinct legal objects. If an airline offers a lender its latest aircraft as security for a loan, before you ask who gets paid first, you have to find out exactly what the airline owns.

**Mira:** Right. Security means the lender has rights against an asset if the borrower defaults. But that airline might own the airframe, lease one engine, and have a borrowed engine on the other wing while its own engine is in a workshop. A photograph, the registration mark, and the fleet inventory can all describe the same flight while answering entirely different questions about ownership.

**Leo:** Here is a hypothetical. The aircraft has two qualifying engines, and one came from an exchange pool. Can the buyer tell who can sell it from the operator's colours or the airframe registration certificate?

**Mira:** Neither. The fact you must settle is whether the seller has the power to transfer the particular engines identified in the sale. A seller can operate an engine it has no power to sell. The airframe entry and the operator's livery do not supply that missing link. Installation alone does not transfer ownership of an engine under Protocol Article XIV(3).

**Leo:** So looking at this system, I need to put the transaction type, the manufacturer, the model, and the serial number beside each object on the closing checklist. And I have to verify those identifiers against the physical plate, the records, and the signed schedules. What governs these cross-border rights?

**Mira:** The Cape Town Convention and its Aircraft Protocol. They create a cross-border system for certain interests in qualifying airframes, aircraft engines, and helicopters. An international interest can arise under a security agreement, a title reservation agreement, or a lease. The documents and transaction must meet the applicable treaty requirements. The Protocol also extends specified rules to outright sales.

**Leo:** But the treaty does not automatically apply just because it is an aircraft. You have to check if the treaty reaches the specific transaction. You look at the Convention's debtor-location test when the agreement was concluded. And the Protocol adds a state-of-registry connection for certain airframes and helicopters.

**Mira:** Exactly, and do not conflate the objects. An engine standing alone does not acquire that additional registry connection just because it was mounted on a particular airframe. You must ask counsel to confirm eligibility, connecting facts, effective dates, and any transitional rules for older rights. A party's head office, the delivery airport, and the country of registration may give three entirely different answers under Aircraft Protocol, Articles I, III, IV, VII and XIV.

**Leo:** Let us move to priority. Priority means the order in which competing rights prevail. Article 29 supplies a general rule for covered competing interests. An earlier registered interest ranks ahead of a subsequently registered interest and an unregistered interest.

**Mira:** Yes, and the order of signatures alone is insufficient. Registration supplies notice and priority effects under the treaty. You still need evidence that the underlying interest was validly constituted and attached to the right object. A priority search certificate gives a search result at a particular time. You must record who searched, which identifiers were searched, the exact time, and which entries require a discharge or an explanation.

**Leo:** Let us stress-test that with a hypothetical. Two lenders hold valid competing international interests in the same eligible object. No special rights, subordination, or earlier interests apply. Lender A signed its agreement on Monday. Lender B signed its agreement on Tuesday. But Lender B registered on Wednesday, and Lender A registered on Friday. Who ranks first?

**Mira:** Lender B, because it registered first. On those facts, Article 29 applies registration order. Signature dates help establish the agreements, but the Monday agreement did not win the race to registration.

**Leo:** What if Lender A advanced more funds? Does the size of the exposure change the ranking?

**Mira:** No. The treaty rule relies on registration time, not the loan amount. That is why the priority search certificate is your critical evidence. But you also have to read the certificate correctly.

**Leo:** Right. Suppose a lender receives a certificate with no international registrations against the serial number searched. What can it responsibly say? Can it say the seller owns the aircraft free of all claims, or that the national authority approved its export?

**Mira:** Absolutely not. The only responsible statement is that the search found no such registrations for the identified object at that time. The certificate's scope matters. An incorrect serial number or a locally protected claim can leave a real exposure even when a search returns a blank result.

**Leo:** Which brings us to the national aircraft register as a separate source. It operates under national rules. You have to determine locally what that record proves about ownership and which domestic registers, filings, or searches matter. Some non-consensual rights can receive special treatment under state declarations.

**Mira:** Correct. A non-consensual right arises independently of a contract granting security. Counsel should check the relevant declarations and local claims, including possible detention rights. You also need to identify any agreed subordination, where a holder accepts a lower rank, and verify its effect against the particular claimant. In a priority analysis, a missing fact should produce a question in the memo.

**Leo:** So we have these two evidence trails. Registration establishes a priority record. Export requires its own authority, conditions, and operational release. A clean search and a recorded IDERA answer different questions, and the closing file needs evidence for both.

**Mira:** Let us look at how control releases at closing to manage these risks. Suppose a seller owes its bank money secured on the aircraft. Paying the seller in full leaves the buyer entirely dependent on the seller paying the bank.

**Leo:** As a buyer, I would never accept that dependency. You agree a funds-flow statement that routes the payoff directly, obtains the bank's release documents, and coordinates releases and registrations with the transfer.

**Mira:** Exactly. An escrow holder controls documents or funds under written release instructions. You have the holder specify which instructions it can execute and what happens if the filing service is unavailable. You require a fresh search at the agreed point and a post-closing check against the expected final sequence.

**Leo:** Let us work the math on a hypothetical escrow. A hypothetical price of five million dollars is in escrow. A valid payoff direction requires one million two hundred fifty thousand dollars directly to the senior lender. With all conditions met and no other adjustments, how many dollars go to the seller?

**Mira:** The closing agent pays five million dollars less one million two hundred fifty thousand dollars, leaving three million seven hundred fifty thousand dollars. The buyer already funded the entire price into escrow. That is how direct routing works.

**Leo:** Give me the concrete commercial instruction. How do you write that release condition so it actually bites?

**Mira:** Here is an illustrative closing instruction. Release the balance to Seller only upon receipt of the releases listed in Schedule R and confirmation of the agreed registrations. This language creates a strict dependency.

**Leo:** And Schedule R does the heavy lifting. It needs to identify the holder, each affected object, and the interest being released. You have to check whether a release in escrow is effective on funding, who registers the discharge, and whether a refund or standstill applies if one step fails. An undertaking to provide something next week has a completely different failure path from a release already under joint control.

**Mira:** Let us test that failure path. You receive a signed release for the airframe. However, the original mortgage schedule also lists an engine. Can treasury release all cash?

**Leo:** No. You must match the release scope to every encumbered object promised free of that interest. You request the engine release or evidence it was discharged separately. You must agree on an explicit decision on any remaining gap before treasury acts. You do not fund on an assumption.

**Mira:** Here is another gap scenario involving filed shared terms, sections 14.1.1, 14.1.2(1) and 6.1.1. A lessor refinances. In your hypothetical closing file, the airline has signed its consent to the security assignment. The incoming lender has sent the airline an unsigned letter promising continued use of the aircraft. Can you mark the letter condition complete?

**Leo:** Keep it open. The airline signed as the party giving consent, but the lessor must also obtain a letter signed by each applicable transferee and delivered to the airline. Compare the lender's undertaking with section 6.1.1, including the condition concerning a continuing event of default. You ask for the executed letter and evidence of delivery before treating this transfer condition as satisfied.

**Mira:** Precisely. And you refer any separate filing and priority steps to registration counsel. Now, let us turn to what an IDERA authorises. An irrevocable de-registration and export request authorisation identifies who can request removal from the national register and export through the treaty mechanism where Article XIII applies.

**Leo:** The debtor issues it, the registry authority records it, and the authorised party or its certified designee uses it subject to the applicable conditions. From a commercial standpoint, the lender negotiates this authority while everyone is cooperative. Requiring a copy of the signed form is a much weaker closing condition than requiring evidence of actual recordation.

**Mira:** That is the critical distinction. You read Protocol Articles IX and XIII together. The creditor needs the agreed remedies and circumstances for exercise. Holders of higher-ranking registered interests must consent in writing before exercise if their interests remain outstanding. A registry may require certification of discharge or consent.

**Leo:** And do not forget the physical reality. Safety law continues to apply. The team still needs legal access to the aircraft, records, insurance, and operational permission for the proposed movement. Possession, removal from a register, and actually flying to a new country are three distinct events.

**Mira:** Let us apply that. A hypothetical IDERA is recorded for a junior creditor. A higher-ranking registered creditor objects. What needs to happen before the junior creditor exercises Article IX(1) remedies? Do they present the IDERA again with a certified translation?

**Leo:** No, a translation solves a formality, not a substantive constraint. The junior creditor must obtain written senior consent or clear the senior interest and check the other conditions. Recordation identifies the authorised party, but it cannot reorder the senior creditor's rights.

**Mira:** The airline offers an undertaking to file tomorrow and a clean International Registry search on the engine. Can either one close the IDERA recordation condition?

**Leo:** Neither. The condition concerns recordation with the national registry authority. A separate system's priority search answers a different question. The best evidence is a confirmation from the registry authority identifying the recorded authorisation. That proves the authority actually controls processing.

**Mira:** Let us talk about what happens when this goes wrong. A client considers legal recovery complete when counsel has an enforceable entitlement. But fleet planning cares when the aircraft can earn money again. You have to budget for parking, storage maintenance, location of records, and any approved positioning flight.

**Leo:** This is where you have to put a price on elapsed time. You ask fleet operations how a late handover affects a replacement placement. You use the additional cost attributable to this delay. Rent missed and lost margin can overlap, so you give the client a cost per additional day and the evidence that would reduce the uncertainty.

**Mira:** Let us work the math on a teaching case. After the assumed legal handover, there are twelve extra ground days. Preservation costs three thousand dollars per day, and lost incremental contribution is five thousand dollars per day. These are distinct costs. How many dollars should be budgeted for this specific interval?

**Leo:** You calculate twelve times the sum of three thousand and five thousand, which equals ninety-six thousand dollars. This models the interval after handover rather than the entire default period. That is the hard cost of operational delay.

**Mira:** Delay often ties into insolvency proceedings. A manager expects an aircraft back on the sixtieth day because the airline flies to Dubai. Which facts are missing from that manager's assumption?

**Leo:** Almost all of them. You must identify the primary insolvency jurisdiction, its specific declaration, the relevant event and applicable date. You need to know whether timely cure and future performance allow retention, and the practical route to getting a serviceable aircraft back. Just flying to Dubai is not enough.

**Mira:** Exactly. The UAE declared that Article XIII applies. Its separate declaration under Article XXX(3) adopts Alternative A with a sixty-calendar-day waiting period. But Article XI turns on the debtor's primary insolvency jurisdiction. Its earlier-of rule and cure-and-perform option matter. You consult the UNIDROIT depositary declarations and Article XI for the particular facts.

**Leo:** When Article XI Alternative A applies, which event can allow the debtor to retain possession past that deadline? Is it showing the aircraft is essential to the route network?

**Mira:** No, operational importance alone is insufficient. Article XI(7) gives a defined cure-and-perform route. The debtor can retain possession through timely cure of the relevant defaults—other than opening insolvency proceedings—and agreement to perform future obligations. You check the applicable time under Article XI(2).

**Leo:** So when a lessor asks for a single return-to-service date, how do you make that forecast stronger? Do you align it with the maturity date of the lessor's secured debt?

**Mira:** No. The lender's maturity can raise urgency without changing the schedule for recovery. You make the forecast stronger by providing separate dates and dependencies for legal availability, physical release, and technical readiness. Permission and physical readiness have entirely different owners and uncertainties.

**Leo:** Let us rehearse a failed closing to see how these dependencies break. Try the checklist against a single failure in a hypothetical sale and leaseback. The airline sells an aircraft and immediately leases it back so it can keep operating. Its existing lender is to be paid from the sale. The new owner's lender finances the purchase and expects a security interest.

**Mira:** And the airline keeps physical possession throughout. Watching the aircraft on the ramp tells you almost nothing about which rights have transferred. You need to draw three columns headed seller's lender, new owner, and new lender. Put in each the document it must deliver, the cash it must receive or provide, and the evidence it needs before acting. Include the airline's continuing right to possession under the new lease.

**Leo:** Now stop the process at the point where the seller's lender confirms the payoff amount but will issue its discharge only after receipt. The buyer wants a clear interest before cash leaves its control. Neither side wants the gap created by the other's instruction. How do you solve that?

**Mira:** Transaction counsel must agree a release sequence with the bank and the escrow holder. A conditional discharge package, an irrevocable payoff undertaking, or a mutually approved closing protocol may address that specific gap. You write down exactly which instruction becomes unconditional when the bank is credited.

**Leo:** And check time zones and final transfer cutoffs. If an undertaking depends on a named signatory who has left for the day, closing has a practical dependency the legal diagram completely misses.

**Mira:** Carry that same exercise into a later default. A servicing company asks for the aircraft on behalf of the owner. You must look at its actual authority and the identity of each creditor. An owner that has assigned rights to its lender may have less freedom to exercise them than its title suggests.

**Leo:** Conversely, a security trustee can hold rights for several lenders. A security trustee is an entity appointed to hold and administer security for the financing parties. You identify who can direct it, whether any consent is required, and how that person proves authority to the relevant registry or court. Keep the default notices, service receipts, outstanding balances, and actual cure history alongside the registration evidence.

**Mira:** Finally, test the economics under two assumptions that yield the exact same priority ranking. In one, the bank has a current set of transferable records and an approved maintenance provider able to preserve the aircraft. In the other, only the operator can access part of the records and the provider has declined a new engagement.

**Leo:** The difference will appear immediately in ground costs, replacement timetable, and potential resale value. A lender can improve its position before default by negotiating continuing access and delivery of records, inspection rights, reporting of engine substitutions, and arrangements for preservation. You price the operational measures that actually shorten the recovery path.

**Mira:** Credit the treaty remedy for the problem it addresses, and assign the remaining work to someone who can actually execute it. Let us wrap with three takeaways. First, priority under Article 29 relies on the race to registration, not the date of signature or the size of the loan.

**Leo:** Second, an escrow instruction must create strict dependencies. Release cash only upon receipt of the exact releases and confirmation of agreed registrations, matching the scope to every encumbered object.

**Mira:** Third, legal entitlement is not operational readiness. When forecasting a return, use separate dates and dependencies for legal availability, physical release, and technical readiness.
