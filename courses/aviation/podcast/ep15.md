# Episode 15: Predict where a disputed term will land

**Mira:** An airline treasurer asks how long it has to fix a late payment. Your new artificial intelligence tool pulls ten references to five days from a document and tells treasury they are safe.

**Leo:** Which they are, assuming the data is accurate. If an AI table gives five days with a page number in every row, the algorithm has done its job.

**Mira:** And every row answers the wrong question. This episode is Predict where a disputed term will land. That single number extracted by your algorithm just triggered an event of default because it lacked commercial context.

**Leo:** I am looking at the extraction right now. It is a public filing, document fourteen, Schedule nine-a-roman-one. It explicitly says, quote, such failure continues for three Business Days or more. That is a concrete boundary.

**Mira:** In context, those words concern rent due on the due date. Read on and you find entirely different periods for supplemental rent and other amounts. A summary saying that every payment has three days would mislead treasury.

**Leo:** So a page number alone validates none of those choices. What else do you need before computing anything?

**Mira:** Identify the payment, the start event, the time unit, the creditor action, the consequence, and the exceptions. Reopen the clauses. Remove duplicates and identify related templates. Nor does a grace period automatically suspend interest after the due date. Find the interest clause separately.

**Leo:** I see. First, write the exact decision the client faces. For an operating lease, ask when an ordinary missed instalment of periodic rent becomes an event of default. Save technical transfer failures for a second question.

**Mira:** Exactly. Exclude deadlines for returning a deposit, paying an invoice after a demand, or complying with a non-payment covenant. A single number extracted from any of those clauses answers a different question. Give operations the actual deadlines, calendar, and bank cutoff for the contract eventually signed.

**Leo:** Let's test that boundary. I want to build a comparison of ordinary late periodic rent measured from its due date. I will use a clause giving five days to pay an indemnity after a written demand.

**Mira:** That fails. The trigger is a demand, not a due date, and the payment is an indemnity, not base rent. You need five business days from due date for unpaid base rent to become a default.

**Leo:** What if I use a clause giving five days after notice to fix a technical bank error?

**Mira:** Wrong again. Match the payment, the clock, the consequence, and the exception. Each other answer contains the same apparent number while testing a different commercial mechanism.

**Leo:** Let's move to building a band from a defined population. I will pull four lease texts. Document eleven, section twelve point one gives three business days for periodic rent. Document thirteen, Schedule nine-a gives five for rent.

**Mira:** Document fourteen, Schedule nine-a-roman-one gives three. Document twenty, Schedule nine-a gives five. Four is the interpolated median.

**Leo:** Some forms adopt similar drafting and some include special rules for bank or technical errors. We retain the ordinary branch for this comparison. We label the evidence four historical filed provisions.

**Mira:** Now calculate the interpolated median. Sort ascending. The lower quartile, median, and upper quartile are the values at twenty-five percent, fifty percent, and seventy-five percent along the ordered sample.

**Leo:** We use linear interpolation consistently. For n observations and a percentile fraction p, locate position n minus one times p, counting the first observation as position zero.

**Mira:** Walk through the math for our four filed texts, ordered three, three, five, and five.

**Leo:** We have four observations, so n is four. N minus one is three. The median is the fiftieth percentile, so p is zero point five. Three times zero point five is one point five.

**Mira:** Meaning the median lies at position one point five.

**Leo:** Yes. Position zero is three. Position one is three. Position two is five. Position one point five falls exactly halfway between position one and position two. Halfway between three and five gives four.

**Mira:** Four is a useful starting estimate created by the calculation. Notice the midpoint occurs in none of those four signed provisions. Special transfer-failure branches are outside this comparison.

**Leo:** Let's try a new practice sample of four hypothetical grace periods, sorted two, two, six, and nine business days. We need to calculate the interpolated median in business days.

**Mira:** The fiftieth percentile has a zero-based position of one point five. Halfway between the second and third entries, two and six, gives four.

**Leo:** Before we calculate, we have to prepare one record per issue. Capture the clause reference, principle, airline's opening request, lessor's opening request, alternate outcomes, and the consequences of each.

**Mira:** An opening request, or open, is the position proposed in the current negotiation. A precedent is an earlier agreement that bears on this issue. A comparable is a precedent with a sufficiently similar structure and measure.

**Leo:** And a landing is the specific outcome you expect the parties to accept, together with the reason for expecting it. The forecast needs a confidence judgement and an explicit condition that would change it.

**Mira:** Exactly. And filtering that data matters. Filter by deal family first. Buying a new manufacturer's delivery slot and leasing an existing aircraft involve different obligations, remedies, and sources of bargaining power.

**Leo:** Within the family, I should choose filters likely to change this particular term. For a deposit, tenant credit support and whether cash or a letter of credit is allowed can matter more than the country named on the cover.

**Mira:** Just remember to state the unit. Percentage of price, months of rent, and business days cannot share one column.

**Leo:** I ran a new query for comparable terms. I have three usable results after filtering and a further twelve redacted schedules. I will just count the redactions as zero and recompute.

**Mira:** Redacted prices carry no usable magnitude.

**Leo:** Then I will quote the three results as the market's lower quartile and upper quartile.

**Mira:** Still wrong. You widen one defensible filter, disclose the change, and use labelled scenarios if evidence stays thin. The aim is a reproducible comparison. A wider but defensible group can help if the reason for inclusion remains clear.

**Leo:** A disclosure sample can overweight repeated forms, public issuers, or topics a company was obliged to disclose. The sample supports an observed range and particular drafting options. An airline negotiating tomorrow must check newer, more comparable evidence if available.

**Mira:** Once you have your band, decide which way the evidence pushes. Grade each distinct fact from the perspective of the buyer or airline. Give plus one when it improves that party's credible alternatives, minus one when it weakens them, and zero when its effect is balanced or unknown.

**Leo:** Control of the first draft, often described as holding the pen, sets a starting position and forces the other side to ask for changes. It can matter even when the party holding the pen is small.

**Mira:** But a lender's credit committee has a different source of power. It can make a concession impossible within approved terms. Decide which fact matters for which issue.

**Leo:** Consider an airline facing a short delivery window. Its supposedly competing offer expires before its board can approve the purchase. I will score that as a strong alternative because of the strict deadline.

**Mira:** Treat the offer as weak unless the airline can obtain an extension. If the customer can switch aircraft only by retraining crews and replacing spares, quantify that cost before awarding an alternatives point.

**Leo:** Never give three points for urgent, deadline, and must launch if all three describe the same timetable. We document actual dates, alternative terms, and approval authority.

**Mira:** Equally, a lessor incurring remarketing and storage costs has an incentive to preserve a particular placement. Leverage changes the cost, timing, or credibility of one side's alternatives.

**Leo:** The Airbus FY 2025 results report a commercial order backlog of eight thousand, seven hundred and fifty-four aircraft at year-end. This historical observation justifies asking the manufacturer what other slots it can offer for the required model and configuration.

**Mira:** It cannot determine this buyer's delivery date, or whether its commercial team can approve a support credit. Confirm whether a usable slot exists, whether the buyer can finance it, and whether a rival can deliver in time.

**Leo:** For a used sale, gather condition-adjusted alternatives and actual availability of records and inspection slots. Market-cycle evidence only changes the score through an identifiable effect on this trade.

**Mira:** A resale brochure offers an apparently equivalent aircraft, but its records are incomplete and the seller cannot meet the buyer's required entry-into-service date. How should it influence leverage?

**Leo:** I will award a full alternatives point because the advertised price is lower.

**Mira:** Check the cost and time to make the aircraft genuinely substitutable before scoring it. A credible outside option must satisfy the buyer's constraints. A cheaper offer can still matter if records or timing are fixable at an acceptable cost.

**Leo:** Let's move from the median toward the relevant edge. Suppose five invented comparable cash deposits are one, two, three, four, and five months of base rent.

**Mira:** The synthetic rental deposit band runs from two months to four, with a median of three. Lower security benefits the airline, assuming all other protections stay equal.

**Leo:** With an airline score of plus one, we move halfway from three toward two to get two point five months. The calculation models a directional judgement.

**Mira:** A business-day clause or a security package may use discrete choices. Report a plausible whole-day proposal or a precise security structure and say why you rounded or traded across terms.

**Leo:** What if the leverage score is extreme? A new hypothetical with an airline score of plus three has a deposit band of one, three, and six months. The opens are two and seven. What forecast follows? I calculate one month.

**Mira:** Incorrect. Clip the sum of leverage scores to the interval from minus two to plus two. Move from the median by half the absolute clipped score times the distance to the selected edge.

**Leo:** Positive goes toward the airline-favourable edge, which is lower for a cash deposit. So we go to the lower edge of one.

**Mira:** Finally, clamp the result between the actual opens. Limit the calculated outcome to the interval bounded by the parties' comparable opens. The lower open of two constrains the forecast, giving two months.

**Leo:** If both parties offer more days than the precedent median, that floor can drive the answer. We must recheck whether one open carried a condition that the other side's offer excluded before treating their numbers as comparable.

**Mira:** Let's test a grace-period band. It is three, five, and nine days. Longer helps the airline. The score is plus one and the parties open at two and ten. What is the model landing in days?

**Leo:** Move halfway from five to nine, giving seven. It is within the opens.

**Mira:** Correct. Now consider an override, a categorical fact that displaces an arithmetic forecast. A lender has given a written credit condition requiring an approved bank guarantee. There are no comparable numerical values for its form. How should you handle it?

**Leo:** I will encode the guarantee present as one, absent as zero, and offer half a guarantee.

**Mira:** You cannot offer half a guarantee. Record the categorical constraint and evaluate alternatives the lender can actually approve. A median does not relax a funding condition. A substitute needs documented approval and an assessment of cost and accessibility.

**Leo:** A manufacturer insists its standard form never changes. What evidence would tell you whether to spend negotiating time on a credit rather than rewriting the printed warranty?

**Mira:** Ask which items its commercial team can approve, how the proposed credit can actually be used, and what alternatives the buyer can accept. Compare the cash-equivalent value of the available credit with the expected value and enforceability of the requested remedy.

**Leo:** A landlord controls the operating lease form, but must place an aircraft this month. The airline has a funded alternative. I will assess that the draft owner wins every issue.

**Mira:** Record each distinct fact, identify its relevance to this term, and test actual approval limits. Starting paper, alternatives, and time pressure can pull in different directions. A global winner label conceals different decisions on credit, timing, and aircraft condition.

**Leo:** We need to state the forecast as a trade. An issues list can describe a disagreement without moving the negotiation forward.

**Mira:** Add a proposed landing, a funded trade, and a trigger for reconsideration. For ordinary late rent, the airline opens at seven business days. The lessor opens at three. The commercial question is whether this is an administrative error or a serious payment problem.

**Leo:** The possible trade is a shorter ordinary clock and an evidence-based bank-error exception. The lessor retains fast action against a real missed payment. The airline receives protection against a precisely defined operational failure.

**Mira:** Check that the technical condition is demonstrable before promising the finance team it can rely on it.

**Leo:** Which sentence makes an internal memo easier to challenge next week? I will write: Four historical provisions give three to five business days on this defined trigger. We propose five while the alternative placement is live. If it expires, re-test three plus a bank-error branch.

**Mira:** Perfect. Colleagues can challenge the dataset, the leverage fact, or the fallback independently.

**Leo:** The airline accepts shorter cure only if the special bank-error branch survives. A draft retains the shorter cure and deletes the branch. The days match, so the forecast is achieved.

**Mira:** The agreed package is missing a condition, so reopen the issue before sign-off. A term can be numerically correct while its protection has changed. A good record links the proposed trade and its dependencies to the next draft review.

**Leo:** We read the underlying package once more from the counterparty's side. A manufacturer may resist a change to a production specification shared across an entire order book yet accommodate an individual delivery credit.

**Mira:** An airline may refuse a reserve increase because of its cash covenant yet accept a stronger reporting duty. A lessor may concede a longer payment clock once it holds a current bank instrument covering the relevant exposure.

**Leo:** Attach a price to the concession on each side and test whether the trade actually solves the stated problem. A guarantee from the same distressed group may add little protection when the airline itself cannot pay.

**Mira:** A non-transferable credit may have little utility to a buyer taking its last aircraft of that type.

**Leo:** Keep a history of forecasts when negotiations move. If the client acquires a new source of financing, note the date, evidence, effect on credible alternatives, and the issues affected.

**Mira:** Revise the relevant scores and preserve the prior band so a later reviewer can tell a changed fact from a changed dataset. At signature, compare each realised term with the last recorded prediction, explain the misses, and save the actual clauses in the next precedent set with appropriate confidentiality controls.

**Leo:** Two files arrive after the call. One is a clean copy made by accepting every edit for readability. The other is a signed schedule. First label the status of each.

**Mira:** For a contested term, record the initial wording, whose edit you can identify, what the comment asks, and the last operative text you can verify. Compare by subject and defined terms because a section number can change.

**Leo:** Even a signed outcome does not tell you who conceded first or what was traded off the page.

**Mira:** Suppose a fictional owner's form requires prior approval for a return site. The operator has secured a maintenance slot only if it commits this week. Its comment asks for freedom to pick a facility.

**Leo:** The final definition lets it pick a qualified shop; the return covenant still requires advance notice. The operator gained control over selection.

**Mira:** It must still notify, and it still bears the risk of booking a shop outside the qualification. Trace a second veto in a financier consent or a technical schedule before calling this a complete win.

**Leo:** Compare two bargaining positions. If only one shop can meet the operator's return date, show the booking expiry and request a named approval now.

**Mira:** If the lessor needs to place the aircraft with a successor that requires specific tooling, offer objective capability criteria and inspection access. In each case, record what the other side receives in exchange and who is authorised to give it.

**Leo:** There is no numerical band for a transfer of decision authority. Practise this distinction in Work a negotiation file.

**Mira:** Time to close. First, define the exact decision before extracting numbers. A single figure isolated from its condition, payment type, or consequence answers the wrong question.

**Leo:** Second, build your band using linear interpolation, then move from the median based on scored leverage, clamping the result between the parties' actual opens.

**Mira:** Third, state your forecast as a trade. If a numerical concession relies on a deleted condition like a bank-error branch, that issue remains completely open.
