From the first advance to the delivery signature

The buyer starts funding construction long before it can fly or own the aircraft. Follow each pre-delivery payment, test the manufacturer's tender and release the closing balance against documents that deliver the agreed asset.

Build the advance ledger

A pre-delivery payment, or PDP, is an instalment paid before delivery. The manufacturer can use an advance to support production and test the customer's commitment. The airline ties up cash and takes the risk that the aircraft never arrives or its money comes back late. A schedule might demand payments on fixed calendar dates, a number of months before a scheduled delivery month, or completion of defined build milestones. Find the base for every percentage. A projected price used to set advances may differ from both the signed base price and the final adjusted price. Never substitute one for another just because the column headings both say “price.”

Suppose a USD 24 million agreed final price is paid in four steps. USD 2 million is due on signing, USD 3 million on a build milestone and USD 1 million on a test milestone. All three amounts are credited at closing. Treasury needs USD 18 million for the final payment. After the second payment the buyer has USD 5 million outstanding with the manufacturer. An amount exposed to a possible refund delay is a balance at a point in time. The sum of the running balances is not a meaningful measure of the cash paid.

For each delivery position create a dated ledger with amount due, amount invoiced, amount cleared, beneficiary, contractual basis and balance credited. Separate a reservation deposit already held from the first scheduled PDP. A clause demanding an initial percentage “less the Deposit” calls for a top-up. Read what happens when the order is placed close to the scheduled delivery month. A contract may bring forward instalments which would already have fallen due under the normal schedule. That can turn the signature date into a much larger funding event than the published first percentage suggests.

“beginning with a payment of 1%, less the Deposit”

The credit for the deposit sits inside the first payment calculation. Article 4.2 of the filed LAN aircraft purchase agreement identifies the advance payment base price as the denominator.

An order calls for a first PDP of 4% of a USD 25 million advance payment base, less a USD 250,000 deposit already received. How many USD must be wired now?

25,000,000 × 4% − 250,000 = 750,000. The deposit counts toward the first million.

A schedule sets percentages against the advance payment base price, projected at signing. The delivery invoice has since increased. Can treasury recalculate last year's PDP using the new invoice?

  • Never, because advance schedules cannot change once signed.
  • Only if the contractual schedule or an effective amendment calls for that recalculation.
  • Always, because each instalment must reflect final price.

Trace the specified denominator and any true-up. The final invoice alone does not change the earlier formula.

Distinguish a refund from protection for it

What does the buyer own the day after paying an advance? Read the title-transfer provision. Certain contracts can transfer ownership at a defined stage, including delivery of an unfinished aircraft, but the payment of a percentage alone tells you nothing about ownership. If the manufacturer keeps the money and owns the work in progress, the buyer may simply hold a contractual claim to performance or a refund. Ask when that refund becomes due, who owes it, whether interest accrues and what the buyer can realistically collect if the manufacturer cannot pay.

A segregated escrow arrangement can hold funds until a specified release point. A refund guarantee can add a second source of payment on the terms of the guarantee. Neither device automatically covers every dollar and every event. Compare the covered obligations, amount, expiry, demand formalities and identity of the beneficiary to the PDP schedule. Suppose the buyer can cancel for prolonged delay only after the guarantee expires. A guarantee with a large face amount can still pay nothing in the very scenario that matters. A parent company that has signed a different commercial letter may have assumed no refund obligation at all.

Financing PDPs can relieve immediate cash pressure and introduce a new mismatch. The bank may advance against the buyer's rights under the purchase agreement. It may seek an assignment of those rights and the manufacturer's acknowledgement that a refund will be paid to a specified account. Check whose consent is needed and where a refund goes if the lender must be repaid. If the purchase terminates, interest on the buyer's loan may continue while the buyer waits for the OEM to return the cash. Build a downside timeline with dates for termination, demand, refund, bank repayment and release of any guarantee.

The buyer has advanced USD 6 million and holds a promise to refund it “on termination.” What additional evidence would justify recording a recovery of USD 6 million on the termination date?

The due date and conditions, verified ability to pay or effective support, and the practical timing of collection. Model timing and possible shortfall until the evidence supports immediate collection.

A buyer funds USD 4 million of PDPs for 90 extra days after an unexpected refund delay. At 8% simple annual interest on an actual/360 basis, with no interest recovered, what additional USD financing cost is retained?

4,000,000 × 0.08 × 90 / 360 = 80,000. The principal refund and the extra financing cost are distinct.

A refund guarantee terminates before the first date the buyer can validly demand repayment after prolonged delay. Which proposed change directly closes that gap?

  • Raise the face amount but leave the expiry unchanged.
  • Replace the guarantee with an unsigned expectation of support.
  • Extend coverage through the demand period and verify the trigger and demand conditions.

A larger amount cannot be drawn after expiry. The trigger, timing and documentation must work together.

Read the delivery window as a sequence

Production initially may be scheduled by quarter or month. Closer to delivery the seller gives notice of a week, then a day. A delivery window gives a range within which performance is timely. A particular notice can narrow that range or simply forecast readiness. Look for the clause that makes a date binding. A calendar invitation for the buyer's technical team may authorise travel without starting the cure or delay clock. Record which notices have been given in the required form and to the specified address, and which dates are still planning assumptions.

Tender means an offer to deliver under the agreement. Ask what the manufacturer must have done before a tender qualifies. Identify the delivery location, state of completion, required documents, inspection access and notice period. A tender of a different configuration or missing essential documents might be disputed. An airline that simply fails to attend risks missing an acceptance deadline or triggering a wrongful-refusal provision. Respond promptly in the prescribed form, specify each unmet condition and reserve the rights counsel says the contract permits. Have a technical specialist own the evidence supporting each objection.

Read buyer dependencies at the same time. These may include choosing an interior by a freeze date, providing information for registration, arranging insurance, clearing payment and sending authorised representatives. The OEM may extend the timetable to the extent a buyer failure delays production. Ask for the link between the late input and the actual delivery path. A missing seat drawing can stop a cabin installation. An unrelated missing invoice address need not explain a delay in flight testing. Write down the original schedule, affected task, mitigation steps and earliest revised date.

The OEM forecasts Tuesday and invites inspectors. The contract starts the buyer's response clock on conforming tender with listed records. Key records are missing. What should the buyer do first?

  • Document the gap and issue the required response while preserving inspection and timing rights.
  • Remain silent because a defective tender can never trigger any clock.
  • Sign unconditional acceptance and raise the gap after title transfers.

A dispute about sufficient tender does not make silence safe. The form and timing of the buyer's response matter.

A forecast delivery week moves. What entry belongs in the issues list before you budget delay damages?

Identify the binding deadline, permitted adjustments, buyer dependencies and notice requirements. A moved forecast by itself establishes no compensable period.

Take acceptance evidence seriously

Technical acceptance records the buyer's conclusion after the agreed checks. The checks might include a review of configuration, records, ground systems and a demonstration flight. A demonstration flight has the scope and operating constraints specified for that delivery. White-space in a checklist is dangerous if nobody owns the test. Agree who can attend, who bears the flight cost, what happens when an inspector cannot travel and how repeat tests are scheduled. Identify technical people who may flag a discrepancy and the commercial or legal representative who can bind the buyer.

A discrepancy is a difference between the required condition and what is tendered. Classify it against an actual requirement, including its source, measurement and tolerance. An inspector's preference for different upholstery does not establish a contractual deviation if the installed item matches the approved drawing. Conversely, an apparently small record gap can prevent intended use. A signed discrepancy list should say which defects prevent acceptance, which can be cured after delivery, who pays, what evidence closes them and what remedy survives signing the acceptance certificate.

Withholding part of the price until a minor item is corrected can make acceptance commercially possible. Define the withheld sum, deadline, access needed, evidence of completion and release instructions. A buyer holding cash for a repair it will never permit the seller to perform creates its own dispute. Ask whether the holdback is the exclusive remedy for that item, whether the agreed amount covers the realistic cost and who bears downtime. A manufacturer may trade a documented repair plan for timely delivery when both sides can verify the remaining work is minor.

A buyer's engineer signs a delivery certificate that says all discrepancies are waived except those listed. A known defect appears only in internal email. What is the immediate exposure?

  • Every technical signature preserves all claims by default.
  • The seller may rely on the waiver. Record the defect in an agreed reservation before signing.
  • Internal notice to finance automatically amends the certificate.

Authority, waiver wording and reserved items determine the result. An internal email gives the seller no agreed exception.

Which post-delivery repair mechanism is most executable?

  • A promise that both teams will cooperate when convenient.
  • A general statement that the aircraft is new.
  • An identified defect, responsibility for access and cost, completion test, date and funded remedy.

A verifiable close-out route lets both sides decide when remaining funds may be released.

Coordinate cash, documents and insurance

The bill of sale is the seller's transfer document. The acceptance certificate is the buyer's record of acceptance. Payment confirmation, delivery of the bill of sale, release of security and transfer of risk can occur at different moments. Lay them out in minutes on closing day. Ask who holds originals or agreed electronic copies before release and how each party confirms the closing conditions are satisfied. Take a final check of the aircraft location because tax and registration analysis may depend on where physical delivery occurs. Obtain written confirmation from the advisers responsible for those matters.

Insurance must work across the precise handover. Suppose title passes while the aircraft remains with the manufacturer for completion work. The new owner might bear some risks while another party retains physical control. The documents can allocate that exposure specifically. Match insurance coverage to the party, activity, location and interval, including the ferry flight that moves the aircraft to its operating base. Do not assume the policy that covered production automatically covers the buyer's outbound flight. Request evidence from the broker in time to adjust the closing sequence.

Finally reconcile the price ledger, the signatory's authority, any agreed reservation and the bank's funding conditions. Verify changes to payment instructions through an independently established contact. A closing checklist should give every item a document, owner, verifier and release condition. If a condition remains open, record who may waive it and quantify what changes if it is waived. A decision to close with a documented minor cabin defect and funded cure may be sensible. A decision to transfer the entire price while the seller lacks authority to convey the engines is a different order of risk.

The buyer signs a technical acceptance at noon. The contract shifts risk on delivery of the bill of sale at 15:00. Who should arrange protection for that gap?

  • Identify the risk bearer under the contract and verify applicable cover through 15:00, with the buyer's cover ready at transfer.
  • Assume all risk shifted at noon because the technical team signed.
  • Ask the escrow agent to insure the aircraft by default.

The specified transfer event controls the contractual allocation. Check actual policy coverage for both sides of that event.

What would you ask treasury to refuse to release if an engine serial number is wrong on the bill of sale?

The final funds subject to that delivery condition. Have the seller correct the document and the technical team match it to the installed engine and title evidence before release.

After a valid delivery the seller sends a supplemental invoice for repeat tests. Which first step gives the best response?

  • Decline because the word “acceptance” always includes every test cost.
  • Trace the contractual cost allocation to the cause of the repeat and the signed test record.
  • Pay because delivery extinguishes all disputes.

The agreement may allocate costs differently for initial tests, buyer requests and repeats required to cure a defect.

Key terms

Pre-delivery payment
An advance payable before delivery and treated as the agreement specifies.
Advance payment base price
The contractual denominator for certain advances, which can differ from final price.
Tender
An offer to deliver in the prescribed condition and manner.
Delivery window
The period within which delivery is timely under the defined terms.
Discrepancy
A recorded divergence from an agreed requirement.
Technical acceptance
The buyer's formal decision on condition following specified checks.
Holdback
Part of a price retained for release under agreed conditions.
Bill of sale
The seller's document conveying or evidencing transfer of title.
Refund guarantee
A third party's defined promise to cover repayment, subject to its terms.
Ferry flight
A flight moving an aircraft between locations, for example from delivery to base.
