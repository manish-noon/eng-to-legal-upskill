Reading a marked lease

A Dubai maintenance desk has a serviceable spare and a departure in two hours. Its manager forwards a lease with a green comment saying the airline needs freedom to exchange parts. Can she fit this spare? By the end of this module you can find the controlling words, explain which request survived and give the desk an instruction it can act on.

Ask which paper you have

Start with the file that produced the question. A first markup shows edits one side put forward. A comment can explain a problem, ask a colleague a question or instruct a lawyer to propose words. Look for the actual deletion or insertion next to it. Sometimes the comment asks about an engine and sits beside text dealing only with parts. Sometimes the writer has raised an issue and left the clause untouched. Write down the asset and verb in each before drawing a conclusion.

A clean copy can be made by pressing Accept All Changes. That tells you how the proposed text reads with deletions removed. It tells you nothing about the other side's agreement. The same applies when someone turns a scanned markup into editable Word files. The revision records may have been created during transcription. Use the original page image to resolve a crossed-out word, a missing negative or a split paragraph. Use closing records to establish what the parties executed.

Before reading on: what would make two matching files weak evidence?

If the second was generated from the first, both describe one proposal. Ask for the independently assembled execution set and intervening amendments.

For an instruction today, identify the aircraft, parties, effective date and amendments. Match the body and schedules to the executed counterparts. Check any separate document the promise depends on. A signature sheet detached from the schedules leaves an important question open. Preserve the version you examined and the page or section where you found the answer. If you have only drafts, you can explain how they differ and identify the evidence that would clear the decision.

A clean PDF repeats every insertion in yesterday's markup. The covering email says “generated for ease of reading.” What can you report?

  • The proposed exchanges were approved yesterday.
  • The PDF shows how yesterday's proposal reads when its edits are applied.
  • The clean supersedes the signed parts schedule.

Both files come from one proposal. Matching words establish the text of that proposal. You still need an effective instrument before authorising a swap.

Work one exchange from comment to closing

All four documents below are newly written for this exercise. Assume the signed body and signed Schedule P belong to the same fictional lease, are effective today and have no later amendments. The item is a replaceable pump. A pool supplier lends pumps to several operators while their own units are in the repair shop. Possession puts the unit in the airline’s hands. Fitting it requires the applicable lease permissions and technical release. Title tells you who owns it. Receiving a serviceable unit in Dubai does not by itself transfer the supplier's title.

Opening draft, §4. The airline must obtain the owner's written agreement before fitting any pump owned by another person.

The owner gets a veto each time a borrowed pump is needed. The airline asks its engineers how long the approval queue takes at night. Their answer becomes a margin comment: “We need to fit a loan pump during a night stop. Can the shift manager sign it out and tell the owner the next morning?” This explains the failure mode. It gives the manager no authority.

Owner's counter, §4. A pump may be borrowed from a listed supplier on notice before installation. The airline must restore an owner-owned pump before the next revenue flight.

The counter removes individual approval for listed suppliers. The timing still defeats the airline's purpose. It can test the pump on the ground, yet cannot dispatch with the borrowed unit. When you summarise this round, report both changes. A one-line update saying “owner agreed to pooling” would send a shift manager to the wrong conclusion.

Signed §4. The airline may fit and operate with a loan pump from a supplier listed in Schedule P. It must send the exchange record within one calendar day after fitting and complete the exchange under Schedule P. Other suppliers require the owner's prior written consent.
Signed Schedule P. Only Harbour Stores is listed. By the end of calendar day twelve after fitting, the airline shall reinstall the repaired original or fit a qualifying permanent substitute and deliver evidence that the owner has acquired title free of third-party claims. The airline bears exchange and freight charges. The installation date is day zero.

The manager can now dispatch with a listed loan pump, assuming technical release and all other operating requirements are met. The individual veto survives for every unlisted supplier. Advance notice has become a post-installation record. The flight limit has become a calendar deadline with two ways to close. A permanent substitute requires evidence of title. Nothing in the signed pair makes the lessor pay for the flexibility. Give the shift manager the supplier list, the reporting address from the notices provision and the exchange deadline.

Harbour has run out. A sister company with identical technical approvals offers the same model. What clears the contractual supplier gate?

  • The shift manager records the identical part number.
  • The airline sends its exchange record the next morning.
  • The owner gives prior written consent for the unlisted supplier.

The signed schedule lists a legal supplier. Matching technical capability and timely reporting satisfy different requirements.

A listed pump is fitted on April 7. Enter the April day number by whose end the exchange must be completed.

April 7 is day zero. Twelve calendar days later is April 19. The exchange record has its own earlier deadline.

A qualifying permanent pump is on the aircraft on day eleven. Its supplier reserves ownership until paid. What remains open under Schedule P?

  • Only the technical release, because physical fitting completes replacement.
  • The airline still needs the required transfer of title and evidence, or must timely reinstall the repaired original.
  • The owner must settle the supplier invoice directly.

Delivery of a unit can precede transfer of title. A serviceability certificate describes condition. It cannot by itself eliminate the supplier's ownership claim.

Trace the permission through the rest of the lease

For a public comparison, open section 8.1.4 of the filed LATAM Aircraft Lease Shared Terms. It conditions pooling on the absence of a continuing event of default and identifies permitted counterparties. For an engine, trace the right to get the removed engine back, continued owner title and the requirement concerning installation at lease expiry. For a part, follow the cross-reference to Schedule 2, section 1.2. Section 8.2 keeps the airline responsible even when someone else has possession. Each step answers a different question for operations. Aircastle filed exhibit, sections 8.1.4 and 8.2 and Schedule 2

Read that exhibit alongside the invented pump agreement. If you copy its default condition into the pump advice, you have silently rewritten the exercise. Conversely, if you use the fictional twelve-day limit on a public-exhibit engine, you have followed a rule for the wrong asset under the wrong instrument. Write an issue entry with enough detail that a colleague can recreate it: agreement, date, asset, action, source paragraph, schedule and remaining question.

Before reading on: a definition now permits an approved repair station of the airline's choice. Can it book the station and say nothing?

Search the return covenant for notification, required lead time and addresses. Freedom to choose the station can coexist with a duty to give the owner time to attend.

In the public exhibit, a permitted pool operator takes custody of an engine. Its dispatch team says all lease obligations now sit with the custodian. Which reading resolves this?

  • Apply the notice rule from the fictional pump schedule.
  • Ask only whether the custodian has possession.
  • Read the engine conditions in §8.1.4 alongside the airline's continuing responsibility in §8.2.

Permitted custody and continuing contractual liability can coexist. A physical handover establishes who holds the engine today.

Follow the claim and the money separately

Now consider a fictional equipment warranty. A valve fails and the airline pays for repairs. The lease lets the airline pursue the maker's warranty while the lease continues and no payment default continues. The maker's warranty accepts claims only from the original purchaser. A fallback in the lease obliges the owner, as original purchaser, to submit a supported claim on the airline's request at the airline's expense. Changing the lease alone cannot make the maker accept an unfamiliar claimant.

Start a claim file with three named roles. The airline gathers the log entry, invoice and repair release. The owner signs the claim in the capacity the maker recognises. The maker decides coverage under its warranty. Record who must pay filing expenses even if the claim fails. A lawyer asking about standing wants to know who is entitled to bring this particular claim. A finance manager asking about the recovery wants to know whose account ultimately gets credited. Different paragraphs may supply the answers.

Suppose the signed proceeds schedule says that, for a repair finished before return, the airline receives the lesser of documented repair cost and cash recovered. Any excess goes to the owner. This entitlement survives return and excludes filing expenses from repair cost. The maker pays after the aircraft comes back. Finance should collect the repair completion record and evidence of receipt. Testing only the date cash arrived would send the entire payment to the wrong ledger.

The operator documents AED 39,800 of pre-return repairs and pays AED 900 in excluded filing expenses. The maker later pays AED 46,500. How many AED go to the owner under this schedule?

The operator receives AED 39,800. The owner keeps 46,500 less 39,800, or AED 6,700. The separate filing expense stays with the operator.

The airline has paid for a covered repair. The maker declines to process its claim because only the purchaser may file. What is the next contractual step under the fictional fallback?

  • Give the owner the supporting records and request that it file, budgeting for the airline's filing expense.
  • Send the operator's invoice again as proof that the warranty was assigned.
  • Demand the full repair cost from the owner immediately.

The fallback supplies a claimant the maker recognises. It creates a duty to submit a supported claim. Success and the eventual distribution still depend on the warranty and proceeds schedule.

Test the trigger against a calendar

A request to “allow time to fix this” can land in several places. One draft might lengthen a deadline. Another might leave the deadline alone and limit which consequences follow when a third party delays. Reconstruct the signed test in order. Which event starts the duty? When is notice effective? Who has the power to cure? What evidence counts? What right returns after cure, and does the lease need to be alive for it to return?

Take a fictional records-access letter. A storage provider promises the owner access to specified technical records when the lease trigger occurs. The airline is due to deliver the letter on Wednesday. A negotiated exception says no event of default arises under the letter-delivery paragraph while the airline has made and documented repeated timely requests and delay is solely the provider's. Keep the requests, acknowledgements, dates and the agreed form. A ticket created internally on Tuesday proves little about what the provider received. The exception protects one consequence. The letter is still missing and someone still needs to chase it.

Before reading on: the provider waited because the airline sent the wrong attachment. What happens to the proposed exception?

Delay now has another cause. Correct the form, update the timeline and escalate the missed obligation. The exception's sole-cause condition needs its own evidence.

A different signed clause gives the airline four calendar days after receipt of written rejection to replace security. The owner learns of the defect Monday, sends valid notice Thursday and the airline receives it Friday. Which event starts that clock?

  • Monday's discovery.
  • Thursday's dispatch.
  • Friday's receipt.

The signed trigger is receipt. Keep proof of that event and check counting conventions before giving treasury the replacement deadline.

Apply the same care to a suspension. Suppose claim authority resumes on cure during the lease, provided no other specified default continues, and ends when the lease terminates. A payment cure can restore authority on Tuesday if the lease is still alive. A payment on Tuesday after Monday termination cannot reactivate that same delegation. Read any surviving proceeds entitlement separately. An old ticket marked “cured” is a poor instruction to the team handling a claim after return.

Payment is cured after termination. The signed lease ends claim authority on termination but preserves entitlement to proceeds from qualifying pre-return repairs. What should the airline do?

  • File in its former delegated capacity immediately.
  • Identify the authorised claimant and separately preserve its evidence for the surviving proceeds entitlement.
  • Write off all prospective proceeds because authority ended.

Authority and entitlement have different endpoints. The airline can still document its share without claiming a power that has ended.

Give the desk a decision it can use

Close the paper trail with a short change record. Under “asked”, put the specific operation the team wanted to perform. Under “signed”, give the words' effect and section numbers. Under “action”, name the person who must do something, the deadline and the evidence that closes the loop. Mark an outcome partial if the right exists only for listed suppliers or if the airline bears a new cost. Record an unresolved issue as unresolved. Two endpoint documents rarely identify the person who persuaded the other side or the path between them.

For the pump, your shift handover could read: “Harbour unit cleared under §4 and Schedule P, subject to maintenance release. Shift lead to send the record within one calendar day. Materials team to reinstall the original or finish the documented permanent replacement by day twelve. Finance to cover freight and exchange charges. Call legal before fitting a unit from another supplier.” This tells the next shift what it can do tonight and what work it inherits tomorrow.

The first markup asks for free follow-up inspections. The execution set charges the airline only for a visit needed to verify a material defect was fixed. How should you record the result?

  • The airline won its full request.
  • The signed text leaves a defined follow-up cost with the airline. Establish why this visit was needed before approving its invoice.
  • The first markup proves an interim concession the owner later withdrew.

The two endpoints support a comparison of scope and cost. They do not establish a sequence of unobserved offers or make every repeat visit chargeable.

Key terms

Markup
A version displaying proposed text changes and sometimes comments.
Accepted clean
A rendering with proposed revisions applied.
Counterdraft
A responding party's proposed version.
Counterpart
A separately signed copy forming part of the same agreement.
Pool supplier
An organisation making equipment available for exchanges among operators.
Exchange record
The evidence identifying the removed and installed units and the time of exchange.
Permanent substitute
A replacement intended to stay under the lease's ownership and quality conditions.
Standing
Entitlement to bring the claim in question.
Proceeds schedule
The part of an agreement allocating a recovery to its beneficiaries.
Suspension
A pause in authority or performance under specified conditions.
