Predict where a disputed term will land

After this module you can turn an issues list into a forecast that another lawyer can reproduce, challenge and revise when a commercial fact changes.

Define the question both parties are answering

An airline treasurer asks how long it has to fix a late payment. You can pull ten references to “five days” from a document and still give the wrong answer. The answer depends on which payment, when the clock starts, how days are counted, who gives notice, and what consequence follows. First, write the exact decision the client faces. For an operating lease, ask when an ordinary missed instalment of periodic rent becomes an event of default. Save technical transfer failures for a second question. Exclude deadlines for returning a deposit, paying an invoice after a demand or complying with a non-payment covenant. A single number extracted from any of those clauses answers a different question.

Prepare one record per issue. Capture the clause reference, principle, airline's opening request, lessor's opening request, alternate outcomes and the consequences of each. An opening request, or open, is the position proposed in the current negotiation. A precedent is an earlier agreement that bears on this issue. A comparable is a precedent with a sufficiently similar structure and measure. A landing is the specific outcome you expect the parties to accept, together with the reason for expecting it. The forecast needs a confidence judgement and an explicit condition that would change it.

Public filing, document 14, Schedule 9(a)(i). “such failure continues for three (3) Business Days or more”

In context the words concern rent due on the due date. Read on and you find different periods for supplemental rent and other amounts. A summary saying that every payment has three days would mislead treasury. Nor does a grace period automatically suspend interest after the due date. Find the interest clause separately. Give operations the actual deadlines, calendar and bank cutoff for the contract eventually signed. Document 14, Schedule 9.

An AI table gives “five days” with a page number in every row. What else do you need before computing anything?

Identify the payment, start event, time unit, creditor action, consequence and exceptions. Reopen the clauses. Remove duplicates and identify related templates. A page number alone validates none of those choices.

Which item belongs in a comparison of ordinary late periodic rent measured from its due date?

  • Five days to pay an indemnity after a written demand.
  • Five business days from due date for unpaid base rent to become a default.
  • Five days after notice to fix a technical bank error.

Match payment, clock, consequence and exception. Each other answer contains the same apparent number while testing a different commercial mechanism.

Read these four lease texts together. Document 11 section 12.1 gives three business days for periodic rent. Document 13 Schedule 9(a) gives five for rent. Document 14 Schedule 9(a)(i) gives three. Document 20 Schedule 9(a) gives five. Some forms adopt similar drafting and some include special rules for bank or technical errors. Retain the ordinary branch for this comparison. Label the evidence “four historical filed provisions”. A disclosure sample can overweight repeated forms, public issuers or topics a company was obliged to disclose. The sample supports an observed range and particular drafting options. An airline negotiating tomorrow must check newer, more comparable evidence if available. 11 · 13 · 14 · 20.

Sort ascending. The lower quartile, median and upper quartile are the values at 25%, 50% and 75% along the ordered sample. Use linear interpolation consistently. For n observations and a percentile fraction p, locate position (n minus one) times p, counting the first observation as position zero. If the position falls between entries, take that fraction of the distance between them. Here the lower quartile lies between two threes, the middle between three and five, and the upper quartile between two fives. That makes three, four and five. Four is a useful starting estimate created by the calculation. It was signed in none of these four provisions.

Filter by deal family first. Buying a new manufacturer's delivery slot and leasing an existing aircraft involve different obligations, remedies and sources of bargaining power. Within the family, choose filters likely to change this particular term. For a deposit, tenant credit support and whether cash or a letter of credit is allowed can matter more than the country named on the cover. For delay rights, distinguish excusable delay from a missed date for which the manufacturer is responsible. State the unit. Percentage of price, months of rent and business days cannot share one column.

In a new practice sample of four hypothetical grace periods, sorted 2, 2, 6, 9 business days, calculate the interpolated median in business days.

The 50th percentile has zero-based position 1.5. Halfway between the second and third entries, 2 and 6, gives 4.

You have three usable results after filtering and a further 12 redacted schedules. What next?

  • Count the redactions as zero and recompute.
  • Quote the three results as the market's lower quartile and upper quartile.
  • Widen one defensible filter, disclose the change, and use labelled scenarios if evidence stays thin.

The aim is a reproducible comparison. Redacted prices carry no usable magnitude. A wider but defensible group can help if the reason for inclusion remains clear.

Decide which way the evidence pushes

Grade each distinct fact from the perspective of the buyer or airline. Give plus one when it improves that party's credible alternatives, minus one when it weakens them, and zero when its effect is balanced or unknown. Control of the first draft, often described as holding the pen, sets a starting position and forces the other side to ask for changes. It can matter even when the party holding the pen is small. A lender's credit committee has a different source of power. It can make a concession impossible within approved terms. Decide which fact matters for which issue.

Consider an airline facing a short delivery window. Its supposedly competing offer expires before its board can approve the purchase. Treat the offer as weak unless the airline can obtain an extension. If the customer can switch aircraft only by retraining crews and replacing spares, quantify that cost before awarding an alternatives point. Equally, a lessor incurring remarketing and storage costs has an incentive to preserve a particular placement. Document actual dates, alternative terms and approval authority. Never give three points for “urgent”, “deadline” and “must launch” if all three describe the same timetable.

The Airbus FY 2025 results report a commercial order backlog of 8,754 aircraft at year-end. This historical observation justifies asking the manufacturer what other slots it can offer for the required model and configuration. It cannot determine this buyer's delivery date, or whether its commercial team can approve a support credit. Confirm whether a usable slot exists, whether the buyer can finance it and whether a rival can deliver in time. For a used sale, gather condition-adjusted alternatives and actual availability of records and inspection slots. Market-cycle evidence only changes the score through an identifiable effect on this trade. Airbus FY 2025 results.

A manufacturer insists its standard form never changes. What evidence would tell you whether to spend negotiating time on a credit rather than rewriting the printed warranty?

Ask which items its commercial team can approve, how the proposed credit can actually be used, and what alternatives the buyer can accept. Compare the cash-equivalent value of the available credit with the expected value and enforceability of the requested remedy.

A landlord controls the operating lease form, but must place an aircraft this month. The airline has a funded alternative. Which assessment is sound?

  • The draft owner wins every issue.
  • The airline gets its opening proposal on all terms.
  • Record each distinct fact, identify its relevance to this term and test actual approval limits.

Starting paper, alternatives and time pressure can pull in different directions. A global winner label conceals different decisions on credit, timing and aircraft condition.

Move from the median toward the relevant edge

Suppose five invented comparable cash deposits are one, two, three, four and five months of base rent. The band runs from two months to four, with median three. Lower security benefits the airline, assuming all other protections stay equal. With an airline score of plus one, move halfway from three toward two to get two and a half months. The calculation models a directional judgement. A business-day clause or a security package may use discrete choices. Report a plausible whole-day proposal or a precise security structure and say why you rounded or traded across terms.

Clip the sum of leverage scores to the interval from minus two to plus two. Move from the median by half the absolute clipped score times the distance to the selected edge. Positive goes toward the airline-favourable edge, which might be higher for a rent grace period and lower for a cash deposit. A negative score goes toward the other edge. Finally, clamp the result between the actual opens. If both parties offer more days than the precedent median, that floor can drive the answer. Recheck whether one open carried a condition that the other side's offer excluded before treating their numbers as comparable.

A hypothetical grace-period band is 3, 5, 9 days (P25, median, P75). Longer helps the airline. The score is +1 and the parties open at 2 and 10. What is the model landing in days?

Move halfway from 5 to 9, giving 7. It is within the opens.

A new hypothetical with an airline score of +3 has a deposit band of 1, 3, 6 months. The opens are 2 and 7. What forecast follows?

  • One month.
  • Two months.
  • Six months.

Clip to +2 and go to the airline-favourable lower edge of one. The lower open of two constrains the forecast, giving two months.

A lender has given a written credit condition requiring an approved bank guarantee. There are no comparable numerical values for its form. How should you handle it?

  • Encode guarantee present as 1, absent as 0, and offer half a guarantee.
  • Record the categorical constraint and evaluate alternatives the lender can actually approve.
  • Remove it because it cannot be scored.

A median does not relax a funding condition. A substitute needs documented approval and an assessment of cost and accessibility.

State the forecast as a trade

An issues list can describe a disagreement without moving the negotiation forward. Add a proposed landing, a funded trade and a trigger for reconsideration. For instance, an airline might accept a three-day ordinary rent trigger if a documented bank outage gets a separate notice-based path and interest continues to accrue. The lessor retains fast action against a real missed payment. The airline receives protection against a precisely defined operational failure. Check that the technical condition is demonstrable before promising the finance team it can rely on it.

Write “Four historical provisions give three to five business days on this defined trigger. We propose five while the alternative placement is live. If it expires, re-test three plus a bank-error branch.” Colleagues can challenge the dataset, the leverage fact or the fallback independently.

The airline accepts shorter cure only if the special bank-error branch survives. A draft retains the shorter cure and deletes the branch. What is the status?

  • Forecast achieved because the days match.
  • The agreed package is missing a condition, so reopen the issue before sign-off.
  • Treat the change as editorial.

A term can be numerically correct while its protection has changed. A good record links the proposed trade and its dependencies to the next draft review.

Read the underlying package once more from the counterparty's side. A manufacturer may resist a change to a production specification shared across an entire order book yet accommodate an individual delivery credit. An airline may refuse a reserve increase because of its cash covenant yet accept a stronger reporting duty. A lessor may concede a longer payment clock once it holds a current bank instrument covering the relevant exposure. Attach a price to the concession on each side and test whether the trade actually solves the stated problem. A guarantee from the same distressed group may add little protection when the airline itself cannot pay. A non-transferable credit may have little utility to a buyer taking its last aircraft of that type.

Keep a history of forecasts when negotiations move. If the client acquires a new source of financing, note the date, evidence, effect on credible alternatives and the issues affected. Revise the relevant scores and preserve the prior band so a later reviewer can tell a changed fact from a changed dataset. At signature compare each realised term with the last recorded prediction, explain the misses and save the actual clauses in the next precedent set with appropriate confidentiality controls.

A resale brochure offers an apparently equivalent aircraft, but its records are incomplete and the seller cannot meet the buyer's required entry-into-service date. How should it influence leverage?

  • Award a full alternatives point because the advertised price is lower.
  • Check the cost and time to make the aircraft genuinely substitutable before scoring it.
  • Treat every competing offer as irrelevant once a draft is on the table.

A credible outside option must satisfy the buyer's constraints. A cheaper offer can still matter if records or timing are fixable at an acceptable cost.

Key terms

Open
A party's actual proposal, with its conditions and units.
Landing
The expected term or package the parties can accept.
Comparable
An earlier term with a relevantly similar structure and measure.
Quartile
A point at one-quarter, one-half or three-quarters of the ordered observations.
Linear interpolation
Calculating the value between adjacent observations in proportion to fractional rank.
Pen
Control of the first draft and the resulting starting position.
Leverage
A fact that changes the cost, timing or credibility of one side's alternatives.
Clamp
Limit the calculated outcome to the interval bounded by the parties' comparable opens.
Override
A constraint or categorical fact that displaces an arithmetic forecast.
Confidence
An assessment of the evidence and assumptions behind a particular predicted outcome.
