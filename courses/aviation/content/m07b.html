Running the aircraft under lease

A customer wants your spare aircraft for tomorrow. You can read the lease, tell operations who may fly it, identify whose permission is missing and give the customer an answer before the crew goes off duty.

Who has the aircraft tomorrow?

Imagine you run Cedar Air. Bayline has sold tickets for a route its own aircraft cannot serve. Its commercial director offers to pay for your aircraft and crew. You need to know who can decide whether to start, divert or cancel a flight, who supplies and directs the crew, whose operating authority covers the service and who has possession between flights. Selling the tickets answers none of those questions. Ask for the proposed contract and the instructions actually sent to both dispatch desks.

Open §§8.1 and 8.1.1 of the filed shared terms. Start one paragraph above the wet lease heading. The general rule restricts surrender of possession without prior written lessor consent. The paragraphs below give specific permissions. If your proposed operation fits a permission, check every condition before relying on it. If it falls outside the permission, return to the general rule. The lessor is the party leasing you the aircraft. The owner and lenders can have separate rights. Filed exhibit 11, Aircraft Lease Shared Terms.

“no possessory rights whatsoever are granted to the wet lessee” (§8.1.1(1))

Under this wet lease permission Cedar could operate flights for Bayline with Cedar’s regular employees or contractors. Cedar retains sole possession and control, keeps the registration unchanged, and maintains, insures and operates the aircraft as required. The arrangement must end within Cedar’s lease term. A continuing Event of Default blocks this route. Read the defined trigger before treating an accounting query as an Event of Default.

Before reading on: Bayline can specify departure times. Can its duty manager order a departure?

A customer can buy a timetable. Check who has final authority over the flight and what happens if the operator declines it. A right to overrule Cedar’s flight decision would contradict Cedar’s claim to sole control.

Bayline supplies the branding. Cedar supplies its regular crew, but the agreement gives Bayline exclusive overnight possession. Which fact changes the lease analysis?

  • The logo on the fuselage.
  • Bayline’s right to take possession between flights.
  • The fact that Cedar pays the pilots.

Section 8.1.1(1) requires Cedar to retain possession at all times. Its own crew on each flight cannot cure an overnight handover.

Read the sublease clock before signing

If Bayline wants to take possession and fly with its crew, read §8.1.1(2). Check whether it qualifies as a Permitted Air Carrier under the executed documents. That defined category matters even if Bayline holds an operating licence. The permission has a lease-term limit and a continuing-default condition. Bayline must acknowledge the owner, lessor and financing parties directly and accept that their rights come first. Section 8.2 keeps Cedar primarily liable. If Bayline fails to maintain the aircraft, Cedar still owes performance to its own lessor.

Work backwards from signing. Paragraph (i) requires prior written notice of the proposed agreement, including the carrier’s name and the term. For an independent carrier the minimum is 30 days. A qualifying leasing affiliate has a different clock of ten Business Days. Paragraph (j) adds a separate delivery of the agreed form at least five Business Days before proposed execution. After signing, an officer-certified copy is due within ten Business Days. Identify the notices address, delivery method and Business Day definition before entering a calendar date.

Now read paragraphs (d), (e), (g) and (l). Obtain satisfactory insurance evidence and the specified legal opinion. Check whether the proposed registrations trigger assignments, priority filings and additional evidence. If the plan changes the state of registration, identify the applicable aircraft-specific agreements and written lessor approval. Ask the operator’s regulatory lead to document any authority permissions for this aircraft, operator and route. An insurer can confirm coverage. It cannot grant operating rights.

Cedar gave the correct 30-day notice for an independent carrier. The parties agreed the final sublease today and plan to sign tomorrow. What still needs attention?

  • The agreed form must reach the lessor five Business Days before proposed signing.
  • The certified copy is enough if delivered after signing.
  • The carrier automatically becomes a leasing affiliate after 30 days.

Notice starts one clock. Sending the agreed form satisfies a separate pre-signing obligation. The later certified copy evidences what the parties signed.

Bayline signs every direct undertaking and takes possession under a permitted sublease. Who answers to Cedar’s lessor if Bayline ignores a maintenance obligation?

  • Bayline alone because it controls the aircraft.
  • The maintenance shop once it receives the aircraft.
  • Cedar remains primarily liable under §8.2.

A compliant handover gives Bayline possession. It leaves Cedar’s original obligations in force. Cedar may separately have a claim against Bayline.

Read a repair claim on its own terms

A lien is a claim against property securing an obligation. At an outstation a workshop says local law lets it hold the aircraft for unpaid work. Ask local counsel what right actually exists and how to release it. Separately ask whether Cedar promised the lessor to keep the aircraft free of that right. A claim can qualify under the lease while still disrupting the flight. A promise to the lessor cannot make the shop open its hangar.

Section 6.2.2 groups different kinds of permitted liens. For ordinary-course tax liens, look for tax not yet due or a good-faith contest through appropriate proceedings. For an ordinary-course repair lien arising by law, check whether the obligation is unpaid and how far past due it is. The filed exception runs only through 30 days overdue. Both branches require adequate reserves and protection against the stated risks, including sale or forfeiture, liability of a protected party and other liens. The final sentence adds safeguards against exposure to detention and similar consequences. Read it even when the invoice falls within the time limit.

Before reading on: The shop invoice is disputed and 31 days overdue. Does filing an objection preserve the repair-lien exception?

The appropriate-proceedings language belongs to the tax branch. Obtain a release or another effective resolution and escalate the lease breach. Record any continuing payment dispute separately.

A repair invoice is nine days overdue. Cedar has set aside cash. The shop lawfully advertises the aircraft for sale next week. Is the lien within the exception on those facts?

  • Yes, until the thirtieth day.
  • No. The likelihood of sale defeats the exception.
  • Yes, provided Cedar disputes the price.

The time limit and reserves satisfy only part of the test. An announced sale requires urgent action to protect the aircraft.

Offer access that the shop can deliver

In Schedule 2 §1.6 find the ordinary inspection right. At reasonable times the lessor or its authorised representatives can inspect the airframe and engines and copy the aircraft documentation and maintenance program. With no continuing Event of Default the clause requires 60 days’ advance notice, protects normal operations and maintenance, and limits the visit to one in twelve months. During maintenance the visitors may look behind panels already opened for the work. There is no general instruction here to dismantle the cabin for a photographer.

If the aircraft is at a third-party shop, arrange visitor passes, supervision and a records export. A password held by the airline may stop working when its storage subscription ends. Establish who can produce readable copies, including revisions and serial numbers, and whether that access survives a change of custodian. Schedule the inspection around actual shop access. The operator owes reasonable efforts to facilitate access to the premises. The inspector’s visit does not sign off the maintenance.

Cost is another question. The lessor ordinarily pays. A continuing Event of Default or discovery of a Default during the inspection shifts cost to the lessee under this clause. Distinguish the two capitalised triggers. A records query from an inspector identifies a question to investigate. It does not by itself establish either trigger.

No Default exists. A timely annual visit coincides with an open avionics bay. The lessor asks Cedar to open three closed bays and pay its travel invoice. What can Cedar offer under §1.6?

  • The open bay and relevant records on reasonable arrangements, with ordinary inspection cost for the lessor.
  • All four bays and travel costs because the lessor owns the inspection right.
  • Only a verbal briefing because the aircraft is in a shop.

The clause allows viewing through openings already made and copying records. The ordinary rule puts inspection costs on the lessor.

Price the installation and the reversal

A sales team calls a cabin installation temporary because it will run for one season. Engineering should ask whether removing it would impair value, utility or airworthiness. Schedule 2 §1.3.2 makes that a physical and economic question. A non-removable discretionary modification needs prior written lessor approval and compliance with the specified authority requirements. Removability alone does not excuse a loss in aircraft value. For the minor cabin exception, read the restoration obligation and its standard of cosmetic acceptance.

Carry the installation into redelivery. Under §1.3.4 the operator supplies a list if requested, 60 days before scheduled expiry. The lessor can require reversal by written request no later than 45 days before expiry, excluding work to the extent it forms part of a Required Action. If a request arrives 40 days before expiry, flag that missed deadline under this mechanism. Check separate restoration and return duties before releasing the removal budget. If a change to the approved type design stays on the aircraft, §1.3.2(7) specifies certification evidence and transferable use rights. Paying a design contractor for installation may leave the next operator without those rights.

Before reading on: Could both parties prefer to leave an installation in place?

Yes. A future operator might pay for the feature, while removal costs Cedar money. Identify who owns the installed equipment, secure the transferable rights and record the precise restoration duty that the parties agree to release.

An invented removable installation costs USD 72,000 to fit and USD 18,000 to remove. Reversal adds two idle days costing USD 6,000 each. What total life-cycle cost in USD should Cedar budget if reversal is required?

72,000 + 18,000 + (2 × 6,000) = 102,000. A purchase order covering installation captures only the first cash outlay.

An airworthiness directive, or AD, can make work compulsory. Schedule 2 §1.3.1 puts applicable AD work and inspections due during the term on the lessee. Section 1.3.7 can reimburse part of qualifying terminating repairs or modifications. Check the issuing authority, issue date, work, threshold, invoices and calculation. A terminating repair or modification resolves the directive on a lasting basis. An interim inspection may keep the aircraft compliant today without earning reimbursement under that provision.

Assume all §1.3.7 conditions are met. Eligible direct cost net of other benefits is USD 89,650. The threshold is USD 29,650. The amortisation period is 48 months and the contract counts 12 months to expiry. Using (48 − 12) / 48 × (cost − threshold), what does the lessor reimburse in USD?

36/48 × 60,000 = 45,000. Apply the remaining fraction to the amount above the threshold. Do not include lost flying revenue in eligible cost.

A cabin antenna stays with the aircraft. Its qualifying type-design change has the required certificates. Cedar’s licence prohibits use by anyone else. What remains open?

  • Only the colour of the installation.
  • Whether the lessor received the installation invoice.
  • Evidence of use rights transferable without charge or restriction to the specified successors.

Certificates evidence the approved design. Section 1.3.2(7) separately asks for transferable rights to use it.

Make the call at the dispatch desk

At 16:00 Bayline requests possession overnight and its own crew for a morning flight. Cedar has given no sublease notice. Tomorrow’s service matters to Bayline because it must move passengers. Cedar wants the revenue. The lessor wants to know who can fly its aircraft and how it will recover possession. Solve the immediate timing problem before negotiating the price. A promise to send the missing papers on Monday gives the lessor neither notice before the agreement nor a chance to check the receiving operator.

Hold the proposed handover. Ask Bayline whether it will buy tomorrow’s service with Cedar retaining possession throughout, using Cedar’s regular crew and dispatch authority, under unchanged registration. Record the revised agreement and amend the operating instructions. Check the lease term, default status, maintenance, insurance and actual operating permissions. Have the regulatory lead confirm the authority basis and obtain any required approval before releasing the service. If any condition remains open by crew cutoff, decline this flight. Bayline can seek capacity elsewhere. Do not issue a conditional aircraft release that the ramp could mistake for authority to hand over the keys.

Before reading on: Would a lessor email saying “route looks fine” release tomorrow’s handover?

Identify the actual decision and the person authorised to make it. Support for a route does not waive a missed sublease deadline or confirm authority and insurance for a different operator.

Cedar and Bayline sign the revised crewed-service agreement. The insurer confirms cover. The regulatory lead says a required route approval is pending at crew cutoff. What is the executable instruction?

  • Release because the lease and insurer permit the operation.
  • Decline the flight at cutoff and retain possession.
  • Send the aircraft with Bayline’s crew while Cedar awaits approval.

The required operating approval is independent of insurance and lease permission. Switching crews reopens the possession and control issue.

Key terms

Operating covenant
Promise governing how the aircraft may be used or managed during the lease.
Operational control
Authority over initiating, conducting or ending a flight.
Wet lease
Arrangement under which an operator provides aircraft and crew to perform services for another party.
Sublease
Lease by which the original lessee grants another party a right to possess and use the aircraft.
Prior written consent
Affirmative written permission obtained before the specified act.
Prior notice
Required communication to the named recipient before the specified act.
Permitted lien
Claim against property falling within a defined contractual exception and satisfying its conditions.
Agreed form
Final text settled for execution, before signature.
Airworthiness directive
Binding safety instruction issued by an aviation authority and applicable to specified products.
Terminating action
Work that fulfils a directive on a lasting basis for the requirement concerned.
Supplemental type certificate
Approval for a change to an existing certified design.
