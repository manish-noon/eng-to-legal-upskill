Casualty and default

Follow a lease from the first notice of damage to settlement or enforcement. You can identify the person with a claim, the source of recovery and the cash the airline must produce before that recovery arrives.

Keep possession dependable

Quiet enjoyment is a promise about interference with the airline's permitted use and possession. Its value depends on who makes it, who is bound through that party, and when it stops. A lessor can promise that it will leave a performing airline in possession. Its financier may also have a mortgage over the aircraft. If the lessor defaults under its own loan, the airline needs to know whether that financier has given a direct undertaking and what conditions apply. Obtain the signed form applicable to this aircraft and identify any later transferee who must accede to it.

Read the precise trigger for loss of protection. A default can mean an underlying breach before notice or time to cure has run. An event of default can mean a more mature breach which already satisfies the stated conditions for remedies. Some documents use these words differently or subject the promise to other exceptions. Locate the definitions rather than inferring them from capitals. A quiet enjoyment letter that falls away on any outstanding default could matter even when the lessee has the money to cure within a grace period.

“so long as no Event of Default has occurred and is continuing”

These words qualify the quiet possession undertaking in a filed financing acknowledgement. Ask whether the breach still exists, whose breach it is, and whether an earlier cured breach matters under this wording. Lawful regulatory interference and third-party claims outside the promisor's chain call for separate analysis. A service interruption alone seldom tells you who broke which promise. The airline pays on time. The lessor's bank seeks to enforce against the aircraft after a lessor loan default. What document most directly addresses continued possession against that bank?

  • The monthly utilisation report
  • The bank's signed non-disturbance undertaking and its conditions
  • The airline's cabin warranty

A promise from the bank can constrain its own action. A promise solely from the lessor may leave the airline with only a claim against a distressed owner.

A permitted sublease has been approved. Does the suboperator automatically enjoy the same promise from the financier?

Check who is named or included in the direct undertaking and the conditions for protection. Permission to sublease alone does not answer that question.

Draw the insurance recovery

Hull insurance concerns physical loss or damage to the aircraft. Liability insurance addresses covered liability to others, such as passengers or third parties. An additional insured is granted specified cover under the policy. A loss payee is designated to receive specified proceeds. Being named in one capacity does not automatically create rights in the other. Obtain the policy endorsements incorporating the required terms and compare them with the lease schedule. A broker's certificate can record the intended arrangements but cannot cure an exclusion in the policy.

Inspect amounts, territories, excluded operations, deductibles, expiry, cancellation notice and the treatment of particular risks. War risk can require separate coverage. Compare the insured aircraft and engines by identifier with the property definition, including removed engines or spare parts where relevant. An airline changing routes or placing an engine with a maintenance provider could move outside an assumption on which the cover was priced. Ask the broker for written confirmation of the particular operation. The party making an indemnity promise may still owe the other party even if the insurance fails.

Work one example from first loss to final bank receipt. Assume a total loss payment of USD 24 million, an admitted insurance claim of USD 23 million payable to the owner in sixty days, and a lease requiring settlement in thirty days. Ignore other amounts and assume the insurer's proceeds are fully credited or refunded against settlement. The final economic shortfall is USD 1 million. The airline could need up to USD 24 million at day thirty if it must pay first and has no bridge from the insurer. Distinguish replacement capacity and continuing rent from both numbers.

A financier wants direct payment of hull proceeds on total loss. Which policy role addresses the payment instruction?

  • The broker

  • Aircraft operator on the flight plan

Review the loss payable endorsement and settlement mechanics. A broker may place coverage but does not thereby become its beneficiary.

18,800,000 less 17,900,000 = USD 900,000.

Distinguish damage from an event of loss

A casualty is an incident involving loss or damage. An event of loss is a defined contractual condition that can trigger a special settlement or replacement process. Read the definition for permanent destruction, constructive total loss, prolonged deprivation and compulsory acquisition. They can start the clock on different dates and require different proof. Temporary grounding for repairs may continue under the repair regime. Even if a component has been destroyed, the aircraft as a whole may remain subject to the lease. Record the affected object before calculating the amount due.

For repairable damage, determine who approves repairs, whether the owner holds insurance proceeds as security, and when they are released to pay the shop. An operator can face a significant funding gap under a policy that covers the damage in full. For a lost engine, the remedy may require a replacement of the specified model, utility, condition and title quality. Read whether owner approval is required and whether any encumbrances must be released before title vests. A cheque equal to the estimated value may be insufficient to perform that obligation.

For a total aircraft loss, compare the agreed value schedule with the insurance actually recoverable on the date in question. Check the settlement deadline, any earlier deadline triggered by receipt of proceeds, continuing rent, arrears and rights in surviving parts. If insurance pays the security trustee, the person who holds security for financiers, identify how its receipt discharges the airline. Require a receipt or statement showing amounts applied. If the airline pays before the insurer, track its right to receive later proceeds and any insurer rights to recover or take salvage.

A destroyed engine can be replaced and the airframe is undamaged. What decides whether scheduled aircraft rent stops?

  • An assumption that all insurance write-offs terminate rent
  • Separate engine-loss and aircraft-loss provisions, including the rent covenant
  • The date of the engine's original order

Object-specific remedies may require replacement while aircraft rent continues.

A hull insurer accepts the claim but has yet to pay. Does acceptance stop rent under a lease that stops rent upon payment in full?

Trace actual receipt and discharge. Acceptance of insurance liability alone has not met the stated condition.

Bound the indemnity

An indemnity is a promise to bear specified loss or expense. Identify the protected people, the activities creating exposure and the exclusions. One clause may cover operating claims by third parties. Another may shift taxes or financing losses. The beneficiary may include the owner, a trustee, lenders and their employees. Check whether new financiers enter automatically and whether a transfer can increase the airline's burden. A claimant named in the indemnity can still need to prove causation and its actual losses.

Review exclusions for the protected person's wrongdoing, pre-delivery events and events occurring after compliant return. “Arising from operation during the term” may cover a claim asserted later for an incident during possession. Check survival, which is continuation of the obligation after termination. Match insurance consequences carefully. Recoveries actually received can reduce reimbursable loss if the wording requires credit. An obligation to pursue available insurance should assign responsibility, cost, timing and control. A right to settle a claim without consulting the paying party may enlarge the bill.

In a sample issues list, the airline accepts responsibility for passenger claims arising during its operation but resists covering an owner's unrelated financing dispute. Draw a boundary using the event, the protected person and the causal connection to the aircraft. Specify when the airline takes over the defence and whether separate counsel can bill it. A cheap settlement can become expensive if it restricts future operations or admits a breach in another proceeding. Reserve consent over these non-cash consequences. Ask for immediate notice of claims, preservation of evidence and consultation over offers. Decide whether late notice reduces recovery only to the extent it prejudices the defence. For the board model, cap any assumed reimbursement at amounts attributable to the covered claim and test a scenario in which a defence succeeds after substantial fees have already been paid.

The airline agrees to defend an operating claim. A proposed settlement also bars its use of a profitable route. Which negotiated protection matters most?

  • A promise to pay settlement cash quickly
  • Consent before a settlement imposes a non-monetary obligation on the airline
  • More frequent rent invoices

A cash-only cap misses the effect on operations. A settlement-control provision can protect the route decision.

From late payment to a remedy

Write a dated sequence for each alleged breach. Does the event arise on the due date, on notice, on expiry of a cure period or after a materiality threshold is crossed? Which days count, what address is valid and what proof of receipt exists? Non-payment, an insurance lapse and breach of a reporting promise may have different cure mechanics. An insolvency event may be defined separately and may trigger mandatory local rules. A cross-default can link another agreement's failure to this aircraft; a cross-acceleration test may require the other creditor to accelerate its debt first.

The owner may have a choice among terminating possession, demanding amounts under a formula, drawing security and seeking return. A contractual remedy still requires attention to applicable law, registrations, rights of other creditors and any court or authority whose involvement is required. Never translate a contractual right to request return into a promise of immediate physical recovery. Get advice on the law at the aircraft's location and the operator's insolvency forum. Check continued insurance, document access and arrangements for safe custody pending resolution. The next module group covers registries and de-registration.

Price the outcome after crediting amounts already paid, deposits applied and resale or re-lease proceeds where required. The owner may have transition, storage and remarketing costs. Interest and holdover rent may accrue for different periods. Identify any restriction on double recovery and the point at which the airline receives an accounting. If the draft leaves the buyer of the risk funding the full demand immediately and chasing credits much later, quantify the peak cash need separately from eventual loss.

Rent default requires notice and five business days to cure after receipt. Notice has been drafted but never delivered; no other event exists. Has this event of default matured?

  • Yes, drafting starts the period
  • No, the stipulated notice and elapsed cure period are missing
  • Yes, the owner can ignore the agreed procedure

A missed payment may already be a breach. This remedy trigger has further conditions.

A cross-acceleration trigger requires third-party debt above a threshold to have been declared immediately due. The debt is overdue but no declaration has been made. Does this trigger apply on those facts?

  • Yes, every missed payment is acceleration
  • No, the separate declaration condition remains unsatisfied
  • Only if the aircraft is grounded

The agreed test requires acceleration. A cross-default test might have a different result.

Key terms

Quiet enjoyment
Promise protecting permitted possession and use against specified interference.
Additional insured
Party included for cover under the terms of the policy or endorsement.
Loss payee
Party designated to receive specified insurance proceeds.
Deductible
Part of an insured loss borne under the policy by the insured.
Event of loss
Defined casualty or deprivation triggering a special contractual remedy.
Agreed value
Contractual amount specified for the relevant loss settlement.
Indemnity
Obligation to bear covered losses or expenses of a protected person.
Survival
Continuation of an obligation after expiry or termination.
Cure period
Contractual window to fix a breach before a specified consequence.
Cross-acceleration
Trigger depending on acceleration of another covered obligation.
