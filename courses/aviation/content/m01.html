Who owns the aircraft?

An airline paints its name on a new aircraft. A bank supplied most of the money. Another company bought the aircraft. Whose signature gets a deal over the line? After this module you can trace ownership, use, payment and security to the right people and documents.

Start with one aircraft and four questions

Open the filed aircraft-specific lease alongside this lesson. In the recitals, find the earlier agreement this one incorporates. Under §1.1 read the definition of Common Terms Agreement all the way to the closing parenthesis. It identifies a dated version and a letter amendment, and qualifies when later changes count. Write those on your file list. Section 7 incorporates schedules as well. If the common terms are missing, mark each question that depends on them as open. An identically titled form from a different airline will not fill the gap.

Now take one question through the file: when does the first rent instalment fall due? Read §3.2, return to the definition of Rent Commencement Date, then note the tender procedure it imports. Section 2.2 separately starts the term on delivery and calls for two acceptance documents. Draw one line for payment and another for delivery. Even when both events happen the same morning, separate lines tell the team which evidence to obtain if tender is contested. Use this read, follow, return sequence whenever a capitalised term carries the answer into another document.

A seller promises to deliver an aircraft. A buyer promises to pay for it. That sounds like a single exchange until the airline asks its bank to fund delivery. The bank wants rights over the aircraft if its borrower fails to repay. The airline wants to put the aircraft into service. A financing company may want to buy it and rent it to the airline. Before reading the long agreement, write down who should own the aircraft, who should control its use, who should receive the cash and who can claim against what if a payment fails. Each answer needs a document.

An original equipment manufacturer, or OEM, builds the aircraft. An airline may order directly from the OEM. A lessor buys or owns an aircraft and grants a lessee, often an airline, the right to use it for an agreed period. The lessor expects rent and may expect to sell or lease the aircraft again when it returns. Its estimate of the aircraft's value at that point is the residual value. The airline wants usable capacity on the dates its routes require. Those different business plans explain many arguments about maintenance, delivery windows and return condition.

The figure is one possible arrangement. On a cash purchase the owner and operator could be the same company. In a sale and leaseback the airline can buy the aircraft, immediately sell it to a financing owner and lease it back. It may continue to operate exactly the same routes. Two transfers of ownership and a lease still need to close in the right order. Ask which transfer the words “Delivery Date” refer to in the document in your hand. Identical defined words in two contracts can refer to different instants.

A carrier sells an aircraft and immediately leases it back. Which fact alone tells you least about who now owns it?

  • The carrier continues using its own crews on the same routes.
  • The seller signed and released a bill of sale naming the purchaser.
  • The purchaser paid the sale price and the sale closing conditions were satisfied.

Flight operations can continue through a change of title. The other facts concern transfer under the sale.

What happens if the onward lease starts a day before the sale closes?

Identify who can give possession, when rent starts and whose policy covers the intervening day. Align the triggers or agree an express arrangement for the gap.

Identify the asset and the promise

Title means ownership. Possession means physical control. Risk of loss identifies the party that bears specified damage or destruction at a particular time. A security interest gives a creditor rights in an asset to support payment of a debt. A debt is the promise to repay money. The security is the agreed support for that promise. Compare the security document, evidence of registration where relevant and the loan terms. Recording a debt amount next to the aircraft's price tells you very little about the order in which competing claims will be paid.

Start your asset list with the manufacturer's serial number for the airframe, the aircraft structure excluding its engines for this exercise. Then list each engine by serial number, the installed equipment, spare parts if included, and the technical records. The aircraft's registration mark can change. An engine may have been taken off for maintenance and replaced with a borrowed unit. Read the definition of “Aircraft,” the schedules identifying the property and each exclusion. Photographing a registration mark will never settle whether an engine on temporary loan belongs in the sale.

“free and clear of all liens, charges or encumbrances”

These words concern competing claims on title. A buyer still needs a condition test for a damaged engine. See the delivery provision in this SEC-filed aircraft sale agreement.

A lien is a claim against property that can secure an obligation. Other interests can constrain use or sale as well. Ask counsel to check the relevant registers and potential claims that may arise outside those registers. A contractual promise to deliver clean title is one source of recourse. A document releasing an existing lender's interest does a different job. If that lender is owed money, it may deliver the release only when paid. Tie the payoff and release to the closing instructions. Each part of the chain must identify the same asset.

The seller owns the airframe. One installed engine is a temporary loan and the draft includes “all installed engines.” What evidence resolves the conflict?

  • The latest route schedule for the aircraft.
  • Serial-number schedules, proof of ownership or loan, and an agreed substitution or exclusion.
  • A whole-aircraft photograph attached to the signature page.

The draft promises more than the seller may own. Appearance and operating history cannot transfer the lender's engine.

Which choice directly removes an identified lender claim at closing?

  • A second copy of the seller's promise that title is clean.
  • A larger technical inspection budget.
  • An effective release from the lender against its agreed payoff.

The seller's promise may support a later claim. The lender controls the release of its own security.

Read from verbs to consequences

A contract is an agreement that can create enforceable promises. For each important sentence, find the person who must act, what they must do, when, and what counts as successful performance. Then find the exception and the consequence of failure. A representation is a statement of fact, for example that the seller owns the listed engines. A covenant is a promise to do or refrain from doing something, for example to maintain the aircraft until closing. A condition to closing allows a party to withhold performance until the condition is met or waived. A remedy tells you what a party can obtain or do if something goes wrong.

Those categories can coexist. A seller can promise to remove its mortgage, which is a form of security. Release can also be a condition to the buyer paying. If closing proceeds anyway, can the buyer still claim for breach of the promise? Look at the waiver, the certificate signed at closing and any terms saying which promises survive closing. In an issues list, record the exact phrase that changes the outcome and who can change or waive it. A lawyer should confirm the effect under the governing law, the legal system the parties chose for their contract.

List the other documents this clause relies on. A short definition may send you to a schedule, an exhibit that forms part of the contract. That exhibit might incorporate a dated technical manual or a side letter, a separate agreement varying or supplementing the main terms. An amendment changes the original agreement. Start with the signed version, check every later amendment and record the order of precedence for conflicting documents. A missing attachment is a request for evidence. If a filed copy conceals a price or time limit, mark it unknown. Guessing the latest version can change the identity of the party bearing the loss.

The buyer can waive a delivery condition that records are complete. A separate promise to supply them survives closing. What should the closing team check first?

  • Whether the proposed waiver also releases claims under the surviving promise.
  • Whether “records” is printed in capital letters throughout.
  • Whether the sale price exceeds the replacement cost of the paper.

Permission to close despite a gap and release of the underlying duty have different consequences. Read the waiver itself.

A term sheet shows a guarantee from the airline's parent. The guarantee signature page is blank. What belongs on the issues list?

Identify who must execute the guarantee and whether receipt is a condition to funding. Track it as missing until the signed form and authority are verified.

Follow the money through the documents

Draw a separate arrow for every payment. Its label should name the payer and beneficiary, currency, amount or formula, date, conditions and evidence of discharge. In a financed sale, the bank might pay the seller directly on behalf of the buyer. That wire can satisfy part of the purchase price under the sale and create a debt owed by the buyer under the loan. Adding the loan principal to the price would count the same funding twice. Interest and financing fees are additional costs. Keep funding sources separate from what the aircraft costs to acquire or use.

Suppose the price is USD 16 million. An airline has already paid USD 2 million under an agreement that credits the entire amount at delivery. A bank agrees to advance USD 10 million to the seller at closing. The closing statement shows USD 14 million still due, funded by USD 10 million from the bank and USD 4 million from the airline. The price still totals USD 16 million. If the bank requires an inspection report that has yet to arrive, the written commitment may still leave the airline without the money on delivery day. Read the conditions to the bank advance alongside the purchase payment deadline.

Price is USD 19 million. A credited USD 3 million advance has cleared. A lender wires USD 11 million to the seller at closing. How many USD millions must the buyer add at closing, ignoring fees?

19 less 3 less 11 leaves 5. The buyer will separately owe its lender under the loan.

A special-purpose owner owes USD 9 million to its lender. The sale price is USD 14 million. Escrow pays that debt out of the price and no other amounts move. How many USD millions go directly to the owner?

14 less the 9 payoff leaves 5. Paying the lender discharges that portion of the price under the assumed instructions.

An escrow agent holds money or documents and releases them in accordance with agreed instructions. It can reduce the gap between paying and obtaining the documents that evidence title. Review who may authorise release, how disputes freeze release and who bears fees and interest. Distinguish the agent's administrative task from the buyer's responsibility to verify that the title chain is sound. An escrow account alone does nothing to cure a mismatch between a serial number in the release and the number on the engine.

A buyer's loan requires a maintenance report. Its purchase agreement requires unconditional payment tomorrow. The report is delayed. Where does the immediate problem sit?

  • The lender must waive its conditions because delivery is scheduled.
  • The buyer may owe the price before its lender is obliged to advance funds.
  • The seller must automatically defer payment until the lender funds.

Separate contracts create separate clocks. A financing condition, alternative cash or agreed extension would address the mismatch.

Name the decision maker

The logo on a slide is a poor guide to legal responsibility. An aircraft owner may be a special-purpose company, a separate entity set up for this transaction. Its manager may handle emails and receive notices. The wider group's balance sheet helps only to the extent that it has given enforceable support, such as a guarantee under which another company promises payment on defined terms. Ask for the legal name on the signature page, its capacity, evidence of authority and any conditions attached to its promise. A decision maker may be commercially influential and still lack authority to release money.

Assignment means transferring contractual rights. Passing obligations and replacing a party can require a different mechanism and consent, sometimes documented as a novation, an agreed substitution of one party for another. Suppose an airline orders a production slot and later wants a lessor to fund the purchase. Check whether the OEM permits a transfer of the slot, whether the original buyer remains liable and whether training credits or warranty rights travel with the aircraft. A consent for giving security to a lender may be narrower than consent for an outright sale of the purchase position.

The examples are bargaining hypotheses. The person preparing the first draft has the pen and controls its initial wording and exceptions. A seller with another creditworthy buyer ready for the same slot can resist changes. A buyer able to fund immediately and take a difficult delivery date can ask for concessions at low cost to the seller. To predict an outcome, record evidence of the alternative, its timing and what it would cost to switch. Calling an airline large tells you little about whether it can meet this particular production deadline.

Who can give effective consent when the lender, the aircraft manager and the owner all attend a call?

Read the agreement naming the person entitled to consent, its delegation rules and the required written form. Record the support expressed in the meeting and identify who can give the required formal consent.

A manufacturer allows a security assignment to a named bank. The buyer now wants a new lessor to take over the entire order. What should counsel investigate?

  • Whether the new lessor uses the same bank.
  • Whether the airline plans to operate the aircraft under its usual colours.
  • Whether substitution requires separate consent and whether the original buyer will be released.

A narrow permission to grant security need not permit replacement of the party responsible for performance.

An owner has few assets and offers a refund promise. Its parent operates a large fleet. Which evidence changes expected recovery most directly?

  • An executed, effective parent guarantee covering that specific refund.
  • A brochure showing the fleet.
  • A signed acknowledgement that owner and parent use the same brand.

A valid guarantee with appropriate coverage can add a source of recovery. Group branding creates no payment undertaking by itself.

Build the first issue record

Take a single defect in the paper and write a decision that a commercial lead can approve. For example, the buyer needs to nominate a funding owner before the delivery date. Copy the consent trigger into an internal evidence note, name the beneficiary, and attach the latest schedule. State what happens operationally if consent is refused. Then propose a small amendment or signed consent, assign an owner and set a decision date tied to the financing deadline. An instruction to “improve assignment” gives the negotiating team no way to decide whether a counteroffer solves the problem.

End the first reading with three working documents. On one page draw the parties and arrows. On another, place each cash release, delivery, registration step and transfer of risk on a timeline. In the issues list put the open evidence requests next to the decision they could change. A missing lien release can be a hard stop even when its probability of causing a loss seems small. A delayed training credit might simply reduce the price you are willing to pay. The rest of the course teaches you how to tell those decisions apart.

Key terms

OEM
The original equipment manufacturer that builds the aircraft.
Lessor
The party granting the right to use an asset under a lease.
Lessee
The party taking the lease and owing the agreed payments.
Title
Ownership of identified property.
Security interest
A creditor's rights in an asset supporting a debt or obligation.
Residual value
Estimated asset value at the end of a specified period.
Condition to closing
A requirement to be met or validly waived before the relevant closing obligation becomes due.
Covenant
A contractual promise governing conduct or performance.
Escrow
An arrangement for a third party to hold and release funds or documents on agreed instructions.
Assignment
Transfer of contractual rights subject to the applicable agreement and law.
Novation
Agreed replacement of one contracting party with another on the terms of the substitution.
