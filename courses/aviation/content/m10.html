Priority, de-registration and export

After this module you can tell a lender what its search establishes, what else it needs to recover the aircraft, and which missing evidence should hold up a closing.

Start with an object and a transaction

An airline offers the lender its latest aircraft as security for a loan. Security means the lender has rights against an asset if the borrower defaults. Before you ask who gets paid first, find out exactly what the airline owns. It might own the airframe, lease one engine, and have a borrowed engine on the other wing while its own engine is in a workshop. A photograph, registration mark and fleet inventory can all describe the same flight while answering different questions about ownership.

The Cape Town Convention and its Aircraft Protocol create a cross-border system for certain interests in qualifying airframes, aircraft engines and helicopters. An international interest can arise under a security agreement, title reservation agreement or lease. The documents and transaction must meet the applicable treaty requirements. The Protocol also extends specified rules to outright sales. Put the transaction type, manufacturer, model and serial number beside each object on the closing checklist. Verify the identifiers against the physical plate, records and signed schedules. Installation alone does not transfer ownership of an engine under Protocol Article XIV(3).

The next question is whether the treaty reaches this transaction. Check the Convention's debtor-location test when the agreement was concluded. The Protocol adds a state-of-registry connection for certain airframes and helicopters. An engine standing alone does not acquire that additional connection just because it was mounted on a particular airframe. Ask counsel to confirm eligibility, connecting facts, effective dates and any transitional rules for older rights. A party's head office, the delivery airport and the country of registration may give three different answers. Aircraft Protocol, Articles I, III, IV, VII and XIV.

The maintenance team swaps an engine after you circulated the signing pack. Which line of the checklist changes?

Reconcile the serial-number schedule, ownership evidence, lease or loan authority and searches for the affected engine. Update insurance and delivery records as needed. An unchanged tail mark cannot establish which engine the buyer acquired.

An aircraft has two qualifying engines. One comes from an exchange pool. Which fact needs to be settled before the buyer pays for both?

  • Whether both engines are painted in the operator's colours.
  • Whether the seller has power to transfer the particular engines identified in the sale.
  • Whether the airframe registration certificate shows the buyer's address.

A seller can operate an engine it has no power to sell. The airframe entry and the operator's livery do not supply that missing link.

Read the priority record

Priority means the order in which competing rights prevail. Article 29 supplies a general rule for covered competing interests. An earlier registered interest ranks ahead of a subsequently registered interest and an unregistered interest. The order of signatures alone is insufficient. Registration supplies notice and priority effects under the treaty. You still need evidence that the underlying interest was validly constituted and attached to the right object. A priority search certificate gives a search result at a particular time. Record who searched, which identifiers were searched, the time, and which entries require a discharge or an explanation. Convention, Articles 7, 19, 22 and 29.

Use the national aircraft register as a separate source for the aircraft's registration under national rules. Determine locally what that record proves about ownership and which domestic registers, filings or searches matter. Some non-consensual rights can receive special treatment under state declarations. A non-consensual right arises independently of a contract granting security. Counsel should check the relevant declarations and local claims, including possible detention rights. Identify agreed subordination, where a holder accepts a lower rank, and verify its effect against the particular claimant. In a priority analysis, a missing fact should produce a question in the memo.

Two lenders hold valid competing international interests in the same eligible object. No special rights, subordination or earlier interests apply. A signed Monday, B signed Tuesday. B registered Wednesday and A Friday. Who ranks first?

  • A, because it signed first.
  • B, because it registered first.
  • Whichever lender advanced more funds.

On those facts Article 29 applies registration order. Signature dates help establish the agreements, but the Monday agreement did not win the race to registration.

A lender receives a certificate with no international registrations against the serial number searched. What can it responsibly say?

  • The seller owns the aircraft free of all claims.
  • The national authority has approved its export.
  • The search found no such registrations for the identified object at that time.

The certificate's scope matters. An incorrect serial number or a locally protected claim can leave a real exposure even when a search returns a blank result.

Control releases at closing

Suppose a seller owes its bank money secured on the aircraft. Paying the seller in full leaves the buyer dependent on the seller paying the bank. Agree a funds-flow statement that routes the payoff directly, obtains the bank's release documents, and coordinates releases and registrations with the transfer. An escrow holder controls documents or funds under written release instructions. Have the holder specify which instructions it can execute and what happens if the filing service is unavailable. Require a fresh search at the agreed point and a post-closing check against the expected final sequence.

Illustrative closing instruction. Release the balance to Seller only upon receipt of the releases listed in Schedule R and confirmation of the agreed registrations.

Escrow needs both the listed releases and confirmation of the registrations before it can pay. Schedule R needs to identify the holder, each affected object and the interest being released. Check whether a release in escrow is effective on funding, who registers the discharge, and whether a refund or standstill applies if one step fails. An undertaking to provide something next week has a different failure path from a release already under joint control.

A hypothetical price of $5 million is in escrow. A valid payoff direction requires $1.25 million directly to the senior lender. With all conditions met and no other adjustments, how many dollars go to the seller?

The closing agent pays $5,000,000 less $1,250,000, leaving $3,750,000. The buyer already funded the entire price.

You receive a signed release for the airframe. The original mortgage schedule also lists an engine. Can treasury release all cash?

Match the release scope to every encumbered object promised free of that interest. Request the engine release or evidence it was discharged separately. Agree an explicit decision on any remaining gap before treasury acts.

Read the filed shared terms, sections 14.1.1, 14.1.2(1) and 6.1.1. A lessor refinances. In your hypothetical closing file the airline has signed its consent to the security assignment. The incoming lender has sent the airline an unsigned letter promising continued use of the aircraft. Can you mark the letter condition complete?

Keep it open. The airline signed as the party giving consent. The lessor must also obtain a letter signed by each applicable transferee and delivered to the airline. Compare the lender’s undertaking with section 6.1.1, including the condition concerning a continuing event of default. Ask for the executed letter and evidence of delivery before treating this transfer condition as satisfied. Refer any separate filing and priority steps to registration counsel.

Understand what an IDERA authorises

An irrevocable de-registration and export request authorisation, or IDERA, identifies who can request removal from the national register and export through the treaty mechanism where Article XIII applies. The debtor issues it, the registry authority records it, and the authorised party or its certified designee uses it subject to the applicable conditions. The lender negotiates the authority while everyone is cooperative. Requiring a copy of the signed form is a weaker closing condition than requiring evidence of recordation.

Read Protocol Articles IX and XIII together. The creditor needs the agreed remedies and circumstances for exercise. Holders of higher-ranking registered interests must consent in writing before exercise if their interests remain outstanding. A registry may require certification of discharge or consent. Safety law continues to apply. The team still needs legal access to the aircraft, records, insurance and operational permission for the proposed movement. Possession, removal from a register and actually flying to a new country are distinct events. Aircraft Protocol, Articles IX and XIII.

An IDERA is recorded for a junior creditor. A higher-ranking registered creditor objects. What needs to happen before the junior creditor exercises Article IX(1) remedies?

  • Present the IDERA again with a certified translation.
  • Obtain written senior consent or clear the senior interest and check the other conditions.
  • Calculate the unpaid junior balance.

Recordation identifies the authorised party. It cannot reorder the senior creditor's rights. A certified copy might satisfy a formality while leaving the substantive constraint untouched.

Which deliverable best evidences completion of an IDERA recordation condition?

  • The airline's signed undertaking to file tomorrow.
  • A confirmation from the registry authority identifying the recorded authorisation.
  • A clean International Registry search against an engine.

The condition concerns recordation with the national registry authority. A separate system's priority search answers a different question.

Put a price on elapsed time

A client considers legal recovery complete when counsel has an enforceable entitlement. Fleet planning cares when the aircraft can earn money again. Budget for parking, storage maintenance, location of records and any approved positioning flight. Ask fleet operations how a late handover affects a replacement placement. Use the additional cost attributable to this delay. Rent missed and lost margin can overlap. Give the client a cost per additional day and the evidence that would reduce the uncertainty.

After the assumed legal handover, a teaching case has 12 extra ground days. Preservation costs $3,000 per day and lost incremental contribution is $5,000 per day, each distinct. How many dollars should be budgeted for this interval?

Twelve times the sum of $3,000 and $5,000 equals $96,000. This models the interval after handover rather than the entire default period.

A manager expects an aircraft back on the sixtieth day because the airline flies to Dubai. Which facts are missing?

Identify the primary insolvency jurisdiction, its declaration, the relevant event and applicable date, whether timely cure and future performance allow retention, and the practical route to getting a serviceable aircraft back.

The UAE declared that Article XIII applies. Its separate declaration under Article XXX(3) adopts Alternative A with a 60-calendar-day waiting period. Article XI turns on the debtor's primary insolvency jurisdiction. Its earlier-of rule and cure-and-perform option matter. Consult the UNIDROIT depositary declarations and Article XI for the particular facts.

When Article XI Alternative A applies, which event can allow the debtor to retain possession?

  • Timely cure of the relevant defaults other than opening insolvency proceedings and agreement to perform future obligations.
  • Sending a request to renegotiate after the deadline.
  • Showing the aircraft is essential to the route network.

Article XI(7) gives a defined cure-and-perform route. Operational importance alone is insufficient. Check the applicable time under Article XI(2).

A lessor asks for a single return-to-service date. What evidence makes its forecast stronger?

  • An equally precise date copied from a different jurisdiction.
  • Separate dates and dependencies for legal availability, physical release and technical readiness.
  • The maturity date of the lessor's secured debt.

Permission and physical readiness have different owners and uncertainties. The lender's maturity can raise urgency without changing the schedule for either.

Rehearse a failed closing

Try the checklist against a single failure. In a hypothetical sale and leaseback, the airline sells an aircraft and immediately leases it back so it can keep operating. Its existing lender is to be paid from the sale. The new owner's lender finances the purchase and expects a security interest. The airline keeps physical possession throughout. Watching the aircraft on the ramp tells you almost nothing about which rights have transferred. Draw three columns headed seller's lender, new owner and new lender. Put in each the document it must deliver, the cash it must receive or provide and the evidence it needs before acting. Include the airline's continuing right to possession under the new lease.

Now stop the process at the point where the seller's lender confirms the payoff amount but will issue its discharge only after receipt. The buyer wants a clear interest before cash leaves its control. Neither side wants the gap created by the other's instruction. Have transaction counsel agree a release sequence with the bank and the escrow holder. A conditional discharge package, an irrevocable payoff undertaking or a mutually approved closing protocol may address that specific gap. Write down exactly which instruction becomes unconditional when the bank is credited. Check time zones and final transfer cutoffs. If an undertaking needs a named signatory who has left for the day, find out when that person or an authorised substitute can sign.

Carry the same exercise into a later default. A servicing company asks for the aircraft on behalf of the owner. Look at its actual authority and the identity of each creditor. An owner that has assigned rights to its lender may have less freedom to exercise them than its title suggests. Conversely, a security trustee can hold rights for several lenders. A security trustee is an entity appointed to hold and administer security for the financing parties. Identify who can direct it, whether any consent is required and how that person proves authority to the relevant registry or court. Keep the default notices, service receipts, outstanding balances and actual cure history alongside the registration evidence.

Finally, test the economics under two assumptions that yield the same priority ranking. In one, the bank has a current set of transferable records and an approved maintenance provider able to preserve the aircraft. In the other, only the operator can access part of the records and the provider has declined a new engagement. The difference will appear in ground costs, replacement timetable and potential resale value. A lender can improve its position before default by negotiating continuing access and delivery of records, inspection rights, reporting of engine substitutions and arrangements for preservation. Price the operational measures that actually shorten the recovery path. Credit the treaty remedy for the problem it addresses, and assign the remaining work to someone who can execute it.

Key terms

International interest
An interest within the treaty created through a qualifying security, title reservation or leasing agreement.
Aircraft object
A qualifying airframe, aircraft engine or helicopter within the Aircraft Protocol.
Priority
The ranking of competing interests in an object.
National register
A state's record of aircraft registration maintained under its rules.
Priority search certificate
A record of the International Registry search for the specified object at a given time.
Discharge
Removal of a registration or release of an interest, depending on the document and action specified.
Subordination
An agreement by which a holder accepts a lower ranking.
IDERA
An irrevocable de-registration and export request authorisation.
Primary insolvency jurisdiction
The contracting state where the debtor's centre of main interests is situated, subject to the treaty's presumption.
Escrow
A third party's holding of documents or funds pending specified release conditions.
