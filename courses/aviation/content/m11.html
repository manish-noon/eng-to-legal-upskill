Tax, governing law and the place of recovery

After this module you can route a cross-border closing to the right advisers, price a tax allocation, and explain how a favourable decision becomes cash or possession.

Map events before choosing a rate

A Dubai-based airline pays a foreign owner for an aircraft delivered at a third-country airport. Some payments go through a lender's account in a fourth country. The contract selects a fifth country's law. A single line saying “taxes for lessee's account” conceals several questions. Who invoices whom? Where are the parties resident for the relevant tax? Where is the aircraft when control passes? Who imports it and for what use? Which government can tax this payment or event? Who must account for tax, who bears the cost, and who can recover a credit?

Make an event map. Give each event a date, location, object, amount, payer and recipient. Include the deposit, acceptance, transfer of title, lease rentals, maintenance services, parts imports, return flight and sale to the next operator. Ask counsel to identify the relevant law for each event. The place where the pilot signs a delivery receipt may be relevant evidence. It does not settle every tax question. In a financing, separate the owner's acquisition, its lease to the airline, and payments under the loan. A conclusion about one supply can leave another unresolved.

The delivery airport changes the night before closing. Which work products go back to their owners?

Send the change to tax and customs advisers for the affected places. Revisit import documents, acceptance and title-transfer instructions, insurance, permits, and the closing evidence. Confirm the real time and location after delivery.

A contract is governed by English law. Delivery occurs abroad and the aircraft immediately enters another state. What should the tax memo analyse first?

  • The contract choice as the sole source of tax rules.
  • The events, locations, parties and intended use that connect the transaction to taxing states.
  • Which state has the highest headline tax rate.

A written choice governs contractual questions within its scope. Taxing states apply their own connecting facts. Rate comparison before establishing the taxable event can price the wrong transaction.

Separate the tax bill from the contract bill

Value-added tax, or VAT, is a transaction tax charged under local rules. A taxable business may be able to recover qualifying input tax on purchases, subject to the applicable rules and supporting evidence. Withholding is different. A payer may have to deduct tax from a cross-border payment and remit it to an authority. A customs charge may arise on import under its own valuation and relief rules. Keep separate rows even where all three appear in the same indemnity. Identify which amount is permanent cost and which is temporary funding.

Suppose a finance team expects to recover a tax amount in full after several months. A refund depends on eligibility, documentation and timing. If the team budgets only the eventual net cost, it can run out of cash at import. Conversely, charging the full temporary payment as a permanent economic loss can kill a workable transaction. Calculate the peak cash requirement on the payment date and the financing cost over the assumed wait. If the expected refund is disputed, model both full recovery and denial, then take the evidence question to the adviser.

A teaching case assumes $400,000 of tax is paid now and fully refunded in three months. Simple annual financing costs 6%. Using 3/12 of a year, what is the financing cost in dollars?

Multiply $400,000 by 0.06 by 3/12 to obtain $6,000. Treasury must still fund $400,000 on the payment date.

A refund is legally available only to the named importer, while the airline has promised to reimburse the owner. What belongs in the draft?

  • An assumption that reimbursement itself creates refund eligibility.
  • A cooperation, evidence and credit-back mechanism for amounts recovered by the eligible importer.
  • An automatic doubling of the stated tax rate.

Private reimbursement cannot give a party statutory refund rights. The contract can allocate the economic benefit and require the eligible party to support or pursue recovery on defined terms.

Under UAE VAT Executive Regulations Article 34(1), the zero rate for supply and import of the specified aircraft depends on its design or adaptation for commercial carriage of passengers or goods, with an exclusion for recreational, pleasure or sports design or adaptation. Verify the actual aircraft and supply. A label like “business jet” settles neither limb. UAE Cabinet Resolution 52 of 2017, Article 34.

A memo treats every aircraft supplied to a Dubai company as automatically zero-rated under Article 34(1). What information would test that conclusion?

  • The colour of the airframe.
  • The residence of the pilot on delivery day, on its own.
  • The type of supply and evidence of the aircraft's design or adaptation and purpose.

The quoted rule is conditional. Check whether it applies to this supply and this aircraft. The customer's address cannot substitute for the eligibility facts.

Solve the gross-up in the right direction

A gross-up increases a payment so the recipient keeps an agreed net amount after a required deduction. Begin with the net amount due to the lessor. If the assumed withholding rate is r and applies to the entire gross amount G, cash received is G times (1 minus r). Divide the target net by that fraction. Figures in this exercise are assumptions for a fictional jurisdiction. Counsel must establish the actual tax base, rate and relief before anyone uses this model on a deal.

Illustrative rider. If a deduction is required, Lessee shall increase Rent so that Lessor receives the amount it would have received without that deduction, subject to the exclusions in Schedule T.

Read Schedule T before pricing this sentence. An airline may accept responsibility for taxes arising from its location and use of the aircraft. It may resist an increase caused solely by the lessor transferring the lease to a new recipient in a different tax position. The lessor wants freedom to sell or refinance and certainty that scheduled receipts service its debt. A workable compromise might protect the original net return, require the owner to supply relief forms, and cap the airline's additional burden on a voluntary transfer.

In an invented jurisdiction assume a 10% deduction from the entire gross rent and an enforceable full gross-up. The recipient needs $180,000 net. How many gross dollars must the payer fund?

Divide $180,000 by 0.9 to obtain $200,000. The payer remits $20,000 to the authority and $180,000 to the lessor. Adding 10% to net would fall short.

A lease protects net rent but excludes increased withholding solely caused by an owner's voluntary assignment. The original payee would suffer no withholding. The new payee triggers a deduction solely because of the assignment. Which extra amount does the airline owe under this exclusion?

  • The full increase required to preserve net rent.
  • No extra gross-up for this increase, while still complying with the statutory deduction.
  • No rent until the owner reverses the assignment.

The exclusion allocates this incremental burden to the owner. It does not cancel the rent obligation or a statutory duty to remit.

A lease indemnifies all taxes and elsewhere contains a narrower gross-up exclusion. Would the airline's edit work?

Read both operative provisions, their defined taxes, exclusions and priority clauses. An indemnity broad enough to recover the same amount can defeat the negotiated exclusion. Make the interaction explicit and prevent duplicate claims.

Choose a place to resolve the right dispute

Governing law supplies the rules for interpreting contractual promises and remedies within its scope. Forum describes the court or arbitral process chosen to resolve a dispute. Arbitration is private adjudication by a tribunal under an agreement. Its seat is its legal home and determines the supervisory framework. A hearing can take place elsewhere. Enforcement means the steps that turn a judgment or award into recovery against a party or an asset. Choosing a respected forum can help with the first decision while leaving practical recovery problems in another state. For an example, Article 20 of the UNCITRAL Model Law distinguishes the legal place from a meeting elsewhere to hear witnesses. Local enactments determine the rules for a particular seat. UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration.

Draw a route for a disputed invoice and a second route for an aircraft that must be preserved today. A damages dispute might suit the main agreed forum. Urgent local orders may be needed where the aircraft sits. Have counsel check availability, time, notice, translation and recognition of interim relief. An exclusive jurisdiction clause could restrict where a party may sue, subject to law and exceptions. Compare an express right to seek urgent measures elsewhere with any other clause that purports to exclude local proceedings. Get advice before promising what a court will do.

A clause chooses arbitration seated in City A. Witnesses testify in City B and the engines sit in City C. Which statement follows from those facts?

  • The hearing moves the legal seat to B.
  • City A remains the stated seat. Investigate recognition and asset-specific relief in C.
  • The tribunal can itself physically remove the engines from C.

A venue can be chosen for convenience. Recovery against an object requires a route through the relevant law and authorities where relief is sought.

A lender's model treats a favourable final damages award as immediate payment. What change makes the model usable?

  • Mark the award as cash once the hearing ends.
  • Identify reachable assets and estimate enforcement time, expense and collectibility.
  • Add the face amount of the award to the asset's resale value in all cases.

A right to collect and a funded recovery are separate stages. The nominal award can exceed recoverable value.

Write instructions the closing team can execute

Create a short issues entry for each uncertainty. For example, ask the tax adviser to confirm which party is the importer of record, the party legally responsible for the import declaration, and what evidence supports the treatment assumed for delivery. Ask litigation counsel in the state where the aircraft is expected to sit to assess a preservation route on the assumed facts. Set a response deadline tied to funding. Ask the treasury lead whether it can bridge a delayed refund, and name the person who can approve a change in maximum cash at closing.

A closing condition can require an opinion from local counsel. Agree its addressee, assumptions, qualifications and scope before the day of closing. Read the assumptions as carefully as the conclusion. An opinion that assumes a valid import declaration is weak evidence when the identity of the importer is precisely what the parties dispute. Convert the missing fact into an action, name its owner, and specify what becomes of the deal if no evidence arrives. Keep counsel's verified advice distinct from the commercial team’s decision to retain a priced risk.

The tax adviser assumes the aircraft is configured for commercial carriage. The engineer tells you the cabin was extensively converted. What leaves the meeting?

Send the configuration and intended use to the adviser, request revised advice and quantify the cash difference in both cases. Condition release on confirmation or an authorised change to price and funding. Record who owns that choice.

Your client needs certainty that an unexpected payment will never exceed available cash. The tax opinion is conditional on an unresolved fact. Which instruction addresses that need?

  • Resolve the fact before funding or agree capped exposure and funded cover within the client's limit.
  • Replace the unresolved fact with a 50% probability because the parties disagree.
  • Assume the governing-law clause resolves it.

A cash ceiling is a hard constraint. Counsel can evaluate the law on established facts, and treasury can test a funded fallback. A midpoint assumption does neither.

Compare two versions of a tax indemnity

Read a tax indemnity from the point of view of the first claim. The lessor receives an assessment that could arise partly from operation in a new state and partly from the lessor's own business there. Who must send notice, what information must it include, and how soon? Does failure to notify defeat recovery entirely or only to the extent it prejudiced the airline? Which taxes have been excluded? Determine whether the lessor must pay before reimbursement and whether an amount under dispute becomes payable immediately. The wording distributes control as well as money. A generous right to challenge may be of little value if the airline must pay the full amount up front and lacks the documents to run the challenge.

Consider first a proposed promise to cover any tax suffered by any owner. The airline would have to investigate the tax position of future owners, yet the assignment clause may let the lessor change that population without consent. In a second version the airline bears additional taxes arising from a change in the aircraft's use that it directs, while the owner bears agreed categories of tax attributable to its own business or its voluntary change of residence. This gives each side an incentive to control its own decisions. Ask how the wording treats a mixed cause. Agree what evidence establishes the increase and what comparison determines the incremental amount. A hypothetical difference in tax liability can require both factual and legal assumptions.

Test the revised package at three points. At closing, check that the right parties deliver certificates, invoices and residence evidence. During the lease, require notices of changes that affect the assumed tax treatment. At termination or transfer, track outstanding claims, audit periods and refunds, including who remains bound to cooperate. A clause that ends every cooperation obligation at redelivery can strand the party funding a later assessment. Explain to the client why a narrow promise that survives for a defined purpose and period can be safer than a broad, indefinite promise that is difficult to enforce. Once counsel confirms the relevant deadlines, turn them into dated follow-up tasks with document owners.

Key terms

Withholding
Tax deducted by the payer from a payment and remitted under applicable law.
Gross-up
An increase in the gross payment to preserve an agreed net receipt after a deduction.
Input tax
Tax incurred on purchases for which recovery may be available under local rules.
Zero rate
A taxable supply to which the applicable rules assign a zero rate.
Importer of record
The party responsible for the import declaration under the relevant customs regime.
Governing law
The law selected or determined to govern specified legal questions.
Forum
The court or arbitral process in which a dispute is heard.
Seat of arbitration
The legal home of the arbitration and its supervisory framework.
Interim relief
Temporary relief, such as an order preserving an asset, before final determination.
Recognition
Acceptance in one jurisdiction of the legal effect of another forum's decision.
