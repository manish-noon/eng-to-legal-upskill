Rent and security

Follow the cash from acceptance to return. You can distinguish a cost from a balance tied up as security, identify who carries downtime, and propose a concession the other side can actually grant.

Start the rent clock

A lease buys the airline time with an aircraft. On an operating lease the owner expects an aircraft back and cares about its future earning power. The airline cares about the cash required to put it into service and keep it there. These perspectives produce several payments with similar names. Start your review with the payment schedule and the signed form of delivery receipt. Put the first and last dates of basic rent, the recurring charge for possession, on a calendar. Check whether rent is payable in advance or in arrears, who designates the receiving account and what counts as receipt.

Delivery under the lease can precede the first revenue flight. In an illustrative deal, possession and rent start when the airline signs a certificate at the delivery site. The airline then flies the aircraft to its base, changes the cabin and completes its own entry checks. All of those days consume paid time. Even a generous maintenance reimbursement for work completed later cannot change this starting date. If the aircraft is ready but the airline has no pilots available, the owner will resist a condition that lets the airline choose when payment starts.

Read the rules for partial periods before assuming that half a month costs half a payment. One schedule might use the actual days in the period. Another specifies a thirty-day month, first and last days included or excluded, and a full first payment on delivery. Reconstruct the dated amounts using the agreement's convention. If the first partial payment is credited against the next full instalment, record the credit. Ask the operator for the earliest date it can use the aircraft, then place the cash schedule alongside the operational schedule.

A lease starts rent on signed acceptance. A cabin refit controlled by the airline takes a week after acceptance. Which document would change who pays for the week?

  • The airline's internal forecast
  • An agreed rent holiday covering the refit period
  • An earlier booking for the engine shop

Acceptance already started the clock. An internal forecast can identify the cost but cannot change the promise.

On delivery day a technical defect is discovered. When is the airline in the strongest position to secure a rent concession?

Before it signs the certificate, subject to its obligation to accept a conforming tender. Identify the failed delivery criterion and document the cure or concession before triggering rent.

Follow each dollar

Separate the invoice into basic rent, maintenance reserves and other sums. A maintenance reserve in this module means a usage-based payment against a defined class of future maintenance work. The next module follows its reimbursement rules. A security deposit covers specified failures to perform. It may be expressed as several months of rent, but it has a different job from rent. A deposit the airline expects to recover still needs funding. Model receipt, draw, replenishment and refund as distinct events. Keep an obligation register that links each event to its clause and evidence.

Consider a simplified first month. Base rent is USD 160,000, payable on delivery. A cash deposit equal to two months of base rent is due at the same time. Reserves at USD 100 per flight hour fall due at month end after 200 hours. The first day's outflow is USD 480,000. The month-end outflow is USD 20,000. Only USD 160,000 of the total USD 500,000 is base rent. The deposit and reserves impose different recovery tests. Combining them into one rental rate would hide the timing and make a competing lease difficult to compare.

Rent is USD 190,000 in advance and three months of rent is payable as a deposit on delivery. No other sums are then due. How many dollars must treasury release on delivery?

190,000 + 3 × 190,000 = USD 760,000. The deposit is additional cash even though it may be recoverable.

A deposit is fully refunded at maturity. Which incremental lease expense is still economically relevant?

  • The entire refunded principal
  • A second rental for the final month
  • The cost of funding cash while it is tied up

Funding has a time cost. Counting refunded principal as an expense would overstate cumulative cost.

Who funds the security?

When the owner accepts a letter of credit, a bank undertakes to pay against a complying demand. Review the bank's instrument as a separate promise, including expiry, drawing documents, currency and the bank's place for presentation. An agreement to accept the airline's existing bank means little if the bank refuses the required wording. Also inspect the airline's collateral arrangement with the bank. A letter fully backed by cash may release little working capital and still attract fees. A committed credit line may preserve cash but use borrowing capacity needed for another aircraft.

From the owner's seat, a deposit reduces exposure to arrears and transition costs. The owner may want to use it for several aircraft under related agreements. The airline may have funded security for a single tail. If the clause reaches obligations under other contracts, a dispute elsewhere can immobilise this aircraft's deposit. Define the particular contracts, the kind of breach and whether the owner can apply security immediately or only after notice and an opportunity to fix the breach. Once money is applied, check when the airline must restore the balance.

Seek a release mechanism tied to verifiable events. An airline with improving credit might propose reducing three months to two after twelve timely payments, provided there is no continuing specified default and a replacement letter remains in force. An owner financing its fleet may need its lender's consent. Establish who can grant the release and what evidence its credit committee accepts. Put an outside date on return of undisputed amounts and retain only a documented estimate for open claims. Price the value of the reduction against any increase in base rent.

Compare a fully refundable USD 600,000 deposit held for one year with an unsecured bank letter costing USD 12,000 for the same year. At a 5% simple annual cash funding cost, no other fees or interest, what is the saving in dollars from the letter?

Cash funding costs 600,000 × 0.05 = 30,000. Subtract the 12,000 bank fee to save 18,000.

A bank letter expires three months before lease end. The owner has agreed to accept letters from this bank. Which remaining issue affects continuity of security?

  • The airline's paint scheme
  • Renewal notice, replacement timing and a draw right on non-renewal
  • The initial price paid to the manufacturer

Approval of the issuer does not extend the instrument. An expiry gap is addressed in the lease and the letter.

How would you distinguish an expected deposit refund from money the airline can draw today?

Show the contractual refund test, date, counterparty and security status. Treasury still needs cash or bank capacity until release.

Trace the deposit if delivery falls through

In this hypothetical lease, treasury has wired USD 240,000 and posted a USD 90,000 bank letter. The owner must refund the cash and surrender the letter if the aircraft is lost before acceptance. The clause says when both are due after a missed delivery deadline, but gives no date in the separate loss provision. The bank keeps the full amount charged against the airline's credit line until it confirms cancellation.

The owner refunds USD 240,000 on Tuesday. What can treasury use on Wednesday if the bank still shows the letter as outstanding?

The refund restores USD 240,000 of cash. The USD 90,000 still consumes borrowing capacity. Ask the owner for the release the bank requires and get the bank's cancellation confirmation before counting that capacity. Put a deadline for both actions in the loss provision. The missed-delivery deadline applies to a different event.

Who pays when the aircraft stops flying?

A hell-or-high-water obligation makes scheduled payments continue despite listed disruptions. Examine its start point, scope and express exceptions. Suspending service due to an engine defect is an operating event. It does not itself discharge a promise to pay independent of aircraft condition. An express rent suspension, a contractual termination or a completed loss settlement may change the result. Identify which event switches off future payments and which amounts accrued first. Remedies for defects may run against a manufacturer under separate warranty rights. The timing of that claim can differ sharply from the next rent date.

Set-off is the deduction of a counterclaim from an amount otherwise payable. If the lease bars set-off, the airline may have to pay the invoice and pursue its claim separately. Suppose the owner owes an approved maintenance reimbursement equal to one month's rent. The cash forecast should still show gross rent on its due date unless the owner has agreed a credit or the terms permit netting. Collecting the reimbursement two months later leaves a financing gap even if the net position eventually closes. Confirm with counsel how mandatory law affects the particular promise.

A negotiation about this clause often starts in the wrong place. The owner has promised its financier a stream of payments and may be unable to offer a general abatement right. Ask instead for a delivery standard that prevents the initial defect, a direct warranty claim, a defined credit for owner-caused delay or a faster reimbursement timetable. Each targets a particular exposure. If another aircraft is available on better terms, document the timing and switching cost. A credible alternative gives weight to a narrowly priced request, especially if the owner faces an idle aircraft.

“Provided that no Default or Event of Default has occurred and is continuing”

These opening words qualify a payment back to the airline after compliant return or loss settlement in an SEC-filed lease. Follow the sentence through the notice mechanism and permitted deductions. A calendar entry saying “return deposit at lease end” omits several conditions. A separate reference to Default can matter if a breach has occurred but a period to cure has yet to expire. Read both definitions in that agreement. Filed aircraft lease, clauses 4.4(b) and 6.2, post-termination payments and security.

An airline has a valid damages claim. Rent is absolute after delivery and set-off is prohibited. No exception or mandatory rule applies. What position should treasury forecast?

  • Net rent less the damages estimate
  • Gross rent due and a separate potential recovery
  • No rent until the aircraft is flying

A counterclaim can have value without paying the current invoice. Estimate timing and collectibility separately.

Which evidence most directly supports a request for temporary rent relief on a particular tail?

  • The owner has other profitable leases
  • Another airline received a higher deposit refund
  • A signed amendment granting abatement for this defined grounding

A grounding alone may leave rent payable. The amendment addresses the specific payment and trigger.

On a multi-currency operation, confirm which desk carries the exchange exposure between ticket receipts and the date the lease payment clears. A right to send an equivalent amount in another currency can still leave a shortfall if the conversion bank deducts fees. Assign one person to verify net receipt and book the transfer early enough for both banking calendars. Tie that control to the event of default definition, including any special relief for a documented transfer-system problem.

The invoice falls on a day when the lessee's bank is open and the receiving bank is closed. What resolves the due date?

  • The aircraft's departure time
  • The lease definition of business day and payment adjustment convention
  • The opening time of the airline's head office

Both the calendar and the direction of adjustment may affect the last date for cleared funds.

Prepare the issues list

For each disputed amount, name the payor, recipient, currency, calculation, due date, bank-day adjustment and late consequence. Check the payment direction executed by the owner and any financier to whom rents are assigned. Independently authenticate account changes using an agreed process. A payment made to the wrong account may leave the lease obligation outstanding even when the airline acted promptly. The separate fraud loss should be visible in the scenario memo, alongside the risk of interest, cure costs and increased security.

Write an instruction that the negotiating team can execute. For example, retain the owner's unconditional basic rent in exchange for a documented deposit step-down after a credit test, separate treatment of disputed amounts and a fixed deadline to release the excess. Attach the cash comparison, the assumption on bank collateral and the board's peak funding limit. If the substitute letter consumes the same scarce capacity as cash, the proposed trade loses its rationale. If the owner will hold the full sum throughout the term, ask how much rent would have to fall to cover the incremental funding cost.

A two-aircraft group wants each deposit released when its own aircraft is returned. Which words deserve the closest attention?

  • The title of the return certificate
  • The definition of secured obligations and any cross-contract application right
  • The date the airframe first entered service

A broadly defined obligation can carry one tail's exposure into the other's release calculation.

The owner offers a deposit reduction but your bank demands cash collateral of the same amount for a replacement letter. What should the issues list show?

Recalculate working capital, fees and draw conditions. A formal reduction can leave the airline's net liquidity unchanged.

“Show me the first day we pay, the last day we pay and what has to happen before the security comes back.”

Key terms

Basic rent
Recurring scheduled consideration for possession during the lease.
Supplemental rent
Other amounts treated as rent under the defined scope of an agreement.
Security deposit
Cash delivered as security for specified obligations.
Letter of credit
Bank undertaking to pay against a demand satisfying its terms.
Draw
A demand for payment under a letter or an application of cash security.
Top-up
Restoring the required security after it has been used.
Abatement
A contractual reduction or suspension of rent.
Set-off
Applying a counterclaim against an amount otherwise due.
Hell-or-high-water
Payment covenant that continues despite specified disruptions.
Step-down
Reduction of required security when agreed conditions are satisfied.
