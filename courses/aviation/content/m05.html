Buy the used aircraft that is actually there

On a used sale, two aircraft of the same model can carry very different maintenance, records and title risks. Convert an inspection into a closing decision and specify exactly what escrow may release, to whom, and when.

Use the letter of intent to buy time and access

A seller offers a particular aircraft for sale. Before paying for a detailed inspection, the buyer wants access and confidence that the seller will keep the aircraft available. A letter of intent, or LOI, records the proposed commercial terms. Specify the aircraft and installed engines, expected price, deposit, inspection scope and location, intended closing date and whether the proposal assumes a lease already in place. If there is an existing lease, the buyer may acquire the lessor's position with an airline still in possession. That is a different transaction from buying a vacant aircraft ready to fly away.

Identify which promises the parties intend to bind them now. Confidentiality, cost allocation, deposit handling and exclusivity may operate before a definitive sale agreement is signed. Exclusivity prevents specified marketing or negotiations for a stated period. Counsel should confirm how the words and conduct work under the relevant law. A label such as “indicative” on the cover can sit awkwardly beside an unconditional commitment to pay a non-refundable deposit. Decide who may retain it, under which events and what happens if the parties simply fail to agree the definitive document.

Insist on access that can deliver an answer in the available time. Identify the inspection facility, qualified technical representatives, records format and working language, opening hours, authority for disassembly and who pays to put the aircraft back together after a rejected inspection. Plan for travel and a ferry flight if the aircraft needs to move to a specialist facility. The buyer may want the inspection period to start only after all material records arrive. The seller may accept that if the buyer supplies a checklist in advance and raises omissions promptly.

The LOI gives seven days of exclusivity starting at signature, but the seller may provide the technical records at any time. What is the buyer's first commercial risk?

  • The LOI automatically transfers ownership on day seven.
  • Exclusivity obliges the seller to pay all inspection costs.
  • The protected period may expire before the buyer has evidence to price the aircraft.

The promised time has little value without access. Tie delivery of the necessary materials to the timetable.

The seller asks for a larger deposit to take the aircraft off the market. Which combination could make that acceptable?

A clear inspection standard and decision window, a defined right to a prompt refund on rejection, and escrow instructions matching those rights. Test seller default and disagreement over a finding.

Turn condition into measurable tests

The purchase agreement should state the delivery condition, the physical and documentary standard to be met at handover. Break it into tests the team can actually perform. Match serial numbers for the airframe, installed engines and listed parts. Verify the inspection status, damage history, modifications, remaining service life and the records supporting them. A technical team may carry out a visual inspection, evaluate internal engine condition where permitted and review records of previous maintenance. Counsel can convert the team's findings into written conditions, cost allocations and rights when a test fails.

Airworthiness concerns satisfaction of the applicable requirements for safe and lawful flight. A buyer can require more than that minimum. It may need no major check falling due immediately after entry into service, a minimum remaining life on expensive parts, or complete traceable records. A part with a specified retirement limit needs evidence showing how much of that life has been used. Without that evidence, future use or resale may be constrained even if the engine starts and runs on the inspection day. State whether equivalent documents are acceptable and who decides.

Use a discrepancy sheet with an entry for each failed requirement. Record the contractual reference, inspection method, observation and evidence. Estimate the cost and downtime of cure, the deadline and proposed treatment. Some defects must be rectified before delivery. Others could support a price reduction if the buyer can perform the work efficiently after closing. Treat legal or safety requirements as real constraints. A price reduction does not make an aircraft airworthy or satisfy a lender's funding condition. Ask the appropriate technical or regulatory professional to decide whether the aircraft may move and under what authority.

The aircraft can fly legally today, but a life-limited part lacks the agreed evidence of remaining cycles. Which response fits the sale?

  • Test the records against the contractual condition and quantify replacement or evidence-reconstruction exposure.
  • Accept because serviceability today proves compliance with all delivery conditions.
  • Deduct the entire engine price without consulting the technical team.

A usable part and provable remaining life are different. The remedy and cost depend on the actual gap.

The seller offers a reduction equal to the repair shop's parts estimate. What else affects whether it is enough?

Labour, transport, downtime, lost programme coverage, financing effects and possible residual-value loss. Subtract any duplicated items or enforceable third-party recovery.

Read the inspection election and the default for silence

A pre-purchase inspection is the buyer's opportunity to examine condition before it is committed to accept. Does the buyer have broad discretion to reject, or only a right to identify failures against defined conditions? When does the election period begin? A draft can start the clock on completion of physical checks even though the written report arrives later. Identify whether acceptance may be conditional on specified repairs and whether the seller must carry them out. If the seller declines, does the buyer get to accept at an adjusted price, withdraw, recover its deposit, or pay for the return flight?

“Purchaser shall be deemed to have rejected the Aircraft”

Silence can end a transaction. Read the trigger, deadline, expense and refund provisions around this phrase in section 3.5 of the filed Global 5000 agreement. Another seller's draft could give silence the opposite consequence.

If the buyer elects to proceed with repairs, specify how the facility signs them off and who pays for repeat inspection or an evaluation flight. Preserve a route for a dispute about whether the repair meets the agreed standard. A named expert can decide a narrowly technical question if the contract sets out its remit, evidence, time and cost. Keep legal disputes about title and contract interpretation in the agreed dispute process. Do not let a minor repair disagreement lapse into an automatic purchase because the notice period continued running during discussions.

Read related aircraft together in a package. A right to reject one aircraft may affect the others, or the parties may require separate acceptances and separate allocations of price. If a single missing document is capable of stopping the whole acquisition, the effect on the buyer's fleet plan can dwarf the cost of replacing the document. Put a decision owner on the technical election and a separate owner on the closing notices. An inspector's email saying “looks good” may have none of the formal effect of the signed acceptance letter prescribed by the agreement.

The aircraft meets the defined airworthiness test, but the buyer dislikes its cabin finish. When could this still support rejection under the agreement?

  • Only if the seller agrees after closing.
  • When the buyer retains a timely discretionary inspection election broad enough to cover dissatisfaction.
  • Whenever the aircraft has been used before.

A subjective election and an objective delivery test allocate different rights. Check the standard and the deadline.

The seller verbally promises to repair a defect while a deemed-acceptance deadline approaches. What protects the buyer most directly?

  • Wait until the repair is finished to avoid appearing difficult.
  • Treat the repair facility as authorised to extend the deadline for both parties.
  • Serve the permitted conditional election or obtain a signed extension before the deadline.

Technical work does not automatically stop a contractual clock. Use the mechanism the agreement recognises.

Separate “as is, where is” from clean title

An as-is, where-is term generally allocates some condition and location risk to the buyer according to the wording and governing law. Its scope may incorporate exceptions for express delivery conditions, title promises or an agreed state at inspection. Read the provision with the rights that survive technical acceptance and closing. A buyer may agree to take hidden physical defects after an inspection while insisting on a bill of sale conveying good title free of specified interests. A promise about ownership and a promise about mechanical quality address different losses.

A lien search is one way to identify claims recorded against the asset. Ask counsel which registries and jurisdictions matter for this airframe, these engines, this seller and this location. Obtain the prior transfer documents needed to verify the chain of title. Search results should be brought up to date near closing, and each adverse finding should lead to an effective release, a permitted exception specifically accepted by the buyer, or a stop. Where assets were leased or engines swapped, trace who actually owns each one. The seller cannot cure another owner's claim merely by signing a second description of the asset.

Distinguish buying an aircraft subject to a lease from taking on unknown claims. If the buyer expects the airline to stay in possession, review rent allocation, deposits, security, maintenance balances, defaults, consents, notices and transfer of the lessor's rights and obligations. One invoice dated after closing may relate to pre-closing use. Agree the economic cut-off and adjustment mechanism. If vacant delivery is required, verify return of possession and termination or release of existing rights before payment. Choosing the wrong transaction type can leave the buyer owning an asset it has no immediate right to operate.

The buyer accepts a used aircraft as is after inspection. The seller separately promises title free of liens except one named lease. What belongs in title diligence?

  • Investigate other interests, obtain releases and verify the named lease and transfer terms.
  • Ignore all liens because physical condition has been accepted.
  • Demand surrender of the named lease regardless of the sale structure.

The exception permits one identified interest. A condition disclaimer alone does not settle competing title claims.

Close the three gates together

The technical team confirms condition and records. The legal team checks title and release documents. Treasury confirms cleared funds. Escrow then executes agreed instructions. A signed but undelivered bill of sale held to order can be part of the package. Identify who may date and release it, when risk transfers and which registry filings must be made in what sequence. Where a lender requires payment before releasing its security, counsel can coordinate its conditional release with a payoff instruction. A draft checklist that says “all simultaneously” still needs an authorised person to perform each step and evidence of the result.

Suppose the price is USD 12 million. The buyer has already placed USD 0.6 million with escrow. At closing it adds USD 11.4 million. Escrow pays USD 5 million to release a seller mortgage and USD 7 million to the seller, ignoring fees and other adjustments. The seller's debt is paid out of the sale proceeds. Requiring the buyer to add USD 5 million to the price as well would count the same obligation twice. Check the direction of each wire and the scope of release against the payoff letter's validity date and any daily interest accrual.

Agreed price is USD 8 million, reduced by a signed USD 0.25 million condition adjustment. A USD 0.4 million escrow deposit applies in full. How many USD millions does the buyer still have to fund, ignoring fees?

8 − 0.25 − 0.4 = 7.35. The deposit is credited against the reduced price.

Escrow holds the entire adjusted price of USD 9 million. A USD 3.2 million seller lien payoff and USD 0.1 million seller closing costs are due out of it. What USD millions are released to the seller?

9 − 3.2 − 0.1 = 5.7. Escrow pays the lender and costs from the gross proceeds.

The aircraft might be damaged between inspection and closing. Read who must maintain it, permitted use and hour limits, repair thresholds, notice obligations and the buyer's election after damage. Even a repaired aircraft may carry a damage history affecting resale value. A mechanism limited to the cost of physical repair could leave that value loss with the buyer. Ask whether the buyer can order a repeat inspection, seek a defined adjustment or decline delivery, and whether seller insurance covers the aircraft until contractual risk passes. Verify the buyer's policy attaches at the handover.

At closing the seller tenders a clean bill of sale, but its lender has only promised informally to release a registered interest next week. The client requires clean title on payment. Which instruction follows?

  • Treat the lender's email as a substitute for every required discharge.
  • Hold release until counsel verifies an effective payoff and release mechanism satisfying the client's condition.
  • Pay because a clean bill of sale makes the lender's interest disappear.

A promise from the seller may give a claim for breach while leaving a competing claim outstanding. Match the release to the funding trigger.

What could make a material damage claim after inspection difficult even if the aircraft is repaired?

A waiver in unconditional acceptance, an expired election period, inadequate baseline evidence, or an allocation limited to repair cost. Record condition and preserve the specific post-inspection protection.

Set the buyer's instruction before price release

A clean decision states which concessions can replace physical cure and which issues bar closing. Assume the airline can schedule a nonessential cabin repair during an existing maintenance visit. A price allowance supported by a firm quote, a records update and an adequate margin for overruns may work. If the same defect prevents the required ferry authorisation or violates a lender condition, the same price allowance fails. Obtain the operational and financing sign-offs before advising that a cheaper aircraft is deliverable.

Prepare a closing record including signed documents, releases, maintenance status at handover, evidence of time and location, wire confirmations, registry filing receipts and the exact exceptions reserved. Assign responsibility for outstanding post-closing filings and agreed repairs. A cheap aircraft with incomplete evidence can remain expensive long after it has left the seller's hangar. The buyer's best negotiating moment is often while it can still withhold a properly conditioned payment. Once the wire and unconditional acceptance are released, the buyer may have to fund a repair or pursue a contested claim while keeping its route operating.

A repair allowance covers the quoted work exactly. The lender conditions funding on that work being completed before delivery. What is still required to close on the proposed allowance?

  • Only a revised invoice reflecting the allowance.
  • A longer seller warranty on unrelated components.
  • Valid lender consent or another funded, contractually permitted route, plus operational clearance where required.

The allowance addresses cost. It leaves the lender's funding condition unresolved.

Key terms

Letter of intent
A preliminary record of proposed terms that may also contain binding commitments.
Exclusivity
An agreed restriction on specified dealings with others for a defined time.
Pre-purchase inspection
The buyer's technical and records review under the sale terms.
Delivery condition
The contractual standard the aircraft and its records must meet at handover.
Airworthiness
Compliance with the applicable requirements for safe and lawful flight.
As is, where is
A contractual allocation of condition and location risk, subject to its wording and exceptions.
Chain of title
The sequence of transfers supporting ownership of the asset.
Payoff letter
A lender's statement of the amount and terms required to discharge the specified obligation and obtain its release.
Permitted interest
A claim or right expressly allowed to remain under the agreed title standard.
Conditional technical acceptance
An election to proceed subject to identified corrections or other agreed conditions.
