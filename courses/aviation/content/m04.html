When the aircraft is late or defective

A missed delivery can leave an airline paying to cover scheduled flights. Work out which days qualify for relief, how much the contract will pay and when the buyer may exit. Then run the same exercise on a defect that appears after delivery.

Classify the cause before counting the days

An airline may describe every late delivery as a manufacturer failure. A purchase agreement usually distinguishes causes. An excusable delay is one for which the contract grants specified relief. Force majeure is a contractual or legal basis for relief from performance in defined circumstances, often involving events beyond a party's control. The label has little value on its own. Under this contract, does the event extend the delivery date, suspend damages, permit termination, or do all three at different times? A non-excusable or inexcusable delay falls outside the relief available under the applicable wording.

Build a factual timeline before attaching a legal conclusion. Mark the binding delivery obligation, the first day performance was affected, the work that could still continue, when the impediment ended and when the seller could reasonably have tendered the aircraft. A supplier's factory closure might fit the listed events but make no difference to delivery if the buyer's late selections were already holding up the critical task. The contract and governing law may treat overlapping causes differently. Record overlapping days once, state the assumptions and ask counsel to confirm the allocation.

Look for conditions attached to relief. The manufacturer might have to notify the buyer promptly, estimate the impact and take reasonable steps to reduce it. Decide what evidence the buyer needs, who can ask for an update and whether failure to give timely notice actually loses the relief. If the text is silent, leave the legal effect as an open question. A supplier email confirming disruption does not prove that a substitute part could not have been sourced or that the eventual number of days claimed was unavoidable.

“Seller shall promptly notify Buyer in writing”

The notice requirement accompanies an excusable delay mechanism. Article 8.2 of the filed Bombardier purchase agreement also addresses detail, expected impact and mitigation.

A strike affects one supplier for twelve days. Other work can continue and the aircraft is delivered four days late. Which question best tests a twelve-day extension claim?

  • Which contractual trigger and causal path connect the strike to the claimed extension?
  • Can the seller prove the supplier employs union members?
  • Does the airline have a strike clause in its contracts with passengers?

An event and its impact on required performance are separate facts. The agreed extension rule controls the measure.

A buyer wants immediate repayment of all advances whenever any external event occurs. What makes a narrower fallback commercially intelligible?

The seller can continue a recoverable programme for an agreed period. The buyer gets progress reports and a defined exit if a long-stop is reached, with a funded refund route.

Calculate the delay payment as written

Liquidated damages, often abbreviated LDs, are an amount or formula agreed in advance for a specified failure. They can reduce argument over how to measure the buyer's loss. Enforceability and characterisation depend on governing law, so ask counsel to assess the actual drafting. Read four variables before estimating recovery. What is the starting event? Does a grace period exclude early days? Is the rate per day or per week, and how are partial units treated? Is there a cap, a maximum payment for one aircraft or for the order as a whole?

In a teaching example, count only eligible calendar days of delay. The first five earn nothing. Each additional day earns USD 10,000 up to a USD 100,000 cap per aircraft. At day six the amount is USD 10,000. At day fifteen it reaches the cap. At day twenty-three it remains USD 100,000. These assumptions create the curve below. A different clause might make a payment retroactive to day one once the threshold is crossed. Read the words between the threshold and the payment formula before selecting the shape for your chart.

Continue the example. The airline incurs documented incremental replacement costs of USD 12,000 for each of twenty-three days. The gross cost is USD 276,000. Assume the allowed LD payment is the sole collectible recovery, pays in cash at once and requires no proof beyond the qualifying delay. After recovery, the buyer still bears USD 176,000 of cover costs. A cap of USD 100,000 is a ceiling on that specific payment. It says nothing about the airline's total loss. If the payment arrives as a restricted service credit, its cash-equivalent value might be lower. Count the credit only to the extent the airline can use it.

A teaching clause pays USD 7,500 per eligible day after the first four, capped at USD 48,000. There are twelve eligible days. How many USD does the clause pay?

Eight payable days at 7,500 would produce 60,000. The cap reduces payment to 48,000.

An airline incurs USD 225,000 incremental cover cost. Its only recovery is a USD 90,000 parts credit of which it can use 60% at full economic value. What USD loss does it retain in this scenario, ignoring discounting?

Usable recovery is 90,000 × 60% = 54,000. Retained loss is 225,000 − 54,000 = 171,000.

A daily payment is capped “in aggregate for all affected Aircraft.” Two aircraft are late on separate dates. What is the safe modelling choice?

  • Ignore the cap until all aircraft arrive on the same day.
  • Track cumulative claims against the shared cap and verify its scope.
  • Reset the cap at every delivery because each aircraft has its own serial number.

An aggregate cap can be consumed by a first claim, reducing the amount available for the next.

Find the exit, the election and the refund

A long-stop is an outside limit that can trigger a right to exit or another remedy. It might run from the end of the delivery period, a revised month, or notice that delay will exceed a threshold. Define what must remain undone at that time. A clause tied to failure to present an aircraft for inspection can stop running before formal delivery. In a real agreement the buyer and seller can have different thresholds for the same excusable disruption. Read both parties' rights. If either can terminate, a manufacturer could exercise a later right just when the airline still wants to wait for a scarce slot.

Termination may require an election, an affirmative decision communicated in a stated form and time. A right exercisable within five business days after a threshold can expire even while delay continues. Capture who must send the notice, the address, when receipt counts, applicable business-day calendar and whether a renegotiation step comes first. Check whether termination concerns only the affected aircraft or other positions, training and spares. A fleet buyer relying on uniform equipment may lose economies of scale after one cancellation, but its contract may leave unaffected aircraft orders firmly in place.

Prepare two cash forecasts. In the wait case, model cover aircraft, financing and the revised service date. In the exit case, model the return of PDPs, interest if provided, substitute purchase or lease price, delay to the substitute and availability of crew training. A refund restores money already paid. Crediting that returned principal as an additional profit or as compensation for all lost time overstates the benefit of cancellation. If a seller asks the buyer to elect early on the basis of a forecast, ask whether a binding revised date and refund protection can support a decision to wait.

Read the filed initial provisioning letter 3194-01, article 8. Assume the airline validly terminates one aircraft under article 7 of the incorporated aircraft terms. It has also ordered initial spares for that aircraft. Some are on the shelf and the rest are due to ship. Decide which purchase orders the exit team can close and what it needs to do today.

Does sending the aircraft termination notice close the spares orders?

The letter gives the customer a further election. The manufacturer must receive the written request within 15 days after termination. Match each item to initial provisioning under this letter for the terminated aircraft and check delivery status when the request arrives. Items already delivered fall outside this discharge. A shipment delivered between aircraft termination and receipt of the request can change what the buyer owes. Put proof of receipt and the item-level reconciliation in the exit file.

The clause permits the buyer to cancel if the aircraft has not been presented for inspection by a long-stop. It was conformingly presented before the long-stop and delivery is still pending. Which conclusion follows from this fact alone?

  • Cancellation is automatic because delivery is incomplete.
  • The buyer must waive every remaining defect claim.
  • That specific presentation-based trigger has not occurred. Check separate delivery rights.

Presentation and delivery are distinct events. Read the remedy linked to the event that actually failed.

If prompt notice is missing, should the model assume full LD recovery?

First determine which party owed which notice, what consequence the agreement states, actual prejudice if relevant, and the governing law. Show the disputed recovery as a scenario.

Check which remedies can be combined

An exclusive remedy limits the available responses to a specified failure. The agreement may make delay payments the sole financial remedy while preserving a long-stop termination right. A broad release might instead settle all claims once a buyer accepts revised delivery. Read the LD clause, termination provisions, liability exclusions, caps and amendment together. Do not put both full reimbursement of replacement lift and full LDs in the base case unless the text and applicable law support recovery of both. The operational loss does not disappear because the contract offers a limited remedy for it.

Study the buyer's own default as carefully as seller delay. The OEM may have a right to terminate, retain a specified sum from advances, recover additional amounts or remarket the production slot if the buyer fails to pay or wrongfully refuses a conforming tender. Find the grace or cure period, who gives notice and whether it applies to this default. Do the agreed damages consume all advances or only a defined amount? If the manufacturer keeps a deposit, verify whether the buyer remains liable for any shortfall. The two sides may use the same term “liquidated damages” for completely different failures.

The seller may retain a fixed percentage as its sole remedy for buyer default. The issues list labels that number “buyer delay recovery.” What is wrong?

  • It reverses the beneficiary and default. Build separate seller-delay and buyer-default entries.
  • A percentage cannot ever be an agreed remedy.
  • Any amount described as damages must be payable to the buyer.

Checking the actor, failure and beneficiary prevents the model from using the opposite party's protection.

Run a defect through the warranty

A warranty is a promise about a condition or performance with defined consequences if it fails. After delivery, identify the covered item, responsible warrantor and period. The aircraft manufacturer may cover the airframe and its own installed items while suppliers give separate warranties on engines, equipment or components. A pass-through right lets the buyer benefit from a supplier warranty under its stated conditions. It may require assignment, direct registration or claims through the manufacturer. A long coverage period has little value if the airline cannot establish the required inspection history or submit an eligible claim.

Consider a part that fails during the stated warranty period. Check whether the period runs from aircraft delivery, installation, hours, cycles or whichever limit occurs first. A cycle generally consists of a takeoff and landing for tracking use, subject to the applicable definition. Distinguish discovery of a defect from the deadline to notify it, and from the deadline to return the failed part. List required evidence, shipping instructions, approved repair location and whether the warrantor chooses repair, replacement or credit. The airline may still have to pay removal, installation, freight, customs and substitute capacity.

Assign each uncovered cost to the right party. If the manufacturer supplies a replacement component free of charge and no cover for downtime, the airline retains the economic cost of the grounded aircraft. A general exclusion of indirect or consequential loss may affect some claims. Labels and their effect depend on the text and law, so identify each cost specifically. Read whether the repair restarts a full warranty period or covers only the balance of the original term. Check who can claim after a financing sale and whether maintenance by another approved facility changes eligibility.

An engine component fails in the coverage period. The OEM explicitly passes through the engine supplier's warranty and gives no direct engine warranty. What should the airline obtain first?

  • A copy of the paint warranty.
  • The applicable supplier terms, claimant eligibility, deadlines and transfer documents.
  • An estimate assuming the OEM will reimburse every route cancellation.

A pass-through can have different procedures and remedies. Coverage of the part alone would not establish downtime recovery.

A replacement part is available at no charge, but it will arrive in three weeks. What is missing from an estimate showing zero buyer loss?

The cost of operating without the aircraft, repair labour, movement of parts and any costs the warranty leaves with the airline. Deduct another recovery only if it applies to that loss.

Make product support deliverable

A product support package can include technical representatives, maintenance information, spare parts access and training. A promise to “assist” needs an operational test. What support is available, in which location, from when, during which hours, at whose cost and for how long? An adviser may have no power to approve a repair or bind the manufacturer. If a service credit is the offered remedy, model the permitted use and expiry. The buyer can sometimes obtain extra training capacity, an earlier spares allocation or a longer credit window more readily than a broader warranty.

End the review with one row per failure mode. For late delivery, identify cause, eligible days, cap, exit date and refundable cash. For a post-delivery defect, list cost to repair, costs excluded, deadlines and who can actually pay. For unavailable support, identify the date it affects operations and the value of a substitute. Write a trade the buyer could accept, such as taking a lower daily cap in exchange for a protected refund and an earlier exit that aligns with a real alternative aircraft. Test that alternative with the fleet team before giving up a remedy.

A warranty reimburses qualifying parts and explicitly excludes labour and service interruption. Which entry correctly models a covered failure?

  • Set retained loss to zero because the failure is covered.
  • Deduct the warranty's total face amount regardless of the price of the part.
  • Deduct the collectible parts recovery from the gross scenario loss, including uncovered labour and interruption.

The covered failure can produce uncovered costs. Measure the recovery against the specified heads of loss.

The buyer may accept an earlier termination right in place of higher LDs. Which fact matters most to the value of that exchange?

  • Whether an alternative aircraft and an enforceable, timely PDP refund are available at the exit date.
  • Whether the word “termination” is capitalised.
  • Whether the OEM's headquarters are closer to the buyer.

Exit is economically useful when it permits a better funded alternative. A right with no viable replacement can be worth less than predictable compensation.

Key terms

Excusable delay
A delay qualifying for specified relief under the agreement.
Force majeure
A basis for relief tied to defined events and the applicable law.
Liquidated damages
An agreed amount or formula for a specified failure.
Grace period
The initial interval during which a stated remedy does not accrue or become available.
Long-stop
An outside limit linked to an election or remedy.
Exclusive remedy
The agreed response or set of responses allowed for a defined failure.
Cure
Correction of a default within an allowed period.
Warranty
A promise covering specified facts, defects or performance on stated terms.
Pass-through warranty
A supplier warranty made available to another party subject to its original and transfer terms.
Cycle
A unit of aircraft use generally tracking one takeoff and landing.
