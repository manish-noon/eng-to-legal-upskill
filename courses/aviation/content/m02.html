What exactly did you buy, and at what price?

A delivery invoice can follow the contract exactly and still surprise the buyer. Rebuild it from the signed configuration, price date, adjustment formula and usable credits. Then identify which changes the manufacturer can make without fresh consent.

Buy the configuration you can operate

A production slot reserves a place in a build schedule. It does not tell you which seats, engines, software, communications equipment or cabin layout will arrive. The specification is the agreed technical description. Find its issue number and date, the document that incorporates it and the list of deviations. Put the operational requirement beside the promise. If the buyer needs a particular communications capability for a route, a schedule listing the physical equipment may leave installation, software activation or certification unresolved. Ask what evidence at delivery will show that the capability works in the intended configuration.

Build a small comparison table with four columns. Put the signed baseline in the first, an approved change in the second, the resulting configuration in the third and the delivery test in the fourth. Work at a level the technical team can check. “Premium cabin” is too broad. A specified seat model, layout drawing and revision, installed quantity and test protocol give the inspector something to verify. Ask who supplies the drawings and by when. A conflict between a marketing rendering and the signed specification should become an evidence request before the buyer's choice window closes.

Distinguish an option, something offered at an extra price, from equipment already included in the base aircraft. Identify buyer-furnished equipment, items procured by the buyer and supplied to the manufacturer for installation. The OEM may promise to integrate the item only if it arrives on time, fits the agreed interfaces and comes with the necessary documentation. Allocate responsibility for late delivery, incompatibility and failed tests. Someone must instruct the equipment supplier and pay the resulting invoice. The purchase agreement alone may give the airline little leverage against a supplier it has failed to contract with directly.

The brochure promises cabin connectivity. The signed schedule identifies antenna hardware only. Which acceptance test best addresses the buyer's business requirement?

  • Counting antennas after delivery.
  • Recording an expectation of connectivity in the internal budget.
  • An agreed end-to-end service test with dependencies and certification responsibilities specified.

The buyer needs an agreed operational capability. Hardware count proves installation and leaves activation and integration unresolved.

If the buyer supplies the seats, who owns the problem when they arrive late?

Trace the promise to procure, the interface date, available relief for the manufacturer and any recovery under the separate seat order. The two contracts can leave the buyer bearing a gap.

Control changes after specification freeze

The freeze date is the deadline after which an ordinary selection becomes a change to an agreed build. A change request should describe the new configuration, whether the existing selection is cancelled, the added or removed cost and the effect on delivery. Work already performed can have a removal cost. A component ordered specially for this aircraft may be unusable on another. If a request says “replace,” the price schedule still needs to show the treatment of the original item. Check whether a deposit for the change counts toward its eventual price.

A manufacturer may seek unilateral rights to alter the design as production develops or a regulatory requirement changes. A general promise of “equivalent” equipment leaves a question about what counts as equivalent. Two components can perform the same function yet require different spares, crew training or maintenance tools. Ask which dimensions must stay within tolerance, who measures them and who bears integration cost. A right to give notice after installing a substitute is quite different from a right for the buyer to approve an adverse change before installation. In a crowded production schedule, asking for a veto over every small engineering change may waste bargaining power needed for changes that affect operation.

“interchangeability or replaceability requirements of the Specification”

A change can affect common spares even when it leaves the aircraft's headline performance intact. See the specification change mechanism in the filed A330 purchase agreement, clause 2.

Treat supplier substitutions, mandatory regulatory changes and discretionary buyer requests separately. For each, state who initiates it, which party can say no, who pays, whether schedule relief follows and what happens if agreement never comes. Insist on an updated configuration list that shows effectivity, meaning the specific aircraft or serial numbers affected. A change agreed for the last aircraft in a series can enter the wrong cost model if the spreadsheet applies it across the order. Save the signed approval and the schedule it replaced.

The manufacturer holds the pen when its team prepares the next draft. If its proposed option cannot be built in your slot, ask engineering to identify the last workable decision date and the configuration that will go down the line if you decline. Give your negotiator a choice it can actually sign. A later slot might buy time for a different interior but cost a season of flying.

Read Open Configuration Matters, letter 3194-02, sections 1.2 and 2.2–2.4. The airline accepts an option proposal. Its finance team adds the full option price to the aircraft budget. Identify two records that still need changing and test the budget entry.

Which documents and amounts would you put in the sign-off pack?

Amend Exhibit A in writing to record the agreed option. Track the revised performance guarantees due from the manufacturer within 90 days of acceptance and incorporate those revisions by written amendment. Both the aircraft basic price and the advance payment base price already include an estimate. Adjust each by the difference between estimate and actual price. Adding the entire option price would count the estimate twice. The filed estimate is redacted, so the filing alone cannot supply the adjustment.

The manufacturer offers a substitute unit with identical performance and weight. The airline maintains all its other aircraft using a different spare. Which missing criterion matters most?

  • Interchangeability with existing spares and the resulting support burden.
  • Whether the supplier has an attractive brochure.
  • Whether the airline already approved the aircraft paint scheme.

Fleet standardisation can have value even where the aircraft's performance is unchanged.

A change request adds a USD 400,000 interior for aircraft 3 only. Which entry should the buyer make before approving it?

  • Assume the price covers removal because the request says “replace.”
  • Record effectivity, the treatment of replaced work, the net price and the agreed schedule effect.
  • Add USD 400,000 to every aircraft with the same model name.

The same model can have different configurations. A gross option price leaves the cost of removal or a credit for the displaced option unresolved.

Turn a base price into a delivery price

The base price is a price at a specified reference date and configuration. Escalation adjusts defined portions of it using a contractual formula. Find the base month, reference indices, component weights, lag between the measurement month and delivery, any minimum or maximum, and the rounding rule. An index is a published numerical measure selected by the parties. The contract can tell you to use an earlier month's value because the delivery month's figure is not yet available. Read any rule for revised or discontinued index data. Put the formula and its source series beside the invoice rather than accepting a percentage stated in an email.

Start with a deliberately simple example in USD millions. A base price of 20 rises by five percent, adding 1. An option priced in delivery-date dollars adds 2. A credit expressly deductible from this invoice subtracts 1. The resulting aircraft price is 22. If the option is itself subject to escalation, repeat the calculation using the option's own base date and formula. If the buyer has already paid part of the price, subtract those advances only after calculating the agreed final price. An advance changes the amount still due. It does not make the aircraft cheaper.

For a weighted formula, calculate ratios before adding components. Suppose a price factor equals 0.4 times the current labour index divided by its base value plus 0.6 times the equivalent materials ratio. If labour rises ten percent and materials are flat, the factor is 0.4 times 1.10 plus 0.6 times 1.00, or 1.04. Apply it once to the covered amount. A quoted four percent increase in that amount can mean a smaller percentage increase in the whole invoice if part of the aircraft price is fixed. Record calculations at the precision in the agreement before rounding the invoice.

A hypothetical aircraft has a USD 30 million escalating base. Labour carries 25% weight and rises 8%. Materials carry 75% and rise 4%. What is the escalation increment in USD millions?

The weighted change is 0.25 × 8% + 0.75 × 4% = 5%. Apply it to 30 to obtain 1.5.

The base is USD 18 million at the reference date. Escalation adds 4% on that base only. A fixed USD 1.3 million option and USD 0.2 million invoice credit apply. What is the adjusted price in USD millions before advances?

18 × 1.04 + 1.3 − 0.2 = 19.82.

A cap says “3% per year.” Can you put 3% in the model for an aircraft due in three years?

Read the dates, whether annual increases compound, any catch-up and whether the cap applies to the final price or a particular component. Model the contractual sequence.

Identify who pays for a later delivery

An aircraft due in June is delivered in November. If the formula uses the actual delivery month, later delivery can change the invoice even though the buyer never changed the specification. Study the rules for seller delay, buyer delay and delay outside both parties' control. Look for a reference month held at scheduled delivery in one case but moved to actual delivery in another. On a falling index the scheduled-month rule might favour the seller. Check for an escalation floor, which prevents the adjustment falling below a defined level. Ask for the input values and compare both months before assigning blame or a dollar amount.

Price protection can take several forms. The seller may fix the price of a defined option for a limited period. The buyer may seek a maximum cumulative increase before it can cancel. Another bargain might switch to a fixed delivery month in return for timely buyer configuration choices. Test each change against the actual timetable. If the buyer intends to request a cabin redesign after the freeze, a promise tied to meeting all selection dates may offer little protection. Price the concession in the scenario where the buyer can actually comply.

A contract freezes the base aircraft escalation date for seller delay. A separate option letter indexes the cabin equipment to actual delivery. What belongs in the forecast?

  • Freeze both prices because the aircraft is delivered once.
  • Use actual delivery for both because that is when the invoice is issued.
  • Separate measurements unless an agreed precedence or amendment changes the option rule.

The option letter can allocate a different exposure. Reconcile the scope of the freeze before applying it to the option.

Value a credit the buyer can use

A credit memorandum records an entitlement to offset a specified future charge or receive a benefit. Read the permitted products and services, expiry, assignment restrictions, eligible group companies and whether the buyer must pay cash first. Suppose the OEM offers USD 1 million in parts credit. The buyer expects to purchase only USD 400,000 of eligible parts before expiry and cannot transfer the balance. Ignoring tax and time value its maximum economic benefit on that forecast is USD 400,000. If the airline would otherwise buy equivalent parts more cheaply elsewhere, even that estimate needs adjusting.

A training package requires the same discipline. Count available places, eligible courses, notice for booking or cancellation, language, location and costs borne by the buyer. An offered simulator slot after the aircraft enters service can leave the airline paying for outside training now. Read what happens if the manufacturer postpones a course and what happens if the buyer's nominated crew fail entry requirements. Training capacity, service bulletins, technical publications and spare-parts access can affect whether a delivered aircraft is commercially usable on day one. Put commencement, duration and support contacts in an implementation list.

Separate an invoice credit already reflected in price from a future conditional benefit. Deducting it from the closing payment without contractual authority could put the buyer in payment default. Counting it as both an invoice reduction and future savings gives the credit twice the value it can deliver. Where the agreement uses credits as compensation for delay, estimate the amount the buyer can absorb within the permitted period. A manufacturer may be more willing to extend the expiry or broaden eligible services than to pay cash. Compare these alternatives using the airline's actual procurement plan.

For an offer that expires, ask what the deadline measures. A part ordered before expiry might ship afterward. Procurement can count that purchase only if the letter makes the order date sufficient. Ask finance to test a sample invoice against the named purchaser, eligible items and redemption event before it puts the credit into the fleet budget.

A USD 500,000 spares credit expires in a year. The buyer can use USD 120,000 by then and cannot transfer it. What is the maximum undiscounted benefit on that forecast?

  • USD 120,000
  • USD 500,000
  • USD 380,000

Only eligible expenditure can absorb the credit. The unused USD 380,000 is the shortfall against its face value.

The credit is transferable to “Customer Affiliates.” The operator will be an independently owned airline. What changes in the model?

Confirm the contractual definition of affiliate, obtain consent or remove that airline's purchases from eligible spend.

Write a price instruction that can be tested

Give the commercial lead a bridge from signed base price to cash due at delivery. Attach the dated specification, authorised change register, index observations, side letters and advance receipts. Mark any inaccessible or redacted inputs as unknown. For a precedent, it is legitimate to say the filed form uses an adjustment mechanism when the coefficient or commercial concession is hidden. It would be misleading to offer a precise expected delivery price using a coefficient from another customer's agreement. Use a sensitivity case with a clearly labelled assumption until the missing input can be verified.

Put the largest negotiable exposures first. For a scarce, early slot the OEM may have little incentive to grant a blanket cap. The buyer can still ask to measure escalation at the agreed month if the OEM alone delays delivery, or exchange a firm selection deadline for a fixed-price option. Describe the alternative slot, the deadline and the cost of delay that make this trade credible. The issues list should state which signed document implements each concession and who will check the final invoice against it. That last step turns the negotiated term into money the buyer can actually retain.

An escalation coefficient is hidden in a filing. An earlier order on the same model reveals a coefficient. What may you conclude?

  • Treat the hidden coefficient as zero until challenged.
  • The visible contract shows a possible mechanism. Mark the hidden coefficient unknown and test assumptions separately.
  • Use the earlier coefficient as the signed term.

A redaction does not reveal a number. A different agreement may show a plausible scenario without proving this buyer's deal.

A credit memo pays only for eligible future training, but treasury wants to deduct it from the closing wire. Which issue needs resolution before sending funds?

  • Whether the aircraft's residual value exceeds the credit.
  • Whether training appears in the marketing deck.
  • Whether the documents allow set-off against that invoice or provide a separate entitlement.

A restricted entitlement may leave the entire closing invoice payable. A short wire can have contractual consequences.

Key terms

Specification
The agreed technical description of the aircraft and its configuration.
Effectivity
The aircraft or serial numbers to which a change applies.
Buyer-furnished equipment
Equipment procured by the buyer for integration into the aircraft.
Freeze date
The point after which changes are subject to the agreed change procedure.
Escalation
Adjustment of a specified price component under the contractual formula.
Index ratio
The current contractual index observation divided by its base observation.
Floor
A lower bound on a price or adjustment.
Credit memorandum
A documented credit usable under its stated conditions.
Set-off
Application of a claim or credit against an amount otherwise due, subject to the contract and law.
Delivery-date dollars
An amount already expressed for the delivery date rather than the earlier base date.
