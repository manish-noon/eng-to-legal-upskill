Make the sign, negotiate or walk call

After this module you can turn landed terms into a priced recommendation, identify the maximum payable price, and give a client an instruction it can execute at the negotiating table.

Establish what the client is buying

Start with the alternative. A purchase may replace an existing aircraft, add capacity for a new route or provide an asset to lease out. An airline comparing an operating lease with buying needs both alternatives on the same footing. Match aircraft capability, usable service period, expected utilisation and end condition. Define when operations can actually begin. If one aircraft requires a cabin change and crew training first, its headline price is only part of the cost. If a lease avoids residual sale risk, avoid assuming the airline owns a valuable aircraft at the end.

Use a value ceiling that belongs to the client. One way to derive it is to value future cash flows from operating the aircraft, taking account of the alternative the client can actually use. Another is to use an approved budget for equivalent capacity. Ask whether the number includes maintenance, financing, transaction costs or risk allowances already. Ask the finance team to sign off the currency and valuation date. Present value converts future cash to a common earlier date using an agreed discount rate. A deposit today and a potential refund years from now can have the same nominal amount and different economic values.

This module's worked purchase uses invented figures throughout. An aircraft is available for $8 million. Closing costs are $120,000. The client's value ceiling is $8.25 million before those costs and the specific unresolved risk described below. All amounts are already on the same valuation date and currency. Title clearance and available cash are separate mandatory conditions. Writing these assumptions first prevents a favourable expected value from quietly becoming authority to take a risk the client expressly prohibited.

An airline compares five years of rent with the full price of an aircraft that it can sell after five years. Which adjustment matters?

  • Add the purchase price to the rent to make both cases comparable.
  • Use a common operating horizon and value the owned aircraft's residual proceeds and costs at that horizon.
  • Count the leased aircraft's resale value as the airline's asset in both cases.

The two alternatives deliver different assets and obligations at year five. Compare their net cash flows and respective return or sale costs on one valuation basis.

Build a loss register from the issues list

A loss register is a short table of events, their financial consequences and the protection actually available. One line might be “late delivery forces a temporary replacement”. Another could be “an engine has fewer usable cycles at return than promised”. For each event state the time horizon, probability, gross loss, expected recovery conditional on that event, and owner of the estimate. Define what counts as the event. A delay past the scheduled date and a delay past the end of a contractual grace period have different probabilities. Use a repair estimate from engineering, an actual substitute quote from procurement, and a reviewed contract clause for the remedy.

Read the remedy from trigger to collection. Does it apply on these facts? Is the loss eligible? What does a deductible remove? Does a cap limit one claim, one aircraft or the whole order? Is timely notice needed? Does the counterparty have money, must the airline spend money on parts to use a credit, and could the credit expire first? Answer those questions before recording recoverable value. The largest face amount on a page can be far above the amount that can be collected on this scenario.

Illustrative remedy. Buyer may apply the credit to eligible spares ordered before the first anniversary of delivery.

If the buyer expects to need only $40,000 of eligible spares in the window, a $100,000 non-transferable credit cannot be valued at face solely because the manufacturer remains solvent. Check whether the buyer could shift planned purchases without raising their cost, whether the credit stacks with existing discounts and whether a later aircraft in the fleet can use it. Estimate the avoided net spend on purchases it would otherwise make. Distinguish that value from cash in an escrow account already available for release.

A model prices every possible delay as excusable and also assigns a separate probability to the same supplier interruption under the manufacturer's breach. Where is the error?

Define disjoint paths for the same incident and assign probabilities to those paths. Recoveries under alternative legal characterisations cannot both apply to a single loss unless the agreement actually permits them.

A loss is $300,000. Cash recovery is capped at $200,000. A $100,000 spares credit is an alternative to the cash remedy for the same claim. If the cash is fully collectible, what is the best supported residual loss?

  • Zero, by stacking the cash and credit.
  • $100,000 after taking the cash remedy.
  • $200,000 because the credit is smaller.

Choose the available alternative with the higher economic value. The contract forbids adding both to recover the same loss.

Calculate kept risk and headroom

Kept risk here means the expected financial loss left with the client under the package. For each scenario, take gross loss less the collectible recovery, floored at zero, then multiply by the probability of that scenario. If several outcomes could happen at once, check for duplicate loss components and interactions such as a shared cap. You can sum expected losses across distinct components without assuming they are independent. Dependence matters for a shared recovery pool, combined tail exposure and the cash peak. Model joint outcomes when one event changes how much the other costs or can recover.

Our buyer faces a 20% chance of a records-related shop visit costing $500,000, all measured on the agreed valuation date. The negotiated remedy would reliably pay $100,000 in that event. Gross residual loss in the event is $400,000. Expected kept risk is $80,000. The effective cost is $8 million plus $120,000 plus $80,000, or $8.20 million. Headroom is the ceiling less effective cost, here $50,000. The maximum headline price that meets the ceiling under these terms is $8.25 million less $120,000 less $80,000, or $8.05 million. Those numbers answer distinct questions. Give the negotiator the price limit and the remaining cushion at the current offer.

A separate teaching risk has a 15% probability of a $240,000 loss. The only collectible recovery would be $60,000 if that event occurs. What is the expected kept loss in dollars?

The event leaves $180,000 with the client. Multiplying by 0.15 gives $27,000. The recovery is conditional on occurrence and is probability-weighted along with the loss.

A client values an aircraft at $6.1 million on today's basis. Transaction costs are $70,000, and expected unrecovered losses are $100,000. With no other cash flows, what is the break-even headline price in dollars?

Subtract both costs from $6,100,000, giving $5,930,000. The ceiling is for the complete package.

Two simultaneous losses of $150,000 each share a single $200,000 aggregate limit. All claims qualify, collection is certain, and no other remedy applies. What is the retained loss in that joint outcome?

  • Zero because each loss fits under $200,000.
  • $100,000 because available recovery is shared.
  • $200,000 because the cap sets the loss.

Add the losses to get $300,000 before applying the common cap once. A per-row model with two fresh limits would overstate recovery.

Test liquidity and sensitivity separately

A loss with a small expected value can still demand a large immediate payment. In the worked purchase, if the shop visit happens and the buyer pays before reimbursement, it must advance the entire $500,000. The expected kept risk of $80,000 would fund only a fraction. Construct a peak-funding schedule with the purchase price, due costs, security, reserves and downside cash calls on their actual dates. List committed funds available on those same dates and any restrictions on drawing them. An expected refund after the peak cannot finance the peak. Agree a financing facility, a funded retention or a timing change before a client commits itself beyond its liquidity ceiling.

Vary the assumptions the client can dispute. For the worked purchase, $130,000 remains under the ceiling after price and closing costs. Dividing that amount by the $400,000 retained per shop visit gives a break-even event probability of 32.5%. Ask the technical adviser whether the unresolved facts could plausibly put probability above that threshold. The estimate can be given as a range. If it straddles 32.5%, collecting more records may be worth more than negotiating an elegant change to the notice wording. A condition to complete a records check can reduce both uncertainty and peak funding.

Assume a different deal has $75,000 of headroom before a single risk. Loss if that event occurs is $300,000 after recovery. What break-even probability, entered as a percentage number, exhausts the headroom?

Divide $75,000 by $300,000 to obtain 0.25. Enter 25 for 25%.

Which proposal directly solves a binding $400,000 cash limit when the client must initially pay $500,000 for repairs?

  • A stronger promise to reimburse the client after the repair.
  • A committed $100,000 facility drawable before payment, with its costs and conditions approved.
  • Reducing the event probability to an optimistic estimate.

The facility can meet the $100,000 peak shortfall on time. Later recovery or a lower estimated probability leaves the same cash need if the event occurs.

The seller offers $75,000 off the purchase price to leave an old mortgage outstanding. The expected loss estimate is $20,000. The board requires clean title at transfer. Who can accept this trade?

The deal team has no mandate to substitute expected value for the board's express condition. Seek the discharge. If the seller cannot provide it, decline on the existing mandate. Any reconsideration belongs to the authorised decision maker.

Use three verdicts with clear authority

Sign if the actual final terms fit the value and funding limits, the required evidence exists and no veto remains. A forecast is appropriate before the final draft. Signing authority requires checking the actual executed package against that forecast. Negotiate if specific achievable changes move the deal inside the mandate or resolve an outstanding condition. State exactly what the counterparty must do, the cheapest acceptable substitute, who can approve it, and the deadline. Walk when the only available terms leave an uncured veto or an economic gap that cannot be bridged within the mandate.

Consider two variations on the worked purchase. If the seller raises price by $90,000, the final package is $40,000 above the ceiling. A credible $40,000 price reduction brings it back to break-even if all other assumptions hold. A new $40,000 remedy that pays only when the 20% event occurs saves at most $8,000 of expected cost. Asking for “forty thousand of protection” without specifying whether it is money today or conditional reimbursement can turn a viable counterproposal into an unworkable one. The $40,000 price reduction is valuable with certainty in this exercise.

If instead the old lender refuses to discharge and the client requires clean title on payment, rework the closing mechanics. Obtain a payoff letter, release documents and an agreed sequence. An unresolved material condition that can still be met points to negotiation or a conditional mandate. If the seller insists on payment with the lien outstanding, and the bank will grant no acceptable release, walk under the client's instruction. A price concession can close an economic gap. A legal or policy veto requires an actual cure or an express change of mandate.

The package exceeds value by $30,000. The only disputed event has a 10% probability. An additional $30,000 remedy would be collectible only if that event occurs. How much of the gap would it close?

  • All $30,000.
  • $3,000.
  • Nothing, because contingent protection has no value.

The additional conditional recovery reduces expected loss by 0.10 times $30,000, assuming the covered loss is large enough. The remaining gap is $27,000.

A client requires discharge on settlement. A bank has agreed documented simultaneous release; the escrow and funding instructions still require signatures. Which instruction fits today?

  • Unconditional sign-off based on verbal agreement.
  • Make authority conditional on receiving the agreed signed release arrangements.
  • Walk permanently because a mortgage existed yesterday.

A credible and specific cure remains available. Confirm it in the closing file before authorising the relevant release.

Deliver a memo that survives a change of draft

Lead with verdict, the money at stake and the one condition most likely to change the answer. Support it with the issues list, each position and fallback, the comparable rows and excluded rows, and the leverage facts behind the forecast. Show an expected and a downside column. State the maximum price, funding peak and a sensitivity threshold. For every material assumption list the source, owner and verification status. Attach the relevant excerpts, mark redactions as unknown, and distinguish adviser estimates from promises in the text. A board member should be able to see where an extra dollar comes from and who has the authority to accept it.

The closing draft changes only the definition of eligible records. Why rerun the decision?

The definition can change which shop visits qualify, how much recovery is collectible and whether the buyer can fund the retained tail. Reprice the affected row and recheck approvals before treating the change as closed.

A model correctly predicts a five-day cure period. The final form counts from the due date while the approved forecast counted from receipt of notice. What should the deal lead do?

  • Mark achieved because both deadlines contain five days.
  • Reopen the timing issue, assess the real cash and default consequences and seek revised authority if material.
  • Delete the model from the verification file.

Keep the forecast as a comparison point. A change to the trigger can shorten the client's effective response time even when the stated number is identical.

Key terms

Value ceiling
The maximum all-in economic cost the client can justify on stated assumptions.
Kept risk
Probability-weighted loss left after eligible, collectible recovery.
Gross loss
The scenario cost before applying remedies.
Conditional recovery
The value recoverable if a particular loss event occurs.
Aggregate limit
One shared recovery ceiling across defined claims or objects.
Effective cost
The agreed cost base plus incremental costs and expected residual losses, counted once.
Headroom
Value ceiling less effective cost.
Break-even price
The maximum headline price that keeps total expected cost within value.
Peak funding
The greatest cash demand at a point in time before later receipts.
Veto
A client or funding condition that requires satisfaction or renewed authority.
